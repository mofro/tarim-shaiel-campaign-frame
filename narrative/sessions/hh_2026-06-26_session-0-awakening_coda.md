There is a kind of waking that is not about the sleeper.

The mud does not release everyone it holds. When it releases someone, it is doing something other than returning them — it is making a question available that the world had set aside. Not *who are you*, which is a question for names and histories. The harder question: *what do you remember?* Which is another way of asking what, in the long darkness, was worth keeping.

Two people surfaced into a world that had continued without them. One from earth. One from calculation. Both carrying things that would not let go of their hands, even when their hands had nothing left to offer — a blade worn into reflex, a book worn into skin. The grip is the thing. Not the object gripped.

This is what the session was really asking: what does it mean to wake up already holding something? To discover, on the first morning back in the world, that some part of you made an agreement you cannot remember making — about what mattered enough to carry, about what was worth the grip even in unconsciousness?

The mud doesn't choose. The grip does.

What it means to already be committed before the waking — this is the question the road ahead will keep asking.
