# Session 4 — Prep: Qarshi

⚠️ **GM-ONLY.** A plan for the arrival in [[qarshi|Qarshi]] and the first day and night there. Every scene is modular: run them in any order, drop any of them.

**Sources.** The Session 3 player summary and the [[Session_3_Prep|Session 3 prep]]. **Marking.** Material tagged **[INVENTED]** is new tonight and is not in any file. `qarshi.md` is still a placeholder, so everything about the town's character is [INVENTED] except names, buildings, jobs, hours and faction seats, which come from the generator. **[UNVERIFIED]** marks a detail not checked against canon.

## 1. Session Frame

**Where we left off.** The party slept in the militia town below the pass and left for Qarshi at dawn. Three things carry over:

- Cal read the town's laws, and the militia watched him do it.
- Rill's coin twitched during the tavern celebration.
- All the companions share a sense that something about that town was not right.

**The shape of tonight.** The party arrives, gets through the gate, finds lodging, and has a night in the city and a market morning. Three threads pull them onward:

| Thread | What it offers |
|---|---|
| The Chain-Breakers | Folk memory of the heroes' return, and the history of the liberation (§5a) |
| The temples | Word of where Nādir is, and what each faith says about dead places (§6) |
| The mercenaries | Paid work on the road south, which happens to be the road to Nādir (§8) |

**Tonight's priority is getting the party word of Nādir.** It comes from three directions so that it lands even if they skip a scene: Hilola's letter (§6), the convoy job (§8), and a rumour (§8). Temir and the Chain-follower riders stay offstage: a name in the background, not a thread to pull.

**Calendar [INVENTED, adjust freely].** They arrive **Wednesday afternoon**. In the data, the Chieftain Market (1062) opens **Thursdays only, 06:00–15:00**, and shops close on **Friday**. Arriving the day before market day gives the town a clock: it is full of traders tonight, the market is busy tomorrow, and the shutters come down the day after.

**Target pacing (~3 hrs).**

| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Road in | 15 min |
| Gate | 20 min |
| Lodging and settling in | 20 min |
| Evening split: Pork and Crow / temples / Keep | 75 min |
| Market morning | 30 min |
| Hooks out | 15 min |

## 2. The Road In (optional, 10–15 min)

The Session 3 prep has unused road events. Take one, or skip straight to the walls.

- **Levy checkpoint [INVENTED].** A Watch post stands two hours out of the city. The Watch knows the caravan is coming, and maybe the militia town sent word. This is the price of Cal's legal enquiries: the town wrote him down. *"A stone man who asks for the laws"* has gone ahead of them.
- **Wolves at the pickets (fight, optional) [INVENTED].** See §8a. Use it if the table wants blood early; the pack has followed the caravan down from the pass.
- **Traffic.** Grain carts head for Thursday's market. A Tulpar family is moving its herd in. A Rahban tinker sits under an awning on a cart and calls to Sahir in the trade cant **[INVENTED]**.

**Sensory anchor for the first sight of the city.** Grey-brown walls rise out of irrigated green. Two minarets stand at different heights: the Lamp and the Fire (§6). The Keep sits low and broad in the middle. In the late afternoon the smoke of cook-fires already hangs flat over the caravan quarter.

## 3. The Gate — Oxus Watch Order (barracks 256)

Barracks 256 is an [[oxus-watch-order|Oxus Watch Order]] seat. Its staff are mostly Kuhban, which makes it a good gate post **[INVENTED placement]**. The Watch holds the street and defers to the Tulpar Confederation in matters of war.

**On the gate:**

- **Bilge Hearth-Stone** (Kuhban, 113, m). The sergeant. Old and patient, and impossible to hurry. He has seen every kind of traveller and is impressed by none of them.
- **Laleh Seam-Reader the Left-Handed** (Kuhban, 82, f). She reads papers and cargo. Her byname is literal: she checks the seams of bales for hidden goods **[INVENTED]**.
- Also on the roster: Soraya Anvil-Sworn the Quiet, and Bagavand Third-Gate (99).

**The scene.**

1. **The tariff.** Dilshod **"Second Seal"** earns his name. He handles the seven-day tariff papers, and this is his scene to shine or to stumble. One roll (Presence or Knowledge, Difficulty 12) either gets the papers stamped cheaply or brings a cargo inspection.
2. **The word from the militia [INVENTED].** If the levy checkpoint ran, Bilge has heard of the stone man. He asks Cal, deadpan, whether he would *like to see the law*. Then he points to a board of posted ordinances by the gate. There are seven, and the one about blades in the market is circled in charcoal.
3. **Ancestry looks.** The town data has no Simiah and no Faun at all. There are 45 Vaghri and 29 Tadbir in a town of about 6,300. Rill and Reizan will draw stares. Cal will draw less than he expects: a Tadbir ward-keeper is not unheard of here **[INVENTED]**. Ariel's wings get a note written on Laleh's tally.

**If it goes wrong.** The gate is not a fight; it is a fine. If it becomes one, it ends quickly: the Watch whistles, and a second barracks (257) is three streets away.

## 4. Lodging

The data has four caravanserais. Two are prepped.

### Default: The Cobbler's Lover (8811)

- **Warden Karghul Mansurzoda** (Tulpar, 86, f, lapsed). A veteran with a scarred jaw who keeps a short sword on the counter. The crew, Kettle and Forty, are Tulpar, and she gives them the good stalls on sight **[INVENTED]**.
- **Yarkan Otabekzoda the Tall**, the stable-hand. The Bizhanzoda family works the kitchen and rooms.

**Background only [INVENTED].** Karghul served alongside men who knew Volkath. If the talk turns to the mountains, she spits at the name. Asked why, she says only that there are chain-sign riders up north now "who think they're owed something". She does not know a name, and she changes the subject. **Hold Temir and Volkath's tale** for later sessions.

**Human drama: a birth in the stalls [INVENTED].** A Tulpar drover's wife is in labour in the end stall tonight. Karghul has sent for a midwife and has had the iron put out: a knife under the pallet, and an onion and an iron pin at the door. This is protection against the **Albasty**, who comes for women in childbirth (§6a). The midwife is late. If anyone sits with the family, the drover tells them, without being asked, that his first wife died this way on the road and that he "didn't put out the iron that time". Give the scene a moment to pass, or let a PC help. A healthy child by morning buys the party friends among the Tulpar crews.

### Alternative: The Stylish Boar (12452), for Sahir

- **Warden Tannaz Faramarzugli** (29). Young, and newly in charge.
- **The Low-Gate family.** Homa (45) and Zafar (70) are Rahban, and so is **Mehrnoz Iskanderian**, the cook (Rahban, 68). **Mehran Bijanugli "Half-Price"** is the porter and haggles over everything.

**Hook [INVENTED].** Zafar Low-Gate recognises the iron compass's maker's mark, or claims to: a southern workshop, "Balkh or past it, instrument-makers' guild". It pairs with the market merchant in §7. It points south, the same way as everything else tonight.

The other two caravanserais are unprepped. The Grain and Throne (583) has the cook Nodir Bakhtiyarzadeh the Tall. The Monastery and Warhorse (12425) has a warden, Nasrin Kimak, who is only 19.

**Bathhouses.** The town has eight, e.g. the Alchiian (422) and the Nostalgic (476). After the pass and the wolves, a bathhouse is a cheap, warm scene for anyone who skips the evening's main thread. It is also a good place to overhear things (§8).

## 5. Night — The Pork and Crow (4), Chain-Breakers seat

**Open 20:00–03:00.** In the data this is a gang hangout. The profile makes it the [[chain-breakers-order|Chain-Breakers Order]] seat, following the Dehdadi precedent.

**What the Chain-Breakers are (from the faction file).** They hold the memory of the liberation and the chain-breaking rite. They have no headquarters and are anchored among the Tulpar. They believe the liberation was left incomplete and that **the heroes will return**. They hold memory, not understanding. The Scholar's Purge of ~1175 hunted them, so they keep their rites behind a tavern's back room.

**Who is here:**

| Name | Notes | Stat line |
|---|---|---|
| **Sepideh Zarirzoda the Younger** | Human, 53, f, Forge-Record faith. The boss. Thoughtful, slow to trust, and polite in the way that means she has already counted your knives. | Leader |
| **Ruksana Almaszoda** | 82, non-religious. **Keeper of the rite [INVENTED role].** She knows the words without believing in any god behind them. | Social-grade; the data lists her as a gang member, use Bruiser if it matters |
| **Alp Mehranzoda the Tall** | 52, the door | Bruiser |
| Ilkhan Hamidzoda, Hamid Samanzadeh | Hands | Bruiser |
| **Umida Shapurugli** | 19, the youngest member. Eager, and the one most likely to talk too much. | Bruiser |
| Tegin Jahanzadeh | 59 | — |
| **Aybek Slag-Hand**, **Golnar Tally-Hammer** (66) | Kuhban bartenders | Minion |
| Ramesh Dilshodugli, Berke Aybekian (79), Yazdan Ravshanzoda | Servers | — |
| Farid and Zilola Qunghirat | Cooks | — |

**The scene [INVENTED].**

1. **The room.** It is low-ceilinged and loud, with pork fat and crow pie, which the house is named for. Iron links hang from the rafters like a merchant's string of onions. Anyone sharp sees that every link is **broken**.
2. **The rite at midnight.** The back room goes quiet. Ruksana takes a whole chain and lays it across a newcomer's wrists; tonight the newcomer is a Tulpar drover. She speaks the old formula, and a hammer comes down. The words are half-remembered, and people mouth them along the way people mouth a hymn they never learned properly. **Write the formula in fragments.** No one in the room understands it fully, and that is the point.
3. **The coin.** At the hammer-blow, **Rill's coin turns over in his pocket once**, and only once. It does not twitch the way it did in the militia town. It *turns*, as if to look. The Jade Illusionist is watching this. It reveals at structurally perfect moments, and this is not one yet. Nothing more happens.
4. **The folk story.** Afterwards a drunk man tells the house version: the heroes went up into the sky after breaking the chains, and *they'll come back when the chains are whole again, to break them twice.* Others correct him; everyone has a different version. **Do not let any NPC link the PCs to the heroes.** At most Umida says to Veyra, admiringly, "You fight like the old songs." That is a compliment and nothing more.
5. **The ask (optional).** Sepideh has noticed the party's caravan. Her people need something carried quietly south: a letter, or a child of a member who must leave the city. She asks once and accepts a no. This is a hook, not a quest.

**If anyone asks about the chain-sign riders in the north,** Sepideh's face closes. "Not ours." That is all she says tonight. She names no one.

### 5a. The history lesson [INVENTED framing on canon dates]

If the party is curious, stays past the rite, or brings Umida a drink, the Chain-Breakers will tell the history. Here it is the house version: told proudly, wrong in places, and in the wrong order. The Lamp's library (§6) has the scholar's version of the same events. Let the two disagree.

**The canon spine (GM reference).** Years are HB, and the present is ~1200.

| When | What | How the Pork and Crow tells it |
|---|---|---|
| ~200–250 | **The liberation.** The heroes break the empire's chains. | "A thousand years ago, give or take a lifetime." The rite is a re-enactment of it. |
| the following ~70 years | **The chaos.** The freed peoples scatter, and the Tulpar eruption follows. | "Hard years. Free isn't the same as fed." Told as the price, not as a mistake. |
| ~370 | **The Truce of Nine Caravans.** | "When the road was made safe again." Ruksana knows the number nine and nothing else. |
| 400–700 | **The Tulpar epic takes shape.** | The drunk man's song comes from here, which is why it has horses in it. |
| ~1175 | **The Scholar's Purge.** | "About a generation ago. My mother's time." Sepideh lost a teacher. This is why the rite is held behind a tavern. |

**Beats.**
- **The broken links.** Every link on the rafters was cut from a real chain, one for every person the house has "broken free" by the rite. Some are very old. Umida points out the oldest, black and pitted, and says it came "from the first ones". It did not, but it might be two hundred years old.
- **The Purge is personal.** Sepideh's teacher kept the rite's formula *in writing*. He was taken in ~1175, and the writing went with him. Since then the words have been carried by mouth, which is why they are in fragments. "We lost the words. We kept the hammer."
- **Memory, not understanding.** Asked *why* the heroes left, or what "unfinished" means, nobody knows. They know the work was left undone, and that is all. **No one links the PCs to the heroes**, not even as a joke.
- **What the party carries away.** The Chain-Breakers are old and hunted, and they are not one body. The Purge is recent and targeted scholars. The Lamp's library is where the written version survived, which points to §6.

## 6. The Temples (afternoon or morning)

Both are open **05:00–21:00**. The generator labels both as "mosques". Canon has no Islam-analog faith (REGIONAL_FAITH_LIST, ground rules), so play them as F2 **the Lamp** (lodge and courtyard, recitation of verse and the Court's names, shared meals) and F3 **the Fire** (a kept flame, water reverence, purity rules). The two compete politely for the same merchants.

### Mosque of Uren — the Lamp (3)

- **Priest Hilola Mansurzadeh** (77). Social, Tier 2: Difficulty 14, HP 5, Stress 4, thresholds 9 / 18.
- **Cleric Artavan Khazar the Younger.**

**The library [INVENTED].** The Lamp keeps the old lending library, or what the Purge left of it. Shelves stand half empty, with tags still nailed to the gaps: *taken 1175*, *taken 1176*. Hilola keeps the tags up on purpose.

**The Nādir lead [INVENTED].** This is the main delivery for tonight.
- Hilola corresponds with an **Azure Convocation** scholar teaching near **Puli Khumri**. That scholar is **Nādir**, who is living, per the Session 3 prep. The Puli Khumri Azure outpost is [INVENTED] in that prep.
- Her last letter from him came two months ago. It was late, and it asked an odd question: whether the Lamp's records note any place "where the lamps for the dead will not stay lit". She has not answered, because she does not know how. **She shows the party the letter.** That puts the dead-zone thread and the Nādir thread in one hand.
- She will write a letter of introduction for anyone who shows respect and asks good questions. Cal asking about law codes counts, and so does Sahir asking about old songs.
- *Her price:* word of the burned city, Kitabdara, if anyone saw it. A library-keeper asks after every lost library. She may also ask them to carry her answer to Nādir. That is a reason to go, not a requirement.

**What the Lamp teaches about dead places [INVENTED, built on canon practice].**
- **Khvarenah**, the Radiance, rests on the worthy like a visible light. It can be lost, and it can be regained. The Lamp says a dead place is *a place the Radiance has left*: not cursed, simply unattended. "Nothing is lucky there. Nothing is unlucky either." (GM-side: Khvarenah is the Jade Illusionist's Persian mask. The Lamp's priests do not know this. Do not hint at it.)
- **The departure lamp.** When someone dies, a small lamp is lit at once. It is Sraosha's notice that a soul has gone. If it goes out before burial, the departure was not recorded. Hilola has heard of caravans whose dead-lamps would not stay lit in certain stretches of road. The caravaners bury those dead twice, "to make sure".
- **The Lamp's fear**, which Hilola admits only if trusted: the unrecorded dead do not leave, and Albasty (§6a) feed on souls that have not left.

### Mosque of Nuona — the Fire (14)

- **Priest Almas Sorushian the Quiet** (54). Social, Tier 2, same line as Hilola.
- **Cleric Zulfiya Varzakkian the Left-Handed** (69).

**What the Fire teaches about dead places [INVENTED, built on canon practice].**
- **Purity-reading.** Fire priests read purity: in bodies, in water, in a hearth. A healthy flame leans toward what is clean and away from what is fouled. In a dead place, the Fire teaches, **the flame does not lean at all**. It stands perfectly straight, "as if there were nothing on either side of it to choose between".
- **Water.** Zulfiya is the canal-priest, for whom canals are devotion, and she is the one who will talk. She tells of a **dry kariz** two days south. The water stopped twenty years ago, and the water-guild did not find a collapse. The tunnel is sound. The water simply does not come. The Fire calls it unclean ground. The Lamp calls it abandoned. The ghul story (§6a) lives there.
- **The disagreement is the clue.** One faith says something has *left*. The other says nothing is *pressing* on either side. Both describe an absence. Neither priest will put it that way, and neither should.

**The dead-zone beat (optional) [INVENTED].** Use it once, briefly, and do not explain it.
- **The flame.** The eternal flame stops leaning and stands straight for one breath when the party enters. Zulfiya sees it. Almas sees her see it.
- **Cal's tome.** Cal's tome will not open; the clasp holds as if rusted, and then it works.
- **Reizan.** Reizan's shift takes two tries.
- **Rill.** Rill feels nothing at all, and feels *calm*. Note that for the GM.

Almas says nothing, and later asks the party to leave by the side door, *politely*. If they come back alone and ask, he says only: "The fire did not know what to make of you. That has happened twice in my life. The other time was the kariz." This is a practical effect only. The cosmology stays offstage.

### 6a. Regional myth: rumours, folk beliefs, god-talk [INVENTED except where marked canon]

Drop these anywhere: the bathhouse, the inn yard, the market, the Pork and Crow.

**Death is many (canon).** Death here is plural: a company, not one figure. Qarshi has a Persian and Turkic mix, so you will hear both registers.
- **The Shadowing (Persian, canon).** A death-shadow follows everyone from birth. "My shadow's close lately" means *I am afraid I will die soon.* A watcher at the edge of vision, or a heavy breath at the neck, means it has moved closer. Someone says it tonight and laughs it off.
- **The Ölüm Sürüsü, the Death-Flock (steppe, canon).** The Tulpar drovers name the flock's members. **Susuz**, the Thirsty, is drought-death; the water-carriers do not say the name aloud, and they shush anyone who does. **Kara Yel**, the Black Wind, is the road that kills.
- **The Crow Court (eastern, canon).** Eastern traders bring it with them. One crow on a roof means the appointment is made. A flock means the Court is still deliberating, so there is time. **Crows that do not startle** mean the normal order is broken. This is the cleanest dead-zone signal for the road south (§8a).
- **Erlik's Messenger (steppe, canon).** A nameless rider on a black horse. In a house of the dying, someone sits facing the door to keep it open for him.

**Monsters of the stories.**
- **The Albasty.** Seen as a woman with long hair, or as a goat. She comes for women in childbirth and for newborns, and iron keeps her off. This is the birth at the Cobbler's Lover (§4). Canon ties the Albast to souls that have not departed.
- **The ghul of the dry kariz.** Something lives in the tunnel since the water stopped. Travellers hear a voice calling them by name from the shafts. Kariz-diggers say never to answer a voice in a tunnel.
- **The div of the salt flat.** Big, stupid and old, the div makes a bargain and keeps it to the letter. Tell it as a children's story with a moral about wording. (A Div-Born or Infernis PC changes how this story feels at the table. Let whoever tells it notice that halfway through.)

**God rumours.**
- "Khvarenah's left the Mingh house. You can see it on the children."
- "Anahita's angry. They've been burying the dead upstream at the new barracks." This is a purity quarrel between the Fire and the Watch.
- "The Road-Keeper's ledger priest at the south gate has stopped writing down the caravans to Puli Khumri. He says he's run out of ink. Nobody believes him."
- "The Sky-Father sent the falling stars as a sign." This ties to the Night of Falling Stars from the Session 3 prep. Steppe folk and Lamp folk quarrel over what it was a sign *of*.

## 8a. Encounters [INVENTED]

Four fights. Run whichever fits; none is required. The Daggerheart core adversary names below are **[UNVERIFIED]**: check their numbers in the core book. The fallback lines are the pipeline's Tier 1 Standard and Bruiser lines, so they match the NPC table.

**1. Wolves at the pickets (road in, or the first night).** The pack that followed them from the pass, testing the horses at the caravan pickets. Their goal is to scatter the horses, not to kill anyone. Kettle and Forty shout the alarm.
- Core: *Dire Wolf* plus a pack leader [UNVERIFIED]. Fallback: 4–5 wolves on the Standard line (Difficulty 12, HP 5, Stress 3, 6 / 11, +2, bite 1d8+1), and the leader on the Bruiser line (Difficulty 13, HP 7, 8 / 14, +3).
- **Twist:** if the party wins cleanly, Farid Wolf-Road hears of it by morning (§8).

**2. The kariz ambush (the road south, if the convoy leaves early or as a preview).** Bandits use the dry kariz shafts as hides, coming up behind the carts. They want grain, not a fight. Break their leader and the rest run.
- Core: *Jagged Knife* bandits [UNVERIFIED]. Fallback: one Leader (Difficulty 14, HP 7, Stress 5, 8 / 15, +3, short sword 1d8+1), 3 Standards, and 4 Minions.
- **Twist:** one bandit, the youngest, won't go back down the shaft. "Something *calls* in there."

**3. The thing that does not blink (the dead-zone animal).** A jackal, or a vulture, sitting on the road and watching the convoy pass. It doesn't flee. The crows nearby don't startle. When it attacks, it attacks without sound. This is **Tier 1 Wrongness**: absence, not monstrosity. Nothing about it is wrong to look at. It is wrong because nothing moves around it.
- Fallback: Bruiser line (Difficulty 13, HP 7, Stress 3, 8 / 14, +3, bite 1d8+1). It takes no Fear-based effects and makes no noise.
- **The practical effect:** within ten paces of it, Cal's tome won't open, as it wouldn't at the Fire. Rill feels calm. Spells and charms work, but slowly, as if pushed through cold water. Once it is dead, everything resets. Do not explain any of it.
- **Do not show undead.** Nothing rises.
