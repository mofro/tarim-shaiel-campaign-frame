## The Setup (recap for your own use, not to narrate wholesale)

Some of Volkath's Tulpar never accepted the terms the Imperial tax collectors forced in the earlier renegotiation (the tariff standoff from Ariel's Session 0 courtyard scene). They've lured those same soldiers back into Rabati Malik under a pretext and are staging an ambush in the streets. If the soldiers already reported in before walking into town, a larger force retaliates against the caravan by name. The leader of the ambushing group is Volkath's son.

Two threads, decided at the end of Session 1: one group goes to intercept the soldiers before they walk into it; another goes to reach Volkath's son and call it off, or at least get between the two sides before the first blow lands.

## Structure — Dual Dynamic Countdowns

This is not a scripted fight. It's a race against a shared clock, using the core rules' Dynamic Countdown mechanic (the same pattern the book uses for chase scenes — one side's progress, one shared consequence). Full explanation below — don't assume you'll have the core book's countdown rules memorized mid-session.

Three dice sit on the table for the whole scene, visible to the players the entire time. That visibility is the point — it's what makes both clocks feel real:

- One **Consequence Countdown die** — the shared fuse, **"Blood in the Streets."** Starts at **6**.
- One **Progress Countdown die** — **Thread A, reaching the soldiers.** Starts at **4**.
- One **Progress Countdown die** — **Thread B, reaching Temir.** Starts at **5**.

**Neither thread is assigned to a specific PC.** Whoever the players send takes that thread's Difficulty as written; if someone has a genuine mechanical edge (flight/speed for Thread A, real prior standing with Temir for Thread B), apply the noted modifier — don't pre-script who goes where.

Every action roll a PC makes in pursuit of their thread's goal produces one outcome that moves **two dice at once**: that thread's own Progress die, and the shared Consequence die. Use this table for every such roll — it's the only thing that moves the dice:

| Roll result | Their thread's Progress die | The shared "Blood in the Streets" die |
|---|---|---|
| Critical Success | Tick down 3 | No change |
| Success with Hope | Tick down 2 | No change |
| Success with Fear | Tick down 1 | Tick down 1 |
| Failure with Hope | No change | Tick down 2 |
| Failure with Fear | No change | Tick down 3 |

"Tick down" = turn the die to the next-lower number (or cross off a box if tracking on paper). Note the built-in cost on Success with Fear: even succeeding at your own thread's roll still nicks the shared fuse. There's no fully safe roll here — every attempt on either thread spends some of the party's shared time, win or lose.

**What "hitting 0" means:**
- **Thread A hits 0** → the soldiers are reached, warned, and believe it. Narrate the stand-down actually landing.
- **Thread B hits 0** → same, for Temir's side.
- **The fuse hits 0 first** → it goes off, regardless of where either Progress die stands. Temir's crew and the patrol engage each other in the streets. See "If It Goes Off," below.

**Worked example** (for your own read-through, not to run verbatim): Rill's on Thread B, closing distance on Temir's position, rolls Success with Fear. Thread B ticks 5→4; the fuse also ticks 6→5 — he made noise, or took a wrong turn first. Meanwhile Ariel's on Thread A, rolls a Critical Success catching up to the patrol — Thread A ticks 4→1, no cost to the fuse. The table now sees Thread A one roll from landing, Thread B still with ground to cover, and the fuse at 5. That's the picture the dice should paint at every point in the scene.

## Thread A — Reaching the Soldiers

**Stage 1 (4→3), closing distance:** Agility or Instinct roll. Advantage if the PC has real mobility (flight, notable speed).

**Stage 2 (3→1), getting Captain Kaan to listen at all:** Presence or Instinct, **Difficulty 13**. He's competent, and has specific history with whoever approaches if it's Ariel — she's the one who humiliated his patrol last time. That cuts both ways: no reason to trust her, but no reason to think she'd bother lying to him either. Don't let the roll succeed on charm alone — he wants a reason, not reassurance.

**Stage 3 (1→0), sealing it:** **Difficulty 12**. Requires one concrete, actionable detail — a position, a number, something a scout would actually know. Pure reassurance doesn't work here; if a player tries it, don't call for a roll, just let it fail and say why.

## Thread B — Reaching Temir

**Stage 1 (5→3), finding the staging point:** Instinct, **Difficulty 11**. Inexperienced tradecraft — easier to find than trained soldiers would be to sneak up on.

**Stage 2 (3→1), getting Temir to listen instead of reacting hostile:** Presence or Instinct, **Difficulty 12** baseline. **−2 (Difficulty 10)** if the PC has real standing with him. **+2 (Difficulty 14)** if they're a stranger to him. Rill has been with the caravan a month and has a face Temir might recognize — that's familiarity, not established trust; treat him as baseline unless play has established more. Sahir is a stranger to Temir specifically, even though he's not a stranger to the party — the +2 applies if he's the one who goes.

**Stage 3 (1→0), the actual persuasion:** Difficulty depends on the argument made, not just the roll:
- Appeal to consequences for Volkath and the caravan (they didn't choose this fight, they pay for it too) — **Difficulty 10**. This is the argument that reads true to who Temir is.
- Telling him he's wrong to be angry — **Difficulty 15**. He isn't wrong, and this doesn't land regardless of the roll's modifiers; keep it hard on purpose.
- If **Sanem** (his second — see below) is backing the PC's case by this point: advantage instead of a fresh roll.

## Resolution Branches

- **Both threads resolve before the fuse hits 0:** clean defusal. Play the stand-down as its own scene, not a summary.
- **Only one resolves:** that side is warned and postures defensively; the other side is still walking in blind. Raises the stakes on whichever thread is still racing — they now know something might already be going wrong on the other side.
- **The fuse hits 0 first — it goes off.** Staged dynamically, not pre-scripted: use whatever's actually true in the fiction at that moment (who's arrived, who hasn't, what either thread discovered) to decide how the ambush actually opens. Any PC not yet on-site enters a fight already in motion — they don't get to start it, they choose how to enter it.

**If it goes off:** use the core rules' "Relative Strength" rule (from the Ambushers environment) rather than pre-built stat blocks — the fight's Difficulty equals whichever side's highest adversary Difficulty. Kaan lands around 13. Hold off fully statting Temir's crew unless this branch actually triggers — no need to build combat crunch for a scene you're trying to prevent.

**Temir** — Volkath's son. The young Tulpar bystander from that same courtyard scene (hand in coat, left quietly — see the correction in `Session_0_GM_NOTES.md`). Chain-Breakers Order sympathies. Not reckless for its own sake — he thinks this is deserved, and telling him otherwise won't move him. What might: he's risking people who didn't choose this fight, starting with his father.

## After the Crisis Resolves — Dead Zone

Once the immediate crisis is handled and the caravan is actually moving again, run the dead zone beat:

- Tier 1 dead zone — Warren disturbance as absence (per `mechanics/GLOSSARY_GM.md`). Framed as absence only. Never "ecosystem damage." Never explained to players.
- Practical effects, no exposition: a lit lantern gutters and won't reignite; Cal's tome refuses to open; Rayzen's shift takes two tries and feels wrong on the way through.
- **Rill is the inversion.** Whatever his patron-bond does, it's uncannily calm and clear here — the one stretch all session where he isn't straining. He has no frame yet for why. Play it as unsettling comfort, not relief.
- Don't name the mechanism. This is Act I's first "dead zones" beat — it lands without a word of cosmology spoken aloud.
