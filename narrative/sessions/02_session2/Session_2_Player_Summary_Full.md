# Session 2

## The Consequences of Chains

Session 2 picked up exactly where Session 1 ended: Imperial riders on the road to Rabati Malik, an ambush waiting for them in the city streets, and a party with two jobs to do and no time to spare. Ariel and Reizan, the latter in animal shape, flew to intercept Captain Idris Kaan before he rode into the trap, while Rill and Cal rode hard for the city to find it. Sahir was left at the gates. The ambush never happened. Kaan agreed to wait and see, Temir's crew was argued out of its plan, and the whole affair ended as an official misunderstanding, with Volkath sending the party on its way with supplies, a cart and horses, and three of his own people. Ariel then went after Veyra, who agreed to travel with the group for a while. The session closed with a stranger gone into the dark and a coin that would not stop spinning.

## Key Events

1. **Intercepting the Riders**

Ariel and Reizan, who had taken an animal's shape for the journey, flew out to intercept the Imperial riders before they reached the city. Their leader, Captain Idris Kaan, had come to carry out a threat and recoup the tariffs he was owed, and pressing on was his duty. Ariel told him that violence was waiting in Rabati Malik, and that it could be avoided. The captain's initial hesitancy was a cause for concern and sparked [[Bearing Witness|a moment of doubt for Ariel]]. It took time, but she slowly convinced him, and despite everything he was obliged to do, he saw fit to believe her.

2. **The Captain's Terms**

Kaan's trust was a begrudging one, and it came with limits. He would ride to the city gates, as his orders required, but not through them, honoring Ariel's plea, and from there he would see what could be seen of the plot. He also said plainly that if the party could pull this off, he would go further than simply looking the other way, and would consider bringing the merchants' grievances back to his lord. Why he gave way, the party could only guess. Ariel's conviction clearly counted for a good deal, and perhaps there was an echo of their first meeting in it, or perhaps he recalled whatever he had seen reflected in her obsidian disc. Whatever the reason, he would hold at the gates, and the rest was now up to the others.

3. **A View from the Rooftops**

Rill and Cal, meanwhile, had ridden fast for the city to find the ambush. Both of them spotted a youth on a rooftop near the central square, and Rill recognized what the rooftops meant: a privileged vantage over the streets below, and the means to build a chokepoint for any riders who came through. A plan like that would need a lookout at street level too, so the pair searched for the most obvious place to put one. There they spotted Sanem, another of the conspirators, and called on the whole group to come out.

4. **Boxes from the Roof**

Temir, angry at the inconvenient interruption, revealed himself and began to argue his case. He seemed determined to show himself a true militant of the Order of the Chain, and he was in no mood to be talked out of it. Cal would not listen, and Temir and another boy answered by threatening him and heaving large boxes down from the roof. The boxes struck Cal and did no real harm. He came away unhurt and, to all appearances, unfazed.

5. **Rill's Argument**

Rill used his agility to get closer to the rooftops and began to explain what the plan would actually accomplish. It would not address the tariff dispute in any real way, he said, and the only people it was likely to get in trouble were Temir's father and the caravan he led. Sanem and the other dissidents had been uneasy since Rill first started to speak, and Temir's companions eventually yielded to the argument. That forced Temir to abandon the plan. His anger was slow to cool, but it cooled enough, and no further violence was needed.

6. **A Misunderstanding**

Sahir had been left at the gates, and it was there that the two halves of the plan met. He went into the city to speak with his companions, then returned to report to Ariel and to Captain Kaan, who had halted at the gates as he had promised. Kaan, impressed by the party's commitment to a non-violent solution, chose to be lenient with everyone involved. He pledged that there would be no repercussions for the caravan masters or their crews, and he said his report would be clear of any charges. As far as the Empire was concerned, it had all been a misunderstanding.

7. **Volkath's Thanks**

Temir was chastised by his father, but he was also welcomed back, and as far as the party could tell, he and Sanem and the other dissidents stayed with the caravan. The eye Cal had given Ariel to pass to Temir never reached him, and it was returned to Cal. Once it was clear that the party meant to travel on by a different road, Volkath showed his appreciation. He gave them information, travel supplies, and his best wishes, along with a cart and horses to carry their loads. He also sent three of his younger but able workers to see them safely on, who went by the names Forty, Kettle, and Second Seal. The information included a suggestion. For answers about their lost memories, and about why they found themselves so far from where they belonged, Volkath pointed them to Nādir Qissakhān, the author of *The Roads*. He could not say where the man was now, but suspected he might still be living or working somewhere near the city of Puli Khumri, and the party set out to find him and seek his counsel.

8. **One Last Conversation**

As the party traveled, it became clear that they were not going to catch Veyra, who was headed roughly the same way but well ahead of them. Ariel, with the agreement of the others, flew forward for one last conversation. She found Veyra well at home just off the road and not looking for company, and she set out her arguments. It was a fraught exchange, full of Veyra's reservations about being manipulated by forces she could not see and her wish to have nothing to do with fighting. Ariel asked her to travel with the group, at least until they could tell whether the link between them was worth keeping. Veyra, curious about the answers she was looking for, agreed there was no reason not to journey together while the roads ran roughly the same way, and promised to wait. The party, skirting a river town on the way, found her waiting.

9. **A Stranger at the Fire**

About a day out of Rabati Malik, shortly before the party left the road for an overland route to the southwest, a traveller came to their fire alone. He gave his name as Calberri Davis, played down the fact that he was on the road by himself, and asked to share the fire. He offered almost no useful information. To explain the trade that took him down the road, he showed his wares instead: a number of magical items, impressive but, as far as anyone could tell, useless. One was a coil of rope that never seemed to run out, and each came with a trader's patter.

10. **What He Left Behind**

One of the curiosities stayed behind, a coin. When it was flipped or tossed it hung in the air and spun along the axis of the throw without ever seeming to slow, and it could be plucked out of that spin with no difficulty or resistance at all. The traveller left in the night with little fuss, though everyone had cautioned him against going. The coin was found, in Rill's pocket, after the stranger had departed.

## Critical Decisions & Unresolved Threads

The session turned on a shared choice to settle the crisis at Rabati Malik without violence, and on the trust that made it possible: Kaan's begrudging belief in Ariel, and Rill's argument that the ambush would cost the caravan far more than it could win. Kaan's offer to consider carrying the merchants' grievances back to his lord was conditional, and it remains to be seen whether anything comes of it. Temir and Sanem stayed with Volkath's caravan, though how fully the argument settled them is unclear. Veyra agreed to travel with the party only provisionally, until they could judge whether there was a link worth keeping, and her wariness of being manipulated by unseen forces is still part of the arrangement. The party left Rabati Malik with supplies, a cart and horses, and three of Volkath's workers, bound for a road that was about to turn overland. And the stranger who left the coin remains a question.
