# The Fork in the Road

*Session 1 · Waycross to Rabati Malik · GM: Mo*

---

## A Rahban Stands Still

Three days on the road, and the road had been good to him. Sahir moved through country with the ease that comes not from familiarity but from something older — a Rahban's birthright, the bone-knowledge that there is always a direction and the direction is always findable. He had never once stood somewhere and not known the way forward.

Then the fork.

Both paths pulled — that was what stopped him. Not one pull, the familiar singular certainty, but two, simultaneously, with equal weight. The Rahban vocabulary had no word for this. It wasn't being lost. It was something more like watching an instrument he'd relied on his entire life give two different readings, both authoritative, neither wrong enough to dismiss.

> [!polaroid|right] Death Sorcerer
> ![[Daggerheart-Sorcerer-portrait.png]]


He'd been standing there long enough that a small brown bird landed near his boot, concluded his toe wasn't food, and left. He'd barely registered it. When he finally looked up, a merchant's cart was planted at the center of the junction — a flat wagon with cases of small goods and a man who had been watching him stand here for some time.

> [!fas-infobox|black-and-white float-left] The Merchant at the Junction
> <p>**Name:** Kalas (with a K)</p>
> <p>**Marks:** Jade-bead abacus, worn smooth from use — not decorative</p>
> <p>**Carried:** Small curiosities; a compass in a balsa-wood case almost too light to be real</p>
> <p>**Question:** *"Which pull do you usually trust?"*</p>
> <p>**Answer:** *"The one not known."*</p>
> <p>**Payment:** The compass. Not a gift. *"I took my payment in kind."*</p>

The merchant explained that the world was a globe. A consistent field. The needle always knew where it converged. Sahir said the maps were flat. The merchant smiled without conceding anything, wrote something in a small notebook, and pocketed it without explaining what.

Sahir took the right fork. By golden hour a caravan was visible in the distance. He pointed himself at it, turning the compass in his hand, watching it hold its course regardless of direction.

## The Stars Sit Wrong

> [!profile]
> ![[Gemini_Generated_Image_jloj8fjloj8fjloj.png]]
> *Volkath, caravan master*

Volkath held the telling carefully. Words arranged over many tellings, spoken with the gravity of someone who understood that certain histories stay alive only if someone keeps saying them aloud. The geas. The chains. The empire's fall. The breaking all at once, everywhere — and how that freedom arrived before his people had anything to receive it into. The rage that followed. Decades. Then the road, reclaimed.

He finished. Looked at each of them in turn. Asked if there were questions.

The group knew the world's broad shapes — its peoples, its geography. The history Volkath had just described was simply absent. Not dim. Not half-remembered. Gone, the way a map is gone of a country you've never visited.

Then Veyra looked up at the stars and said they sat wrong.

> [!cite] Veyra, plainly — as the most economical account of the evidence
> "Perhaps we have died. Perhaps something pulled us back from a deathly sleep of a thousand years."

Cal produced the tome of constellations. The patterns documented and the patterns visible above them were structurally different — as if drawn from a different sky entirely. Not the small drift of latitude or season. A different sky.

Sahir arrived while the tome was being examined, bearing sweets. His entrance opened the conversation rather than interrupting it. The right questions, offered to the right people at the right moment. The fire crackled on, unconcerned.

## Two Artifacts, One Experiment

Rill had been sitting on something since joining the fire. Certain senses — nothing with a name — went quiet near this group. He brought it up carefully, not wanting to be taken for unwell.

Ariel handed him her disc — the object she woke holding. Giving it up cost her something she hadn't known she was carrying; the tension in her shoulders became more of itself the moment the disc left her hand. Rill backed away to test whether the artifact extended whatever he was sensing. It didn't.

Second test: disc on the ground, Rill walking toward Ariel on foot. At the halfway point — perhaps seventeen feet — his ring finger itched. Just that. Briefly. Then nothing. He made it all the way to Ariel without the connection resuming. When she retrieved the disc, the tension released quietly.

Two artifacts. Two distinct behaviors. The experiment answered less than it cost to run.

> [!pinned] Veyra walks east, before full light
> "Higher purpose we're supposed to devote our lives to — it's for suckers. Just go to where you find peace and stay there."
> 
> *Nobody ran after her.*

## A Father's Problem

The runner came from Rabati Malik thirty minutes past breakfast. Volkath crossed to the group with a directness that meant urgency. Some of his caravan never accepted the renegotiated tax deal with the Imperial collectors. They'd lured those same soldiers back inside the walls and were staging an ambush.

If the soldiers had sent word before walking in, a larger force would come — and come to the caravan as an identified party. Younger members would carry legal exposure they hadn't earned. The road west would close in ways that couldn't be simplified.

He couldn't compel the group. This was outside the agreement. But the persuasive ability they'd already demonstrated would be the most useful thing they could put into this situation.

Then, quietly:

> [!cite] Volkath
> "I will allow for some full disclosure. That head miscreant is my son."

> [!fas-infobox] Road & Stakes
> **Location:** Waycross staging ground, dawn
> **Destination:** Rabati Malik — fortified caravanserai, one hour east
> **Threat:** Imperial patrol, lured into an ambush inside the walls
> **If soldiers sent word:** A larger force comes. Caravan identified. Road closes.
> **Volkath's ask:** Reach the son before the first blow lands

Two threads. Ariel for the Imperials — speed was the whole game on that side. Rill for the son — a face he'd recognize after a month with the caravan. Cal offered to send one of his eyes along as proof of origin. The table received this with silence, then a specific quality of horrified amusement.
