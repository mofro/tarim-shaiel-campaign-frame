## Session 1-A

The party gathered around a campfire, listening as Volkath told them a tale meant to ground them in a world they barely remembered. What began as an evening of cosmology and history became something more intimate, as the companions grappled with the strange truth that they knew one another deeply yet could recall no shared stories. The session closed with that quiet reckoning still hanging in the air, a bond of trust acknowledged but unexplained.

### Key Events

1. **The Fork in the Road**

Sahir walked a stretch of road that had grown steadily busier over three days, until he came upon a fork that felt wrong. Both paths pulled at him with the certainty of true north, yet neither agreed with the other. He stood so long trying to parse the sensation that a bird landed near his foot and considered whether his toe might be a worm. He took out his notebook and began to sketch the moment, only then noticing a small cart with a merchant nearby, a cart that seemed to have been there long enough to become part of the roadside.

2. **The Merchant at the Crossroads**

The merchant was an unremarkable human in typical roving clothes, with no visible weapons and a well-worn abacus of jade beads at his belt. Sahir approached openly, and the man wordlessly gestured for him to look over the cart. The wares were small curiosities, some covered in cloth, and Sahir searched for anything useful for the road. The merchant produced a small piece of metal wrapped in leather, tapered on both ends, and suggested it was a compass. When Sahir held it, his hand turned slightly, and the device seemed always to point toward what he had always understood as north.

3. **A Strange Cosmology**

The merchant explained that the world itself was one giant orb of metal and stone, radiating a predictable energy field divided into lines that wrapped the globe and converged on a single point. Sahir found the device fascinating and asked how it worked, though the explanation struck him as entirely new, something absent from the stories of his clan. The merchant then asked which pull Sahir usually trusted, and Sahir answered that he trusted the one not knowing. When asked what the unaccountable pull felt like, Sahir said it was like the road, but not the road. The merchant wrote something in a small book, then put it away.

4. **A Trade of Questions**

Sahir tried to glimpse what the merchant had written, but could not make it out. He remarked that the merchant asked intriguing questions, and asked whether the cart was his home. The merchant said he had been there long enough to meet Sahir, and that the left road led toward a way station, while the right road surely led somewhere as well. Sahir felt no threat from the man, and when he tried to return the compass, the merchant refused, saying he had already taken his payment in the form of the question Sahir had answered. Sahir hesitated, saying he did not know the man well enough to feel comfortable accepting a gift, but the merchant insisted it was payment for a service. Sahir accepted the exchange with thanks.

5. **A Name Given**

The merchant asked if Sahir had decided where he was going, and Sahir said he had. When Sahir offered his own name and asked how the merchant would be known, the man said he had known very many names, most of which he did not favor, but that Sahir could call him **Calus**, with a K if it mattered. Sahir said it mattered if it mattered to **Calus**. The merchant said he was just happy to be called at all. With mid-afternoon light around them, Sahir took his leave, walking the right road until it curved, then pausing to sketch the encounter in his notebook before moving on.

6. **The Road Widens**

The further Sahir walked, the less strange the road felt, until he recognized it as a tributary road leading to a caravan meeting area outside one of the larger towns. He played with the compass as he walked, noting that it stayed true to one direction no matter how he turned. The road, he reflected, was an interesting place. By the golden hour he could make out a relatively large caravan stopped ahead, and he pressed on toward it, his staff and traveling gear marking him as nothing other than a wanderer on the road.

7. Volkath's Tale

The scene shifted to the party seated around a campfire, where Volkath had volunteered to give them a tale from his perspective, a story of the world as it is. He spoke of the Tulpar people, who had been servants and slaves for thousands of years before the fall of the last great empire, and who had raged for decades after their release, losing perhaps thirty to forty percent of their people in pointless battles. Over centuries they had found a place on the road as traders, moving goods and culture along the great east-west routes. Volkath ended his long description with optimistic statements about how far his people had come, then smiled at each of them in turn and asked if there were any questions.

8. **The Question of Memory**

Raizen asked whether this information was something they already knew or something new. The answer was that none of them recognized the stories, though they understood the world contained these communities. Cal observed that it had been quite some time since they were last together, and Raizen said he remembered being with Cal before, that they had all seen each other and remembered each other suddenly. Cal said they had no stories about each other, only a deep and abiding trust. The group fell into a side conversation about names, with Veyra admitting she would not remember their names for many sessions, and Cal and Raizen exchanging their own names quietly.

9. **Skepticism and Reunion**

Veyra voiced her skepticism, asking whether this was the same man who had told them they fell out of the sky the last time he told a story. The group acknowledged that Volkath had indeed said that, and that this was the story. Veyra was not sure she believed him. The conversation turned again to the strange nature of their connection, the knowledge of each other without any recollection of context, united before and now reunited. Cal mused that in his experience most things were some great purpose no longer. The firelight held them all in its small circle as the evening settled around their unanswered questions.

## Session 1-B

The companions gathered around a campfire outside the walls of Rabati Malik, a caravan at rest and a world of unanswered questions pressing in around them. What began as a quiet evening of uneasy revelations, an experiment with a strange disc, and a confession about a ring that would not come off, turned by morning into a parting of ways and the threat of an ambush. Veyra, driven by a longing for mountains and goats, chose to walk away from the group, convinced that purpose was a trap and peace lay elsewhere. The others, still bound by an inexplicable trust and the weight of objects they did not fully understand, turned their attention to a crisis within the caravan itself. Volkath, the caravan master, revealed that a splinter faction, led by his own son, had lured Imperial troops toward a deadly ambush. With the caravan's future hanging in the balance, the party resolved to intervene, splitting their efforts between the Imperials and the would-be ambushers as the session drew to a close.

### Key Events

1. **The Experiment by the Fire**

The night air around the campfire was thick with confusion as Rill tried to explain a strange absence, a sense he normally possessed that seemed to vanish whenever he stood near the others. He admitted he had been testing the feeling, moving among them to see if the effect came from one person or all of them, and it seemed to be all of them. Ariel, ever curious, proposed an experiment involving the disc she carried. As she handed it to Rill and backed away, the group watched in silence, waiting for some sign of change. Rill felt nothing at first, and then, as he set the disc down and walked toward the panther, an itch bloomed on his ring finger, a faint echo of the connection he had lost. When he returned the disc to Ariel, the tension he had not realized he was holding eased from his shoulders, leaving him with the unsettling conclusion that he had learned absolutely nothing and only gained a brand new question.

2. **The Ring and the Book**

The conversation turned to the black ring on Ril&#x6C;**'s** finger, a ring he said would not come off. He explained that it seemed to pulse when he was away from the others, and that it had appeared after he read a book at a secular monastery and did the things the book described. Cal remarked dryly that some people woke up with a lampshade, but Rill had woken up with a ring. Rill confessed he had been awake the whole time, and that the ring had been with him for a month and a half, ever since the strange events at the monastery. The others listened, their unease growing, as it became clear that each of them carried an object from their awakening, and that these objects were linked to them in ways none of them understood.

3. **Memories of a Warrior**

The firelight flickered as the group turned their attention to Veyra, whose bearing and presence spoke of a warrior's soul beneath the goat herder's life she claimed. Raizen observed that she had not always been a goat herder, and the others agreed that the goat herding was not the person they remembered. Veyra herself declared that her goats were her primary motivation, and that anyone who knew her would know it, and would likely walk away with goat hair on their clothes. The group pondered the strange gap between the Veyra they knew and the Veyra who sat before them, a woman of two skills: herding goats and killing people, and she was over the killing.

4. **A Walk Among the Animals**

As the night deepened, Ariel rose to walk through the caravan, checking on the animals and watching for any sign of abuse or neglect. What she found was a borrowed respect, a practical ethos that kept the creatures healthy not out of love for nature, but because it was simply the right way to run a caravan. The people were decent, their care born of necessity rather than passion, and Ariel returned to the fire satisfied that Volkath ran a caravan where profit did not come at the cost of cruelty.

5. **Dawn and Departure**

The sun rose over the caravan, and with it came the sounds of a camp stirring to life. Raizen remarked that proper bandits did not rise until after ten, but the caravan was already moving. Veyra climbed to a rise to look for mountains on the horizon, and when she returned, she made a quiet announcement: it had been fun, but she was walking back the way she came. She was looking for a new home, for mountains and goats, and she did not expect to find them with the caravan. The others watched as she gathered her few provisions and began to walk east, away from the caravan's westward path.

6. **The Seraph's Plea**

Ariel flew down to intercept Veyra, asking where she was going and whether she felt the group had been brought together for a reason. Veyra's answer was bitter: the only otherworldly instinct she had ever felt was her sword wanting her to kill people. She had no use for a higher purpose, no faith in the idea that they were meant to find some ultimate good. When Ariel pressed, Veyra asked how many people she would have to kill before they reached that ultimate good, and declared that the feeling of being manipulated was all that purpose ever was. She advised Ariel to find peace and stay there, and then she turned and continued east, leaving the seraph to fly back to the others with the news.

7. **The Aftermath of Her Leaving**

Back at the camp, the group absorbed the news of Veyr&#x61;**'s** departure. Cal asked if they had let her go, and Ariel explained that she had chosen her own path. Raizen accepted it with a quiet fatalism, saying that for now his path aligned with theirs. Cal offered a pragmatic hope: if Veyra's goal was to die as a goat herder of old age, she could have an adventure first. The group considered the road ahead, knowing that Veyra walked east while the caravan moved west, and that unless she stopped or they made very good time, their paths would not cross again soon.

8. **The Caravan Master's Problem**

The morning's routine was interrupted when a messenger from the nearby city approached Volkath with news that darkened his face. He came to the group with a problem: the high-spirited folk in his caravan, the ones who had taken umbrage at the Imperial tax collectors' renegotiation, had moved against his directions. They had lured some Imperial troops back toward the city and were now planning an ambush within the city limits. Volkath feared the fallout: if the soldiers had already communicated with their superiors, a larger force could come down on the caravan, and word of the miscreants would travel down the road after them.

9. **The Son's Rebellion**

Volkath revealed the full truth: the head miscreant was his own son. The young man and a handful of well-trained, very spiritual followers had decided that the order to stand down was not Volkath's to give. They wanted the fight, and they had acted on that desire. Cal pressed Volkath on what he was willing to pay the Imperials, what he was willing to lose, and the caravan master admitted he would not choose to lose his son or any of the people under his care. The group understood then that this was not a simple matter of discipline; it was a family crisis wrapped in a political one.

10. **A Plan to Expose the Ambush**

Raizen declared that they should go expose the ambush, and the group began to divide their efforts. Ariel would fly to intercept the Imperials and persuade them not to continue, while Rill, who had traveled with the caravan for a month, offered to go to the son and try to reason with him. He did not want to see anyone get hurt. Cal made a startling offer: he pulled out his eye and gave it to Ariel, telling her to give it to the son so he would know what Cal looked like. The group prepared to split up, with Ariel carrying one of them as a passenger, and the session ended with the party poised to act, the ambush still hours away and the fate of the caravan hanging in the balance.

### Critical Decisions & Unresolved Threads

The session was defined by two major decisions: Veyr&#x61;**'s** choice to leave the group in search of a new home, and the party's commitment to intervene in the caravan's internal crisis. Veyra's departure left the group without their warrior, and her bitterness toward the idea of a higher purpose made clearer the mystery of why they had all been brought together. The experiment with Arie&#x6C;**'s** disc and Ril&#x6C;**'s** ring deepened the mystery of the objects they carried, suggesting a link between the artifacts and the strange connection, or lack thereof, that Rill felt around the others. The unresolved question of the missing shared memories, the trust they felt without stories to explain it, and the strange pull of north that Sahir had encountered all remained open threads. The immediate crisis, however, was the ambush: Volkat&#x68;**'s** son had defied him, Imperial troops were walking into a trap, and the group had only hours to prevent bloodshed.

### Critical Decisions & Unresolved Threads

The next session will likely open with the party split between two urgent missions: Ariel flying toward the Imperials to persuade them to turn back, and Rill, possibly with Cal or another companion, heading toward the ambush site to confront Volkat&#x68;**'s** son. The tension between the caravan master's desire for peace and his love for his family will test the group's loyalties and their ability to negotiate without violence. Meanwhile, Veyr&#x61;**'s** journey east may intersect with the party's path in unexpected ways, especially if the ambush draws Imperial attention or if her search for goats leads her back toward the caravan. The deeper mysteries, the objects, the missing memories, and the strange forces at work in the world, will continue to simmer beneath the surface, waiting for the moment when the group is forced to confront them.
