# Session 0: Sentinel Archetype Awakening (Actual)

## The Waking

Height first — an openness that had no walls, extending toward a horizon the dark had made theoretical. Something vast lay below. The stone under the cheek was cold and hard, just as solidly real.

Where this was, the question surfaced without urgency. The dark didn't answer.

Wind moved — cold air, old stone, and beneath that, something almost loam. Stars overhead, closer than they should have been. Fitted stone under the hands, worn smooth by other hands on other nights. A courtyard resolved in starlight: walls chest-high in places, gone entirely in others.

The signal post stood at the parapet's highest corner. The iron bracket was cold, ash long dead inside it. Beside it, sheltered: a lantern, flint and tinder set next to it, ready.

The spark caught on the third strike.

The light came out divided — a crack ran the lantern's full length, old damage, and the flame found both paths at once: forward over the valley, sideways at an angle no whole lantern should allow.

Then, from below, in two directions at once: voices.

---

## Two Directions

The fortress showed itself in pieces. Chest-high walls in places, gone entirely in others. A narrow approach a single person could hold against many. The drop sheer on three sides.

Below: two gorges. Left, the correct path, climbing toward the post in switchbacks. Right, a tributary that looked like it ran parallel but didn't — it curved away, narrowed, and dead-ended.

Left, closer: several voices, feet on stone, someone giving direction with the calm of a person used to deciding things in the dark. Water sound beneath them. Rising.

Right, fainter: fewer voices, slower, a silence between exchanges that wasn't good news. Going deeper instead of turning back.

Then the left voice reached up — someone had spotted the light, asking if anyone was there, asking for the path down. The water had already taken one of the lower crossings.

From the right, fragments carried up through the narrower walls — someone arguing about a way through that wasn't there, someone else suggesting they turn back. Then, louder, aimed upward this time: was anyone there. They'd taken a wrong turn. They needed direction.

---

## The Choice

It didn't take long to decide. Left was close, climbing correctly, disciplined — people who might make it with a guide, if a guide could reach them fast enough. Right was already lost, already headed the wrong way, and the post's light wasn't going to reach that gorge in any way that mattered — crack or no crack.

The lantern stayed at the post, still burning, still throwing its divided light over a valley that no longer needed to be shown where the parapet was.

The descent started on foot. It didn't stay that way.

## Not This Fortress, Not This Gorge

![[Not this Gorge - Sentinel MUSIC.mp3]]

*Not this fortress. Not this gorge.*

*A watchtower at a different pass — larger, older, a structure that had held its position for three hundred years before the night in question. The torches lit along the wall as they always were, the signal fire at the tower's crown, everything correct, everything in place.*

*The position was held. The signal burned.*

*Below, on the road, the column moved through in good order — four thousand people, the count that mattered, the ones the position existed to protect. They came through the narrows and out the other side and the passage was clean.*

*The fourth thousand. The last of them.*

*And then the messenger, arriving from the direction that had not been watched, because the position was designed to watch the road and not the eastern ridge, because presence at one point cannot be presence at all points, because a watch-fire is a fixed thing and fixed things have limits.*

*What happened on the eastern ridge that night?*

*The count that mattered was four thousand. The count that came through was four thousand. Both true.*

*And the watch-fire was still burning when morning came, exactly as it should have been, having done exactly what it was built to do.*

*The lantern still in hand. And yet... did the signal stand correct?*

*The question that has no answer forming in the bones like cold: what is protection, if it only covers what it was aimed at?*

## The Fox

The change happened somewhere on the switchbacks — the shape going smaller, lower, four legs finding purchase where two had been negotiating loose stone. A fox's build: quick, low to the ground, built for exactly this kind of terrain in the dark.

It covered ground the climb wouldn't have allowed. Close enough to the left approach to see the group without being seen — gauge how many, how close to the water they were, how much longer the path held before the flood decided the question for everyone.

Moving well. Scared, but moving well.

That was enough to know what came next.

---

## The Guide

The group rounded a bend in the switchback path and found a stranger standing where the trail leveled — not where anyone expected someone to be, but there all the same.

No one had seen the change happen. One moment the path ahead was empty; the next it wasn't.

The woman leading them — scarred at the jaw, the kind of scar that comes from teaching yourself to give directions in the dark — stopped the group with a raised hand before anyone else did.

"Who—"

"This way's faster," the Sentinel said. "There's a ledge past the switchback that cuts across. I'll show you."

She looked at him for a beat too long to be simple trust, then nodded once, sharp. "Show us."

The Sentinel led. The woman kept the group close behind, moving them together over the harder sections, hands finding hands wherever the footing gave out.

They came up onto the fortress stones with the particular silence of people who'd been keeping quiet too long and didn't yet know they were allowed to stop.

"You found us," the woman said. Not quite gratitude. Close to it.

---

## The Silence

From the right gorge — nothing, for a long while. Then, once, faint: a voice, maybe several, carried up wrong through the narrow walls. Whether it had been a cry for help was impossible to say from this distance.

No one went to check.

Later — how much later was hard to say — the sound from that direction changed. Water where voices had been.

---

## The Road

Dawn came into the gorge from the east, later than the open sky above, like the canyon needed convincing.

The people who'd made it were scattered across the high stones of the courtyard. Some sitting. Some still standing, because sitting down meant admitting the thing chasing them wasn't chasing them anymore, and not everyone was ready for that yet.

The flood wasn't going anywhere for hours. The valley road was impassable. The only way forward was the ridge trail the fortress sat above, connecting eventually to the road west.

A caravan was already out there somewhere.

"West's the only direction," the woman said — Zere, she gave her name somewhere in the climb, though it took a while to stick. "There's a merchant train. We saw their dust before all this happened. They'll take survivors. Good business for them."

The lantern hung unlit, forgotten at the belt — it hadn't done the work tonight. Something else had. The crack still ran its length, old, worn smooth. Nobody looked at it twice.

From the right gorge: still nothing. No one asked. No one needed to.

The road west formed out of the people who'd made it. The Sentinel walked among them — not quite leading, not quite following — carrying the same question the fortress hadn't answered, and wouldn't.
