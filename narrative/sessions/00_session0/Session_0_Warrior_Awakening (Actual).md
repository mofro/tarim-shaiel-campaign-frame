# Session 0: Warrior Archetype Awakening

## The Waking

The Warrior came up cold — not clean winter cold but something that had settled into bone. Her left arm was numb, crushed beneath her. When she flexed her fingers, they moved like things belonging to someone else.

The smells arrived before anything else made sense: unwashed bodies, low fires, grain going sour, and underneath everything the sharp copper of fear-sweat. The reek of people who understood the value of being small.

A child saw her wake and bolted — not toward safety, just away, bare feet finding purchase in the muck. Running deeper into camp, toward the woman with the soldier's eyes.

Near the closest fire, an old woman stirred a pot. Didn't look up. Her shoulders tracked the sound of movement anyway.

The blade came up with her arm, trailing muck. Crude iron — no shine, no pride. Just function hammered into shape. She looked at it.

*--This is my chain--*

---

## Confrontation

The camp reacted. Voices spiked — not panic, commands. The woman with military bearing rose in one motion, hand finding her spear. She didn't point it. She didn't need to.

Around the fires, a man gathered his family and moved them back. The old woman kept stirring. Her shoulders had angled toward the stranger.

The Warrior's hand had found the blade without decision. Muscle memory.

The woman approached — measured, each step tested before commitment. Twenty feet out she dropped into a crouch.

The soldier: "Don't move."

Fragments from deeper in camp: *"...where did they..." "...just there, no warning..." "...armed..."* One older voice cut through: "Keep the children back."

The soldier: "I'm going to ask you something. Think before you answer. What do you remember?"

Warrior: "Not a lot. Who are you all? Refugees it seems."

The soldier: "That's right."

---

## Questions and Answers

The conversation settled into something tense but not unfriendly. The Warrior asked many questions — where they'd come from, why they were out here. The woman explained: their town was assaulted. Something monstrous that no one could make sense of — a battle no one understood, that everyone simply fled. She'd marshaled the retreat with everyone she could save.

The Warrior asked about the town. Which direction.

The woman said it was gone: Nothing to see. But she gestured vaguely. The Warrior scanned the horizon anyway and could just make out a smear of smoke still hanging there. Perhaps a day's walk. She decided not to go tonight.

At some point — without announcement — the blade went back. Not surrender. Not a gesture of trust. Just a recognition that standing in a stranger's camp with a weapon drawn while not leaving was its own kind of statement. The wrist-binding still carried its weight. The blade was still there.

The camp adjusted. Not full acceptance. Adjusted distance.

---

## The Child and the Old Woman

The child watched from behind the fighter for a long time — tracking movement, cataloging threat. Eventually curiosity started winning.

Child: "Are you like the ones who burned the village?"

Warrior: "No. I just raise goats."

Child (confused): "So you... You're a goat-herder."

The old woman didn't announce herself. One moment the Warrior was sitting with the soldier's eyes on her; the next there was a bowl. Thin soup — barely more than water with grain's ghost in it. Hot.

The handoff — bowl to hands — was the whole transaction. The old woman smiled as she did it. Not warmth, exactly. Something older. Something that had already made up its mind and found no need to explain itself.

She moved on. Didn't settle. Didn't press.

The woman watched. Eyes narrowed slightly. Not disapproval. Assessment.

---

## The Scout

A scout ran in from the camp's edge — urgent and efficient. Old blood caking her tunic's shoulder.

"Soldiers. Imperial patrol. Moving through. Maybe an hour."

It took very little for the news to spread. The camp moved. Practiced. No panic. Tents folding, fires dying, possessions gathered, children pulled close. The choreography of people who had done this before.

The woman looked at the Warrior directly.

"We can't fight them. Eight soldiers. We're thirty — most children, most old. Fight, and everyone dies. Run, maybe they let us go. But we need someone watching the perimeter. In case they come from a direction we're not expecting. You can do that. Can't you?"

The Warrior assented. Went to the perimeter, scanned the distance, saw movement. Moved back into a covered position and waited.

---

## The Perimeter

Questions surfaced in the stillness — position, distance, direction, purpose. The answers came from listening and watching.

Eight men. Loose formation. No consistent direction to their scanning.

They didn't move like soldiers who knew what they were after. They moved like men who were running from something. Perhaps whatever had driven these people into the ravine had been chasing them too.

The blade waited. The Warrior held. When it became clear that they were almost close enough to discover the camp — Something... flashed.

---

## Stone Beneath Your Feet

![[Stone Beneath Your Feet - Warrior MUSIC.mp3]]

Stone beneath your feet. Worn smooth by years of standing watch, or perhaps just worn by your memory of standing watch—hard to tell which came first. The darkness around you isn't empty. It's full of breathing. Thirty people hiding in the ravine behind you, trusting you to be the thing between them and what hunts.

Not soldiers. Not warriors who chose this. Refugees. The old woman who brought you soup without asking questions. The child with eyes too old, who wanted to know if you're like the ones who burned the village. People who didn't ask for this war, this hunt, this fear. They just want to survive another night.

Your duty is simple. Clear. Keep them safe.

But the blade—

The soldiers are close now. You can hear boots testing ground, voices conferring in low tones. Your hand knows the grip. Your body knows the draw. The blade at your hip doesn't sing—it *howls*. One motion and you could end this. One stroke and the threat dissolves.

Because the blade is *there* and the threat is *there* and your body is screaming that the space between them should be closed. Now. Fast. Final.

Whose voice is this? Whose thirst?

The blade howls but your gut whispers: what about the people behind you? What begins when steel meets steel and thirty terrified souls realize the armed stranger they're hiding with just became the violence they were fleeing?

So you stand. Muscles coiled. Breath controlled. Power surging through you like water behind a dam, and you—you are the dam. You are the choice to hold.

The boots pass. The voices fade. They never knew you were there.

You're still standing. Still holding the flood inside. Still choosing restraint over release.

And somehow—despite every instinct, despite the blade's hunger, despite the screaming need to ACT—

This feels like victory.

---

## Morning

Dawn came. The woman approached.

"Scouts say there's a caravan. Two hours west. Merchants heading for the coastal cities. We're going with them—safer in numbers than out here alone."

A pause.

"You can come. If you want."

Another pause. Eyes reading.

"You can fight, can't you? I mean... really fight."

The Warrior went with them. Didn't say much. Just stood when the camp moved, and moved when they did.

The questions that surfaced weren't about the caravan — they were quieter and more fundamental: how did I get here. Which direction was the farm. What is happening. Nobody had answers, and asking out loud didn't produce any. The camp moved west. The Warrior moved with it — not because the path made sense, but because staying alone in a ravine made less.

She went. Quietly. Still confused.
