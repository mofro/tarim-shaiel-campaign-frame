# Two Doors

*A choice for your Vanara Warlock. Read both. Pick the one that makes your chest tighten.*

---

You were a student at a mountain academy. You know what was expected of you: the body trained, the text mastered, both disciplines feeding each other, both offered up to something larger than yourself. You know — better than anyone else will ever know — the exact size of the gap between what was expected and what you could deliver.

And you know what you did about it.

You used the archives. That was the one thing you were genuinely good at — finding what others had stopped looking for. Somewhere in the deep stacks, in texts nobody had opened in living memory, you found the shape of an old rite. A way of asking. A way of offering.

You asked. Something answered.

The power is real. It has never once failed you. That is not the problem.

The problem is that your teachers found out — and they did not rage. That would have been easier. They looked at you the way you look at a beautiful scroll that has been water-damaged: with grief, and with the terrible knowledge that what's done to certain things cannot be undone. They didn't cast you out. They *let you go*, which is worse, and they made it clear that what you carry cannot come back through the gate with you.

So now the question — the one that decides who your patron is, and what kind of story you're in:

**When you asked, who answered?**

---

## Door One: The One Below

*The register of this story: haunted.*

You didn't find this one in the treaty records. You found it in the margins — in fragments the archive kept but did not catalog, in the kind of text that survives because destroying it felt riskier than shelving it. The old Orcish sources call it **the First Gone**. The Dwarven miners' glossaries call it the **Lord of Exhausted Seams** — the thing that is left when a seam is worked out. The thing that presides over whatever is finished.

You performed the rite on the worst night of your life. You want to be honest about this part: you were not thinking clearly, and you were not precise about what you offered, and the texts were fragmentary, and you have never been able to reconstruct exactly what you said.

What answered was patient. Old the way the dark under the mountain is old. It did not bargain sharply. It did not seem hungry. It listened to what you wanted — *the strength to be what they need me to be* — and it granted it, simply, the way you'd hand a coin to a child.

You have been waiting ever since to find out what it took in exchange. You don't know. That's the truth you can't say out loud: **you don't know what you paid.** The power comes when you call it, cool and steady and bottomless, and every time it comes you listen for the sound of the other shoe.

**What playing this door feels like:**
- Your secret is a *secret*. Nobody who looks at your magic can tell where it came from — but you can feel where it came from, every time.
- Your patron has never asked you for anything. This is the most frightening thing about it.
- The question that follows you: *what did I promise, and when does it come due?*
- Protecting people feels like defiance. You draw on something that presides over endings, and you spend it to keep things from ending. Some nights that feels like theft. Some nights it feels like the only genuinely good thing you've ever done.

---

## Door Two: The Storm Claimant

*The register of this story: indebted.*

This one you found exactly where it was supposed to be — that's the bitter joke of it. The old mountain treaties, the ones the Dwarves sealed and your academy archived, carry a mark: the sign of the **Sky-Lord's General**, the martial heaven, the storm-court that has held territory and taken tribute since before the passes had names. The rite of petition wasn't hidden. It was *filed*. An appendix. A protocol for asking the sky for strength, preserved by scholars who assumed no one would ever be desperate enough to use it.

You were that desperate. And your petition was answered promptly, formally, and in full.

There was no seduction and no trick. The terms were read to you the way a quartermaster reads a requisition: this is what you receive, this is what you owe, this is when it is owed. You understood every word. You agreed to every word. The power arrived like a commission — strength lent from a standing army, with your name entered in a roll somewhere above the weather.

Your academy found out because this kind of fealty is *legible*. You had bound yourself — used the sacred instrument of devotion, the thing your whole culture is built on — and pointed it at a foreign heaven, on terms your teachers never sanctioned. They could see the bond the way they could see a vow. The grief on their faces wasn't fear of what you'd become. It was recognition of what you'd *signed*.

**What playing this door feels like:**
- Nothing about your situation is mysterious. That's the weight of it. You know exactly what you owe. Ignorance would be a mercy you don't get.
- Your patron communicates. Expectations arrive. Service is periodically required. It is never cruel and never negotiable.
- The question that follows you: *what happens when what I owe conflicts with what I protect?*
- Protecting people is why you did it — and your strength genuinely serves that. But the strength is *lent*, and lent things get recalled. You are strong on schedule. You dread the day the schedule and the need disagree.

## The Short Version

| | Door One | Door Two |
|---|---|---|
| The answer to your petition | Something old, patient, unhurried | Something formal, martial, exact |
| What you know about the price | Nothing — and it keeps you up at night | Everything — and it keeps you up at night |
| Your secret | Invisible, deniable, corrosive | Legible, undeniable, public to those who can read it |
| The story you're in | A haunting | A debt |
| Your quiet fear | *What did I promise?* | *What will they ask of me?* |

Both doors give you the same thing: real power, honestly obtained by a desperate young scholar, at a cost that drove a wedge between you and everyone you did it for.

The difference is the shape of the shadow it casts.

Pick your shadow.
