## Act 0 — Frame

### Beat 0: The Roads

**Setting:** The trade roads of Tarim-Shaiel. No physical location. The voice of the road itself.
**Present:** (framing narration only — no player characters)
**What happened:** GM reads the Opening Narration. Post-imperial world established: the roads were here before the empire, survived its fall, are busy again. Commerce, culture, movement. Underneath all of it — a held breath. Something expectant.
**Turning point:** "As if the road itself is waiting for someone."
**World-state change:** The world is now present to the players. The wrongness is named: not broken, not dangerous — *expectant.*
**Narrator hooks:** Road as structural metaphor and living geography. Post-imperial melancholy without nihilism. Yūsra's epigraph belongs here. The gap between what the roads carry and what they remember.

## Act 1 — The Awakenings

### Beat 1: Warrior — The Waking

**Setting:** Ravine refugee camp. Night. Cold mud, low fires barely confessing heat, 30 people in a depression carved into the landscape. Smell-dominant: fear-sweat, unwashed bodies, sour grain, smoke so low it's barely there.
**Present:** Warrior (alone in mud), Camp (sleeping/hiding)
**What happened:** Warrior comes to awareness — cold before sight, smell before context. The blade emerges from the mud with the arm, bound at the wrist by a leather thong. Simple iron, crude, no shine. When Warrior looks at it, the first thought that arrives is not recognition or relief.
**Turning point:** "This is my chain." — weight, not weapon-memory. The blade as burden before it is a tool.
**World-state change:** Warrior awake, armed, unknown to themselves. 30 refugees now have an armed stranger in their camp.
**Narrator hooks:** Cold comes first. Mud that has given up. Burial-layered smells. Blade: "function hammered into shape by someone who knew what they were doing but didn't waste time making it pretty." The thong tight enough to announce itself with every pulse.

---

### Beat 2: Warrior — The Camp Sees Them

**Setting:** Same ravine camp. Warrior stands or sits up. The camp is dim but not dark.
**Present:** Warrior, Camp (30 people), Child (~5, wide eyes, already learned to be wary), Old Woman (~70, stirring a pot with deliberate patience), Kira (woman with military bearing, scars on forearms, systematic perimeter scan)
**What happened:** Movement brings context piece by piece. The child is the first to register the Warrior's consciousness and bolts — not toward safety, just away. Voices spike, not into panic but commands. Kira rises in one motion. Hand finds spear. Doesn't point it.
**Turning point:** The child runs. The Warrior is the problem that doesn't belong.
**World-state change:** Camp on alert but not panicked — this is practiced choreography. Warrior identified as threat.
**Narrator hooks:** "Dolorous architecture" — cloth over frames never meant to bear weight this long. Kira's scan as professional assessment, not fear. Old Woman keeps stirring. The child who has learned not to cry too loud. Warrior's hand found the blade grip before they noticed they'd stood.

---

### Beat 3: Warrior — Kira's Question

**Setting:** Same camp. Kira 20 feet away in combat crouch — ready to spring but not advancing.
**Present:** Warrior, Kira, Camp watching (even the Old Woman has stopped stirring)
**What happened:** Kira approaches measured, settles into crouch. "I'm going to ask you something. Think before you answer." The whole camp waits.
**Turning point:** "What do you remember?" — not accusation, not demand. She's looking for honesty, not coherence. The silence of the camp carries the question.
**World-state change:** Warrior faces the first moment of genuine agency in this world. Their answer sets the trust trajectory.
**Narrator hooks:** Weight of the silence. Kira's crouch as professional stance, not threat. The blade felt natural before the Warrior noticed they were holding it. First "What do you do?" of the session.

---

### Beat 4: Warrior — Trust Architecture

**Setting:** Same camp. Extended — this beat breathes.
**Present:** Warrior, Kira, Old Woman, Child (watching from distance), Camp
**What happened:** Sustained presence. Warrior demonstrates vulnerability or triggers threat response over several minutes. The Old Woman eventually rises without announcement and sets a bowl of thin soup nearby. Doesn't ask permission. Doesn't ask questions.
**Turning point:** The Old Woman's soup. Her trust arrives like weather — chosen, not earned, not requiring proof. When she sits, Kira's readiness shifts slightly: small nod. "The old woman trusts. Maybe we wait."
**World-state change:** Trust threshold crossed. Conditional and provisional, but crossed. The Old Woman has made her decision; Kira is watching that decision.
**Narrator hooks:** Soup "barely more than water with grain's ghost in it, but hot." The Old Woman's unhurried quality — someone who has made a decision that doesn't require proof. The specific geometry of where people stood and sat as trust built. Kira's nod: small, carrying the camp's weight.

---

### Beat 5: Warrior — The Child's Question

**Setting:** Same camp. Child still behind Kira, watching. Minutes have passed.
**Present:** Warrior, Child, Kira (watching), Old Woman (nearby)
**What happened:** Curiosity has been winning slowly over fear. The child speaks across the space, not coming close: "Are you like the ones who burned the village?"
**Turning point:** The question is not accusation. It is need. The real question underneath: *Are you what happened to us?* A child who shouldn't know yet to ask this — but does.
**World-state change:** How the Warrior answers and how they say it determines whether the child believes them safe. Through the child, whether Kira does too. This is the session's moral center.
**Narrator hooks:** The question a child asks when they've already learned the answer might be yes. The Warrior making themselves smaller. The weight of being capable of harm while trying not to be. Kira watching everything.

---

### Beat 6: Warrior — The Scout's Warning

**Setting:** Camp edge. Movement.
**Present:** Warrior, Kira, Scout (female, fit, shoulder wound bleeding through cloth), Camp
**What happened:** Scout runs in urgent. Not panicked — this is practiced. "Soldiers. Imperial patrol. Moving through. Maybe an hour." The camp immediately begins disappearing: tents fold, fires die, children pulled close. Choreography of people who know what happens if they don't.
**Turning point:** Kira looks at the Warrior directly: "We can't fight them. Eight soldiers. We're thirty — most children, most old. Fight, and everyone dies." Pause. "We need someone watching the perimeter. You can do that. Can't you?"
**World-state change:** Warrior formally assigned as protector. Not invited — required. Role defined by the camp's need, not the Warrior's capability. This is what protection looks like before it is called by that name.
**Narrator hooks:** "Can't fight them" as the constraint the Warrior's whole being resists. Kira's phrasing — not "will you?" but "you can, can't you?" — she's already decided. The soldiers are real; the camp has seen this before.

### Beat 7: Warrior — The Perimeter Choice

**Setting:** Camp perimeter. Warrior alone. Night sounds, approaching boots.
**Present:** Warrior (alone), 30 refugees (hidden behind), Imperial soldiers (8, loose formation, scanning without purpose — something is wrong with them)
**What happened:** Soldiers approach near-range. Archetypal memory screams "draw." The blade howls. Warrior must hold or draw. [PATH BRANCHES HERE]

**Path A — Restraint:** Warrior holds. Muscles coiled, breath controlled, power behind a dam — and the Warrior is the dam. Soldiers pass. They never knew anyone was there.
**Path B — Draw:** Hand moves before thought does. The body takes over. [Roll determines outcome.]
- *High:* Soldiers prostrate — hollow men who've already been through something they can't name.
- *Mid:* Soldiers scatter. Warrior chased men who were already running.
- *Low:* Combat. Warrior's body handles it with unsettling fluency. Blade quiet after. Hands steady.

**Turning point:** The moment of choice. For Path A: the instant of holding. For Path B: the hand moving without being asked.
**World-state change:** Path A: 30 people survive. The Warrior discovers restraint is its own form of arrival. Path B: consequences differentiated — from full trust (B3) to trust-at-distance (B2) to camp mostly gone (B1).
**Narrator hooks:** Snippet text belongs here for both paths. "The blade doesn't howl now. It hums." (Path B) vs. "This feels like victory." (Path A). The soldiers' broken state — they're survivors of something that destroyed their company in ways that don't match any known force.

### Beat 8: Warrior — The Morning After

**Setting:** Dawn. Camp, or what remains of it.
**Present:**
- Path A: Warrior, Kira, Old Woman, Camp
- Path B2/B3: Warrior, Old Woman (bridge), Kira (at distance)
- Path B1: Warrior, Old Woman, Man with Wrapped Hand (stayed — didn't run; why is not explained)

**What happened:** Dawn reveals the shape of what the night made.
- Path A: Kira approaches. Soldiers passed. Warrior protected through restraint. "You can come. If you want." A pause. "You can fight, can't you? I mean — *really* fight." The first time she's treated the Warrior as a person, not a problem.
- Path B2: Old Woman extends the invitation Kira won't. "There's a merchant train. West." She mentions Kira by name — only time it's spoken.
- Path B1: Most of the camp is gone before light. Two remain: the Old Woman, the Man with the Wrapped Hand. The Old Woman: "I'll be going the same direction." The Man gets to his feet without being asked, doesn't speak, keeps distance. He carries a question, not a confession.

**Turning point:** The invitation to join the caravan. Regardless of path, the direction is west.
**World-state change:** Warrior integrated into the westward group. Trust level varies; trajectory is the same.
**Narrator hooks:** Kira's nod (Path A) vs. her absence (Path B1). The Old Woman as the constant. "Scouts say there's a caravan. Two hours west." The Man with the Wrapped Hand — do not explain him. He travels at his own distance and carries something unresolved.

### Beat 9: Seeker — Desert Awakening

**Setting:** Rocky outcrop above desert wasteland. Dusk bleeding into night. Heat still radiating from stone. Sand in all directions.
**Present:** Seeker alone. Desert, stars, tome.
**What happened:** Seeker has been walking — doesn't know how long. Reaches high ground. Stars emerge. Body responds with relief before thought arrives: *stars mean navigation.* Then: the star charts in the tome don't match the sky overhead. The patterns are wrong in a way that is subtle but unforgiving. The tome is in hand — leather worn, pages from much use, confident ink. When did they pick it up? No memory of acquiring it.
**Turning point:** "Perhaps they're correct, and the fever is yours alone." — the moment where perfect knowledge and present perception become two incompatible truths.
**World-state change:** Seeker holding both: the certainty in the tome, and the evidence of their own eyes. Neither resolves. The tool they carry is also the thing that might be lying to them.
**Narrator hooks:** "Copper-taste" and throat closing around each breath. Stars as the Seeker's equivalent of the Warrior's blade — the tool that is also the weight. "What madness, what waste, what carelessness could convince an adept to spend so much time devoted to marking things so poorly?" The tome clutched without memory of picking it up. Sensory register: heat, dust, expanse, the unreliability of the horizon.

---

### Beat 10: Seeker — The Direction Choice

**Setting:** Same outcrop. Full dark. Two directions pulling equally.
**Present:** Seeker alone. The tome. The desert.
**What happened:** Northeast: the charts say water. Mathematics hold. Two days, certainty, oasis exists. West: something animal, unnamed, no evidence, no charts, no reason. The tome's weight resists west. The body wants west.
**Turning point:** "The winds whisper: Choose." — not dramatic, geometric. Northeast = certainty. West = the thing beneath certainty, the thing that doesn't have charts.
**World-state change:** The Seeker's method — knowledge, precision, cosmic mathematics — is insufficient for the first time. The choice is about which they trust more: what they know, or what they are.
**Narrator hooks:** "Animal knowing that doesn't have words, can't be explained, just *is.*" The tome resisting like a partner unconvinced of the wooing. The Seeker's hands on the leather, deciding. Both paths lead to convergence. Both are valid. The wound they carry going in is different.

---

### Beat 11: Seeker — Resolution (Path-Dependent)

**East path — Knowledge Vindicated, Tool Lost:**
Northeast leads to the oasis. Knowledge was right. Water. Vindication. Then: six riders. Seeker on foot, exhausted, surrounded. The leader notices the tome. "Let me see it." Hands locked, won't open — fingers clenched around leather, knuckles white. Eventually: they open. Tome transfers to raiders. Seeker lighter, emptier. "It felt like something you were always meant to do."

Seeker does not beg or chase. Instead: "Which way are you headed?" Misdirects the raiders northwest (canyon passes, slower, false route). Raiders ride northwest. Seeker turns southwest — body navigating by hero-tier instinct. Tracks the caravan by reading sand (wind, shadow-angle, carrion birds over thermals). Arrives with a warning: raiders incoming, 3–4 hours out.

**West path — Faith at Cost:**
Tome pushes back in words: *Death lies West.* All true — northeast means water, northeast means survival. Seeker continues west. "No." Something small and stubborn. Falls at dusk. Unconscious when caravan scouts find them. Tome still clutched in locked hands. Severe dehydration, heat exhaustion. Someone tries gently to remove the tome. The hands won't open even unconscious.

**Turning point:**
- East: Hands opening. The letting go. Lighter than expected.
- West: "No." The defiance that costs everything and feels like a homecoming.

**World-state change:**
- East: Seeker arrives with warning and without the tome. Demonstrated competence; clear stakes (tome recovery possible future action).
- West: Seeker arrives rescued and dependent. Demonstrated stubbornness and faith; vulnerability as entry to the group.

**Narrator hooks:**
- East: "Certainty is the cage." Raider's care in handling the tome — it means something to him too. Hero-tier navigation without instruments (sand as language, dune-shapes as meaning). The misdirection as the first instinct-action.
- West: "The defiance hurts. And it feels like a homecoming." The tome warm against an unconscious chest. Second awakening in the presence of strangers.

## Act 2 — Convergence

### Beat 12: Caravan Meeting

**Setting:** Merchant caravan on the western road. Evening or mid-travel. Wagons, outriders, the particular pace of refugees and merchants who travel together because alone means death.
**Present:** Warrior (with refugees / Old Woman / NPC companions from awakening), Seeker (arrived with warning [East] or rescued [West]), Caravan merchants, NPC threads from awakenings
**What happened:** Heroes discover each other at the caravan. They arrive through different paths, different wounds. Neither knows what the other is. Strangers, by every visible measure.
**Turning point:** Eyes meeting across the camp or the road. Something resonates — not memory, not yet. Just recognition that something is different about these faces.
**World-state change:** Both awakened heroes now in the same place. Westward journey begins. The party is not a party yet — it is a collection of strangers with reasons to travel together.
**Narrator hooks:** "Not memory. Just recognition." The caravan as the world organizing itself. NPC threads: Kira (Path A) or her absence (Path B1), Old Woman (constant), Man with Wrapped Hand (distance), Seeker's warning giving the caravan preparation time (East path). The tome still somewhere in the northwest.
