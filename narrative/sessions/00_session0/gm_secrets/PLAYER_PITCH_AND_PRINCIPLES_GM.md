# Tarim Shaiel — Player Pitch & Principles
## GM Reference Document

⚠️ **GM DOCUMENT — Contains design context players should not have access to.**

The companion document (`PLAYER_PITCH_AND_PRINCIPLES.md`) is what you hand to players at Session Zero — before character creation, before they've seen the full campaign frame. This document explains why each section is written the way it is, provides the cosmological context that gives those choices meaning, and gives guidance on how to deliver the material at the table.

---

## The Dual-Truth Architecture

**What players know:** Their characters are people the world has already formed opinions about. They carry weight they didn't choose. Something unusual is happening in the world's magic. Their essential approach — their method — is the spine of who they are.

**What the GM knows:** These characters ARE the returned heroes of the Heroic Age — drawn back from Hero Heaven to Tarim Shaiel for reasons unknown to them, retaining their essential natures (methods, deep instincts, relationships to action) but not their memories. They are not diminished. They are not weakened. They are not diminished versions of something larger. They are those heroes, now, without access to the history that would tell them so.

This dual-truth structure is the campaign's central architecture. The revelation is meant to arrive through play — through recognition scenes, through Memory Fragments and Vestiges behaving in ways that defy explanation, through NPCs whose reactions carry a weight that can't yet be accounted for.

**Protecting this architecture is the reason the player-facing document is written exactly the way it is.**

---

## Why the Archetype Descriptions Are Written This Way

Every archetype description in the player document describes the character's *current psychological pattern and role function* — the way they habitually meet the world, the gift that comes naturally, the doubt that travels with them. Nothing more.

**This is a locked design decision.** The alternatives — descriptions that hint at legendary past, fallen power, cosmic mystery, or the idea that something has been forgotten or lost — compromise the dual-truth architecture before the campaign starts. Players who sense they're playing returned heroes will reach for that premise rather than staying present with their characters as they are now.

The archetype descriptions are written to be *true* at both layers. The Warrior does stand between others and harm — that is their nature now and was their nature in Hero Heaven. The doubt they carry about restraint is genuinely theirs. Nothing in the description lies to the player. It simply doesn't tell them everything.

**Test for any archetype description:** Does this describe who this person IS, not who they were? Does it contain nothing that would tip a player off to the campaign's premise?

## Cosmological Context Per Archetype

### The Warrior

**Surrendered Layer (Decision 10 — proposed, pending lock):** *Tactical* — force as the clean answer. What was surrendered at the threshold was the version of themselves that solved problems primarily through direct application of their capacity for violence. This is why the Warrior's doubt centers on restraint: it is the precise site of what they gave.

**Tool:** Iron blade with wrapped bone grip. Carries consciousness and agency; can refuse or resist (Decision 5). It howls for use. The Warrior stands in tension with their own tool's nature, and that tension is not weakness — it is the work.

**Awakening status:** ✅ v2.0 complete — the prose benchmark for Session Zero. The defining moment is restraint at the perimeter while soldiers pass. Use as written.

---

### The Breaker

**Surrendered Layer (Decision 10):** *Ontological* — identity defined by what they can destroy. What was surrendered was the operating assumption that destruction of the wrong structure was always justified liberation.

**Tool:** Crude hammer (proposed, pending Decision 10 lock).

**Cosmological note:** The Breaker's aftermath doubt (what did breaking this release?) is the site of the surrendered layer's absence. They feel the question most sharply because the certainty that once answered it was given up at the threshold.

**Awakening status:** 🚧 v0.2.1, Segment 1 only. Scenario: collapsed inn after earthquake; the hammer is also structural support; abandon it or find another way. Use the design framework until complete.

---

### The Bridge

**Surrendered Layer (Decision 10):** *Relational* — the right to have a side. What was surrendered was the capacity to belong fully to any single party, faction, or cause.

**Tool:** Knotted silk cord (proposed, pending Decision 10 lock).

**Cosmological note:** The Bridge's doubt (whether peace built on constant compromise holds anything real) traces directly from the absence. They cannot simply take a side and be done. This is not a failing — it is the form their service takes.

**Awakening status:** Design framework only. Core scenario: prison cell, apparently impossible negotiation, freedom requires finding common ground where none seems to exist.

---

### The Seeker

**Surrendered Layer (Decision 10):** *Epistemic* — certainty as operating system. What was surrendered was the condition of knowing before acting. The Seeker now carries only the question; the comfortable platform of prior certainty was given up.

**Tool:** Tome (proposed — note unresolved tome/bow discrepancy pending Decision 10 lock).

**Cosmological note:** The Seeker's doubt (what do you do with a truth you can't use?) is the exact register of the surrendered layer. They know how to find out. They don't always know what finding out permits them to do next.

**Awakening status:** ✅ v0.4 complete. Scenario: desert wasteland, stars that don't match charts, knowledge vs. instinct. Pending revision to full prose density.

---

### The Sacrificer

**Surrendered Layer (Decision 10):** *Volitional* — the right to refuse. What was surrendered was the option of self-protective non-participation: the ability to say *not my problem* and mean it.

**Tool:** Scale with one pan missing (proposed, pending Decision 10 lock).

**Cosmological note:** The Sacrificer's doubt (whether giving freely over time takes something essential) is the site of the absence because the option to stop giving — to protect the self by withholding — no longer operates cleanly.

**Awakening status:** Design framework only. The Sacrificer does NOT have a standalone awakening — they are woven through Act 2 convergence. They wake already being cared for (poor family's home, recipient of sacrifice rather than its source). This structural inversion is intentional: the archetype that gives without limit must first receive. Do not convert this to a standalone awakening.

---

### The Visionary

**Surrendered Layer (Decision 10):** *Perceptual* — the right to look away. What was surrendered was the protection of not having to see what's coming. The Visionary cannot unsee the path; the option of strategic blindness was given up.

**Tool:** Obsidian disk (proposed, pending Decision 10 lock).

**Cosmological note:** The Visionary's doubt (is this wisdom or comfortable certainty?) traces the precise location of the surrender. They see clearly. They cannot stop seeing. Whether the seeing is trustworthy is the question their archetype turns on.

**Awakening status:** Design framework only. Core scenario: mid-crisis (avalanche/fire/bandits NOW), no time for foresight, act or don't. Structural irony: the archetype that sees the path must act before any path is visible.

## The Surrendered-Layer Framework (Decision 10)

⚠️ **Proposed, pending lock. Do not lock without resolving: Seeker tome/bow discrepancy; Sentinel tool conflict (recording device vs. cracked lantern).**

Each hero surrendered a specific layer of their nature at the threshold — the thing that once made their power clean and fast and unambiguous. What they carry now is the core of what they are, minus the operating certainty that used to make it simple.

This is why none of the archetypes are diminished or powerless. They are the same heroes. The surrendered layer made them capable of something they weren't before: inhabiting the doubt without it destroying them.

**The archetype descriptions describe the character as they currently are — the surrendered layer already gone, the doubt already present.** This is by design. The descriptions are accurate at the player-facing layer and accurate at the cosmological layer simultaneously. Players will feel this as psychological truth without knowing why.

## Cosmic Conscription (Decision 11 — Open)

The reason the heroes were drawn back from Hero Heaven is not yet locked. Two live possibilities:

- **Punishment:** The heroes were expelled from paradise for incomplete duty — they did not finish what they were meant to do, and Hero Heaven does not hold those who have not finished.
- **Deployment:** The heroes were recalled by a force (the Celestial Court, cosmic necessity, something else) because something in Tarim Shaiel requires their specific natures.

**Gameplay implication:** Until Decision 11 is locked, do not let any NPC articulate a clear answer to "why are you here?" The characters' uncertainty about their own origins is structural, not an oversight. The ambiguity is the point until the campaign earns the answer.

## The Held Breath (Decision 2+3)

The cosmic threat framing is plural Held Breath — liminal consciousnesses, not a single sleeping entity. Do NOT use "sleeping entity" language in any GM-facing or player-facing material. "Held Breath" is the canonical term for these liminal presences.

This matters for how the world's magic feels unreliable and why certain places feel wrong. The Held Breath are not dormant. They are present but suspended — waiting for a condition rather than sleeping through one.

## The Campaign's Shape (Decision 8)

Running session zero effectively requires knowing, at least in outline, where this is headed. The campaign resolves in three layers:

1. **Stop the Wizard** — the apparent arc, the threat players can name and point at. This is the surface.
2. **The Threshold breach** — the Wizard's work pushes toward breaching a load-bearing node in the Warren ecosystem. The heroes stop the Wizard; the breach consequence arrives anyway, or nearly. This is the cost.
3. **Warren reckoning** — the heroes must reckon with the disturbance their own liberation has caused and the presence of the Held Breath, whose weight was always the real endgame. This is the truth beneath the truth.

Session zero launches a story that will take players through all three layers. They don't need to know this. You do — so that nothing you say in session zero forecloses a revelation that is still two-thirds of the campaign away.

## Mechanical Identity Carriers

These are how the dual-truth reveals itself through play — not through narration, but through the game's mechanics behaving in ways that defy explanation.

**Vestiges** are physical remnants of the heroes' past — objects, marks, instincts that behave oddly. They awaken slowly. A Vestige doesn't explain anything; it poses a question the character can't yet answer.

**Memory Fragments** are partial recollections that surface at threshold moments — triggered by events, locations, moral echoes of past choices. They are the mechanism through which the revelation arrives. They come as sensation, image, inexplicable certainty. They cannot be forced. They are earned.

**The Wrongness** is the Warren disturbance made tactile — the world's magic feeling broken in ways that correlate with the heroes' presence and proximity to the Threshold. It is not punishment. It is consequence. At low tiers it is ambient; at high tiers it becomes unmistakable.

None of these explain the campaign to the players. They are the breadcrumbs that make the eventual recognition feel inevitable rather than announced.

## How to Deliver the Player-Facing Document

### Timing
Hand `PLAYER_PITCH_AND_PRINCIPLES.md` to players before character creation — not during, not after the pitch. Let them read in silence. Give them time to sit with the archetype descriptions before the Session Zero questions begin.

### What to Say
The document speaks for itself. If players ask about the "weight" the characters carry, or what's meant by "the world has already decided something about who they are," stay in the register of the document: *"The world here has long memories. Your character is significant to people they haven't met yet. That's all you need to know for now."*

Do not add context. Do not editorialize. The document is calibrated to communicate the right amount.

### On "Why Are We Here?"

Players will ask. Their characters will ask. The question is the campaign's central axis — and it has no answer yet. Decision 11 (cosmic conscription vs. punishment) is open. That's not an oversight. The characters' uncertainty about their own origins is structural; the ambiguity is the point until the campaign earns an answer.

Don't mistake your own uncertainty for a gap that needs filling. The world doesn't know why these particular people are here either. The Celestial Court hasn't sent a letter. NPCs with opinions are operating on superstition, pattern recognition, and the unsettling feeling that something has shifted.

When players push: *"I don't know. What does your character believe?"* is a complete answer. Let the characters carry the question. That's what the campaign is for.

### What Not to Say
- Do not hint that the characters were once more powerful
- Do not suggest that anything has been forgotten, lost, or suppressed
- Do not use "sleeping," "dormant," "awakening" (in any divine or cosmic sense), or language implying a previous cosmic state
- Do not describe the characters as diminished, weakened, or incomplete versions of something larger
- Do not explain the Surrendered-Layer Framework — it is GM architecture, not player premise

### On Archetype Choice
Players should choose their approach before building their character, not after. The archetype is the spine; mechanical class and ancestry build around it. Push back gently if a player wants to reverse this order — the question *how do you meet the world?* is more generative than *what can you do?*

### On the Doubt
Each archetype description includes a doubt. Encourage players to sit with it rather than resolving it during character creation. The campaign will do the work of testing and transforming that doubt over many sessions. Players who try to pre-resolve it in session zero deprive themselves of the campaign's best work.

## Session Zero Ending: The Recognition Climax

Session zero ends at the campfire. All heroes have converged. They are, for the first time, sitting in the same light.

**You narrate this.** The players don't ask for it. You don't wait for a natural pause. When the moment comes — when they've eaten and the fire has settled — you take it:

> *Something in the firelight is wrong. Not threatening. Not frightening. Just — familiar. You are looking at people you have never met. And somewhere below cognition, the word that surfaces is not "stranger."*
>
> *It passes. Or nearly.*

Cut to black. Session ends. Don't let anyone speak.

**Why the cut works:** The recognition is not a revelation to be discussed; it's a door the campaign has just opened. If you let players react to it now, they'll reach for explanation, certainty, character reactions that close the question rather than carry it. The cut gives the moment to them and doesn't let them spend it. The next session begins with something that cannot be unremembered and hasn't yet been named.

**What to say when they ask:** They will ask. *"What was that?"* is the correct response to that narration. Answer from inside the world: *"Your characters don't know. What does your character do with it?"* That is the question the campaign will spend the next thirty sessions answering.

- Do NOT reveal to players that they ARE the heroes — discovered through play

- Do NOT explain cosmological architecture to players directly
