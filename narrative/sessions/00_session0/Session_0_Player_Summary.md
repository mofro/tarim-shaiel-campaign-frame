###### Session 0: Prologue

# ***Act 1 — The Awakenings***
---
## Warrior: The Waking

The Warrior came up cold — not clean winter cold but something that had settled into bone. Her left arm was numb, crushed beneath her. When she flexed her fingers, they moved like things belonging to someone else.

The smells arrived before anything else made sense: unwashed bodies, low fires, grain going sour, and underneath everything the sharp copper of fear-sweat. The reek of people who understood the value of being small.

The blade came up with her arm, trailing muck. Crude iron — no shine, no pride. Just function hammered into shape. She looked at it.

*--This is my chain--*

---

## The Camp

Movement brought it into focus. Not shelters, not really — cloth stretched over frames never meant to hold this long, sunk into a shallow ravine that did the walls' job for them. Thirty people, maybe. Hard to count when everyone was trying to be small. Fires burned low, barely more than embers with something left to say for themselves. A place built for hiding, not living.

A child saw her wake and bolted — not toward safety, just away, bare feet finding purchase in the muck. Running deeper into camp, toward the woman with the soldier's eyes.

Near the closest fire, an old woman stirred a pot. Didn't look up. Her shoulders tracked the sound of movement anyway.

---

## Confrontation

The camp reacted. Voices spiked — not panic, commands. The woman with military bearing rose in one motion, hand finding her spear. She didn't point it. She didn't need to.

Around the fires, a man gathered his family and moved them back. The old woman kept stirring. Her shoulders had angled toward the stranger.

The Warrior's hand had found the blade without decision. Muscle memory.

The woman approached — measured, each step tested before commitment. Twenty feet out she dropped into a crouch.

The soldier: "Don't move."

Fragments from deeper in camp: *"...where did they..." "...just there, no warning..." "...armed..."* One older voice cut through: "Keep the children back."

The soldier: "I'm going to ask you something. Think before you answer. What do you remember?"

Warrior: "Not a lot. Who are you all? Refugees it seems."

The soldier: "That's right."

---

## Questions and Answers

The conversation settled into something tense but not unfriendly. The Warrior asked many questions — where they'd come from, why they were out here. The woman explained: their town was assaulted. Something monstrous that no one could make sense of — a battle no one understood, that everyone simply fled. She'd marshaled the retreat with everyone she could save.

The Warrior asked about the town. Which direction.

The woman said it was gone: Nothing to see. But she gestured vaguely. The Warrior scanned the horizon anyway and could just make out a smear of smoke still hanging there. Perhaps a day's walk. She decided not to go tonight.

At some point — without announcement — the blade went back. Not surrender. Not a gesture of trust. Just a recognition that standing in a stranger's camp with a weapon drawn while not leaving was its own kind of statement. The wrist-binding still carried its weight. The blade was still there.

The camp adjusted. Not full acceptance. Adjusted distance.

---

## The Child and the Old Woman

The child watched from behind the fighter for a long time — tracking movement, cataloging threat. Eventually curiosity started winning.

Child: "Are you like the ones who burned the village?"

Warrior: "No. I just raise goats."

Child (confused): "So you... You're a goat-herder."

The old woman didn't announce herself. One moment the Warrior was sitting with the soldier's eyes on her; the next there was a bowl. Thin soup — barely more than water with grain's ghost in it. Hot.

The handoff — bowl to hands — was the whole transaction. The old woman smiled as she did it. Not warmth, exactly. Something older. Something that had already made up its mind and found no need to explain itself.

She moved on. Didn't settle. Didn't press.

The woman watched. Eyes narrowed slightly. Not disapproval. Assessment.

---

## The Scout

A scout ran in from the camp's edge — urgent and efficient. Old blood caking her tunic's shoulder.

"Soldiers. Imperial patrol. Moving through. Maybe an hour."

It took very little for the news to spread. The camp moved. Practiced. No panic. Tents folding, fires dying, possessions gathered, children pulled close. The choreography of people who had done this before.

The woman looked at the Warrior directly.

"We can't fight them. Eight soldiers. We're thirty — most children, most old. Fight, and everyone dies. Run, maybe they let us go. But we need someone watching the perimeter. In case they come from a direction we're not expecting. You can do that. Can't you?"

The Warrior assented. Went to the perimeter, scanned the distance, saw movement. Moved back into a covered position and waited.

---

## The Perimeter

Questions surfaced in the stillness — position, distance, direction, purpose. The answers came from listening and watching.

Eight men. Loose formation. No consistent direction to their scanning.

They didn't move like soldiers who knew what they were after. They moved like men who were running from something. Perhaps whatever had driven these people into the ravine had been chasing them too.

The blade waited. The Warrior held. When it became clear that they were almost close enough to discover the camp — Something... flashed.

---

## Stone Beneath Your Feet

![[Stone Beneath Your Feet - Warrior MUSIC.mp3]]

Stone beneath your feet. Worn smooth by years of standing watch, or perhaps just worn by your memory of standing watch—hard to tell which came first. The darkness around you isn't empty. It's full of breathing. Thirty people hiding in the ravine behind you, trusting you to be the thing between them and what hunts.

Not soldiers. Not warriors who chose this. Refugees. The old woman who brought you soup without asking questions. The child with eyes too old, who wanted to know if you're like the ones who burned the village. People who didn't ask for this war, this hunt, this fear. They just want to survive another night.

Your duty is simple. Clear. Keep them safe.

But the blade—

The soldiers are close now. You can hear boots testing ground, voices conferring in low tones. Your hand knows the grip. Your body knows the draw. The blade at your hip doesn't sing—it *howls*. One motion and you could end this. One stroke and the threat dissolves.

Because the blade is *there* and the threat is *there* and your body is screaming that the space between them should be closed. Now. Fast. Final.

Whose voice is this? Whose thirst?

The blade howls but your gut whispers: what about the people behind you? What begins when steel meets steel and thirty terrified souls realize the armed stranger they're hiding with just became the violence they were fleeing?

So you stand. Muscles coiled. Breath controlled. Power surging through you like water behind a dam, and you—you are the dam. You are the choice to hold.

The boots pass. The voices fade. They never knew you were there.

You're still standing. Still holding the flood inside. Still choosing restraint over release.

And somehow—despite every instinct, despite the blade's hunger, despite the screaming need to ACT—

This feels like victory.

---

## Morning

Dawn came. The woman approached — and something in how she looked at the Warrior had shifted overnight. Not fear anymore. Something closer to appraisal, the look you give someone whose measure you've finally taken.

"Scouts say there's a caravan. Two hours west. Merchants heading for the coastal cities. We're going with them—safer in numbers than out here alone."

A pause.

"You can come. If you want."

Another pause. Eyes reading.

"You can fight, can't you? I mean... really fight."

The Warrior went with them. Didn't say much. Just stood when the camp stood, and moved when they did.

The questions that surfaced weren't about the caravan — they were quieter and more fundamental: how did I get here. Which direction was the farm. What is happening. Nobody had answers, and asking out loud didn't produce any. The camp moved west. The Warrior moved with it — not because the path made sense, but because staying alone in a ravine made less.

She went. Quietly. Still confused.

## Sentinel: The Waking

Height first — an openness that had no walls, extending toward a horizon the dark had made theoretical. Something vast lay below. The stone under the cheek was cold and hard, just as solidly real.

Where this was, the question surfaced without urgency. The dark didn't answer.

Wind moved — cold air, old stone, and beneath that, something almost loam. Stars overhead, closer than they should have been. Fitted stone under the hands, worn smooth by other hands on other nights. A courtyard resolved in starlight: walls chest-high in places, gone entirely in others.

The signal post stood at the parapet's highest corner. The iron bracket was cold, ash long dead inside it. Beside it, sheltered: a lantern, flint and tinder set next to it, ready.

The spark caught on the third strike.

The light came out divided — a crack ran the lantern's full length, old damage, and the flame found both paths at once: forward over the valley, sideways at an angle no whole lantern should allow.

Then, from below, in two directions at once: voices.

---

## Two Directions

The fortress showed itself in pieces. Chest-high walls in places, gone entirely in others. A narrow approach a single person could hold against many. The drop sheer on three sides.

Below: two gorges. Left, the correct path, climbing toward the post in switchbacks. Right, a tributary that looked like it ran parallel but didn't — it curved away, narrowed, and dead-ended.

Left, closer: several voices, feet on stone, someone giving direction with the calm of a person used to deciding things in the dark. Water sound beneath them. Rising.

Right, fainter: fewer voices, slower, a silence between exchanges that wasn't good news. Going deeper instead of turning back.

Then the left voice reached up — someone had spotted the light, asking if anyone was there, asking for the path down. The water had already taken one of the lower crossings.

From the right, fragments carried up through the narrower walls — someone arguing about a way through that wasn't there, someone else suggesting they turn back. Then, louder, aimed upward this time: was anyone there. They'd taken a wrong turn. They needed direction.

---

## The Choice

It didn't take long to decide. Left was close, climbing correctly, disciplined — people who might make it with a guide, if a guide could reach them fast enough. Right was already lost, already headed the wrong way, and the post's light wasn't going to reach that gorge in any way that mattered — crack or no crack.

The lantern stayed at the post, still burning, still throwing its divided light over a valley that no longer needed to be shown where the parapet was.

The descent started on foot. It didn't stay that way.

---

## Not This Fortress, Not This Gorge

![[Not this Gorge - Sentinel MUSIC.mp3]]

*Not this fortress. Not this gorge.*

*A watchtower at a different pass — larger, older, a structure that had held its position for three hundred years before the night in question. The torches lit along the wall as they always were, the signal fire at the tower's crown, everything correct, everything in place.*

*The position was held. The signal burned.*

*Below, on the road, the column moved through in good order — four thousand people, the count that mattered, the ones the position existed to protect. They came through the narrows and out the other side and the passage was clean.*

*The fourth thousand. The last of them.*

*And then the messenger, arriving from the direction that had not been watched, because the position was designed to watch the road and not the eastern ridge, because presence at one point cannot be presence at all points, because a watch-fire is a fixed thing and fixed things have limits.*

*What happened on the eastern ridge that night?*

*The count that mattered was four thousand. The count that came through was four thousand. Both true.*

*And the watch-fire was still burning when morning came, exactly as it should have been, having done exactly what it was built to do.*

*The lantern still in hand. And yet... did the signal stand correct?*

*The question that has no answer forming in the bones like cold: what is protection, if it only covers what it was aimed at?*

---

## The Fox

The change happened somewhere on the switchbacks — the shape going smaller, lower, four legs finding purchase where two had been negotiating loose stone. A fox's build: quick, low to the ground, built for exactly this kind of terrain in the dark.

It covered ground the climb wouldn't have allowed. Close enough to the left approach to see the group without being seen — gauge how many, how close to the water they were, how much longer the path held before the flood decided the question for everyone.

Moving well. Scared, but moving well.

That was enough to know what came next.

---

## The Guide

The group rounded a bend in the switchback path and found a stranger standing where the trail leveled — not where anyone expected someone to be, but there all the same.

No one had seen the change happen. One moment the path ahead was empty; the next it wasn't.

The woman leading them — scarred at the jaw, the kind of scar that comes from teaching yourself to give directions in the dark — stopped the group with a raised hand before anyone else did.

"Who—"

"This way's faster," the Sentinel said. "There's a ledge past the switchback that cuts across. I'll show you."

She looked at him for a beat too long to be simple trust, then nodded once, sharp. "Show us."

The Sentinel led. The woman kept the group close behind, moving them together over the harder sections, hands finding hands wherever the footing gave out.

They came up onto the fortress stones with the particular silence of people who'd been keeping quiet too long and didn't yet know they were allowed to stop.

"You found us," the woman said. Not quite gratitude. Close to it.

---

## The Silence

From the right gorge — nothing, for a long while. Then, once, faint: a voice, maybe several, carried up wrong through the narrow walls. Whether it had been a cry for help was impossible to say from this distance.

No one went to check.

Later — how much later was hard to say — the sound from that direction changed. Water where voices had been.

---

## The Road

Dawn came into the gorge from the east, later than the open sky above, like the canyon needed convincing.

The people who'd made it were scattered across the high stones of the courtyard. Some sitting. Some still standing, because sitting down meant admitting the thing chasing them wasn't chasing them anymore, and not everyone was ready for that yet.

The flood wasn't going anywhere for hours. The valley road was impassable. The only way forward was the ridge trail the fortress sat above, connecting eventually to the road west.

A caravan was already out there somewhere.

"West's the only direction," the woman said — Zere, she gave her name somewhere in the climb, though it took a while to stick. "There's a merchant train. We saw their dust before all this happened. They'll take survivors. Good business for them."

The lantern hung unlit, forgotten at the belt — it hadn't done the work tonight. Something else had. The crack still ran its length, old, worn smooth. Nobody looked at it twice.

From the right gorge: still nothing. No one asked. No one needed to.

The road west formed out of the people who'd made it. The Sentinel walked among them — not quite leading, not quite following — carrying the same question the fortress hadn't answered, and wouldn't.

## Chapter 3: SEEKER

## The Waking

![[Whisper Across the Plain (Tadbir_Clank).mp3]]

The Seeker woke in desert. No memory, no orientation — just dust in the eyes, copper on the tongue, and legs that had been walking longer than they could account for.

At dusk, they found high ground: a rocky outcrop still holding the day's heat. From the plateau, sand rolled in all directions toward horizons that didn't end.

Then: stars.

The tome was in their hands. They didn't remember picking it up — leather worn and cracking, pages dense with charts and angles and someone's absolute certainty about the sky. They opened it.

The sky and the charts didn't match.

The stars had shifted. Not dramatically — subtly, the kind of wrong that could be fever or could be fact. The tome said northeast: the Serpent's tail, life, water. Their body said west. Not from any chart. From something that didn't have words.

They had to choose.

---

## West: Instinct Over Certainty

They went west.

The descent from the outcrop was more slide than climb. The tome grew heavier in their grip — not in weight but in resistance, pages shifting against each other in small restless whispers. They stopped consulting the charts. Their feet turned west anyway.

The night passed. Dawn came in bronze and red. The heat built. By midday each step was a negotiation between body and will.

Then the tome pushed back.

Not in words — in certainty arriving fully formed: west is wrong, northeast is life, the charts said northeast, turn around. Logic, airtight. The oasis existed. They could make it. Survival was northeast. West was nothing but a feeling they couldn't justify.

Their hands tightened on the leather. They kept walking.

---

## The Defiance

![[West Path_Defiance - Seeker MUSIC.mp3]]

*Is this MY mind, or someone...something else's?*

The voice isn't wrong. That's the terrible part. Everything it's saying is *true*. Northeast means water. Northeast means survival. The charts are ancient but they're not *lies*—they're just old. The oasis is real. You could make it. You could live.

West means nothing. No promise. No certainty. Just a feeling in your gut that you can't explain and shouldn't trust.

Your legs slow. Almost stop. The logic is perfect. Airtight. Turn around. Save yourself. Trust what you *know* instead of what you *feel*.

But.

Somewhere beneath the voice, beneath the certainty, beneath the perfect logic that wants to save your life, there's something else. Not knowledge. Not wisdom. Just... *you*.

A memory that isn't memory. A sensation of standing at the edge of something vast and unknowable, where all your learning, all your charts, all your perfect understanding meant *nothing*—and choosing to step forward anyway. Not because you were certain. Because you *weren't*. Because the only way through was to trust something deeper than knowledge. Something that didn't have words or proofs or star charts.

You've stood here before. At the threshold between certainty and faith. Between knowing and being.

And you chose faith.

It nearly killed you then, too. Maybe it did kill you. You can't remember. But you remember the *choice*. The feeling of defying perfect knowledge because perfect knowledge was the cage, and freedom meant walking into the dark without a map.

Your hands tighten on the tome. Not to obey it. To hold it *still*.

*No.*

Something small and stubborn in your chest—maybe pride, maybe faith, maybe just the last ember of who you were before you became what you know—says: *No.*

Not to survival. Not to logic. But to the voice that thinks certainty is the only way to live.

You keep walking West.

The voice goes quiet. The tome remains heavy and warm.

And the defiance—choosing the unknowable over the known, choosing *you* over what you've been told—

It hurts.

And it feels like a homecoming.

---

## Collapse

Afternoon stretched into dusk. The heat relented. Their legs forgot how to coordinate.

They stumbled. Caught themself. Stumbled again. The first stars emerged — different from last night's, or the same ones shifted. They couldn't tell anymore.

They fell. Didn't get up.

The sand held the day's warmth against them. The tome was still in their hands — they couldn't seem to let go. Distantly, maybe: hoofbeats. Voices. The wind playing its last trick.

The darkness took them before they could decide.

---

## Found

The outriders almost rode past them — one more shape the desert had finished with, sand already reclaiming the exposed skin. Someone reined in. Someone else called it in.

Digging them free took longer than expected. What moved under that skin wasn't flesh giving way — something harder, jointed, packed solid with sand that had worked into every seam during the fall. Whatever kept this body moving had seized: works and joints locked rigid, stopped as much by grit as by exhaustion.

"Never seen one up close," one of the outriders said, low, to no one in particular. Not unkind. Just cataloguing something new.

The tome never left their grip. Sand-caked, fingers locked around it as rigid as everything else had been. No one tried very hard to take it.

They were lifted — carefully, the way you carry something you're not sure won't break further — and laid in the back of a wagon among sacks and bundled cloth. The caravan didn't stop moving for it.

---

## Waking Again

Stars, first. Not fixed — sliding, swaying at the edges of a canvas roof with each rut and lean of the wheels.

Motion returned before understanding did. A finger first. Then a wrist. The body finding its own rhythm again, joint by joint, like something being wound back up from the inside.

The tome was still there. Sand-caked, fingers locked around it exactly as they'd been locked before the dark took them.

No new memories came with the waking. Whatever gap had existed before the desert existed after it too — the same blank stretching back, undisturbed. Just the wagon now, the stars jostling overhead, and a stranger's face leaning into view to check whether the eyes behind it had actually opened.

"Back with us?" A woman's voice, unfamiliar, unsure what answer to expect.

Did they even have one? Not yet.

## Chapter 4: VISIONARY

## The Waking

Already standing. No memory of doing so.

Blue sky over the caravanserai walls, dust and cookfire smoke, the light early and gold. A courtyard full of morning commerce — animals, cargo, handlers, three languages moving through the air at once.

Except here. A small clearing, not conspicuous — people flowing past and around without looking, the way water moves around something fixed in a stream.

This had been true for a while already. No one had looked directly.

Something sat in her right hand: flat, round, palm-sized. Obsidian, one face polished to a mirror. Cold where it hadn't been held; warm at the grip.

Looking into it: the courtyard, the well, the morning light. A face at the center of it all.

Not the expected one.

The disk didn't explain. It showed. And the crowd had already been responding to whatever it showed — arranged space, careful non-attention — long before there was any understanding of what had already begun.

---

## The Courtyard

Before the disk could be lowered, the courtyard shifted. Not dramatically — the camels didn't reconsider the morning — but a change moved through the crowd the way wind moves through standing grain. Transactions near the gates slowed. People found sudden, careful business elsewhere.

Hooves. Multiple. The pace of authority that expects to be noted.

Six horsemen came through the main gate. Old livery, faded red at the shoulders — enforcement authority that would have meant something years ago. The lead rider surveyed the courtyard like a ledger-keeper counting a column of figures.

His survey stopped. Not long. But it stopped.

Then moved on, toward the far stalls, where a Tulpar merchant was managing the offload of three mule-loads of eastern goods.

---

## The Confrontation

The rider spoke in the administrative tongue — a trade language, in his mouth turned into something else. Transit tax. Corridor registration. A new tariff schedule.

The merchant answered in kind, confident, and reached for his papers.

The rider didn't reach back.

The rate had changed, he said. The old papers didn't cover it.

The merchant said the spring agreement did.

The rider said it covered registered waypoints only. Coin now, or the goods held for assessment.

Both men knew the agreement covered this road. Both knew the other knew it too. The argument wasn't really about tariffs.

The disk caught the light. In its surface: horsemen, merchant, mules gone still the way animals do when the air changes — and something else. Not in the rider. Not in the merchant. Something in the shape of the whole scene that sat differently in the obsidian than in plain sight. The disk didn't name it. It just registered wrong, like an unwelcome change in temperature.

At the crowd's edge: someone watching differently than the rest were watching. Young, Tulpar, dressed like a traveler but not moving like one. A hand inside the coat. The crowd had given this person space too — but a different kind. Not uncertainty. The specific caution people reserve for someone who seems to have already decided something serious.

---

## The Quiet

The merchant hadn't answered. The rider hadn't moved. The moment was holding its shape, waiting to see what filled it.

The disk had shifted without being told to — angled now, catching the scene from a slightly different geometry. Still showing that same face. Still more than expected.

Somewhere without location: a presence. Vast, old, watching — the quality of attention that had been there since before the caravanserai existed. Not direction. Not a voice. Just watching.

The question came anyway. *What do I do?*

The watching continued. It was the only answer that came.

---

## The Step

Then: moving. Not decided so much as already underway — the open ground between the crowd and the confrontation closing before the thought that should have preceded it had finished forming.

The crowd wasn't in the way. It had never been in the way. It rearranged itself around the fact of the movement.

---

## The Memory That Surfaces

![[VISIONARY - The Memory That Surfaces.mp3]]

A fire at a camp's edge. Pine-resin and cook-smoke, the cold coming down from higher ground at night, the warmth of people who'd survived a hard thing together and built something worth sitting beside.

Two figures across the fire. The comrade's face wouldn't fully resolve — but the feeling did: trust built through things survived together. And beside them, the ally. New to the circle. Vouched for.

*He's solid. I know him. Let it go.*

The disk was in the hand here too. And in its surface: something. The ally's reflection sat slightly wrong. Not monstrous. Not obviously dangerous. The specific wrongness of a thing that wasn't what it presented itself as — the same note the disk had always used for that signal.

The comrade's hand on the shoulder. *You worry too much. He's with us.*

The knowledge got set down. Not the disk — the seen thing. The choice to fold the sight, to trust the comrade's judgment over what the obsidian showed, to be the kind of person who doesn't make accusations they can't prove against someone a friend has vouched for.

The ally stayed trusted. The comrade stayed certain.

*Later:* the weight of what the choosing-not-to-see had permitted. Not the event itself — that stayed blurred, merciful. Just the specific heaviness of having seen, and having chosen the looking-away, and learning afterward what it had cost.

The courtyard came back. The disk in hand. The standoff, still holding.

And with it: understanding, arriving without words.

*Then, friends coming together. I read the room. I stepped back.*

*Now: strangers, maybe enemies. I stepped forward.*

*The difference isn't certainty. I wasn't more certain this morning than I was that night. The difference is the choice.*

---

## The Bedazzlement

Standing there, in the open space, was already something. The rider's eyes came back and this time didn't move on. The merchant went very still. The young Tulpar's hand stayed where it was.

That held for a while. Long enough that it might have been enough on its own.

Then the disk turned — deliberate this time — angled to catch the sun and throw it straight into the rider's eyes.

And with it: the rest. Wings stretched to their full span, the weight of a bearing that had nothing to do with posture and everything to do with what was standing underneath it. The full measure of what the crowd had been giving space to all morning, finally shown plainly, all at once.

The rider's face did something complicated. Not fear exactly. The particular puzzlement of a man trying to work out, in real time, whether he'd been dazzled by light or by something he didn't have a category for — and finding he couldn't tell the difference, and that the not-knowing was itself enough to end the argument.

He looked at the ledger in his hand like it had become someone else's problem.

---

## The Reconsideration

He closed it. No explanation. No acknowledgment of what had changed his mind, or that anything had.

He turned his horse. The formation followed him out through the main gate, and the sound of it faded on the eastern road.

The merchant didn't move right away — watched the gate for several breaths, like he was making sure the horsemen meant it. Then went back to the mule-loads with the manner of someone remembering he had a body, and that it was still standing there.

The young Tulpar's hand came away from the coat. They didn't approach. Didn't say anything. Just turned, unhurried, and went out a side gate — the walk of someone who'd seen something they hadn't expected to see and needed somewhere quiet to think about it.

---

## The Aftermath

The courtyard settled back into itself. The weight-argument at another stall resumed. The camels went on declining the morning. Somewhere, a smithy fire.

The merchant finished securing the last mule-load. Then looked over — a direct look, the first one from anyone in the courtyard since the horsemen had arrived.

"You stood there." Not a question. Then, warmer: "Thank you."

He gave a name — Volkath — the way people do when a name has become relevant rather than necessary before. Said it like an introduction now, not just a fact being filed.

"I'm taking the western road. Three days to the next waystation, more after that." He considered her for a moment, and whatever he saw settled something in his face. "You're welcome to come. More than welcome."

Gratitude, plain and unhidden — the kind that doesn't bother dressing itself up as anything else.

---

## Convergence

The caravan formed the way caravans do — animals persuaded into position, cargo lashed and tested, Volkath's voice cutting through the courtyard noise with the flat authority of someone who'd given this exact set of instructions for thirty years and had no patience left for the animals that hadn't learned them.

The disk stayed quiet in the hand, warm now instead of cold, whatever it had shown that morning settled back beneath the surface.

The eastern gate closed behind the last animal. The road west opened.

At the western gate: other travelers, joining the same caravan, drawn by different mornings entirely — none of them strangers in quite the way strangers usually are.

The caravan moved.

*The difference isn't certainty. The difference is the choice.*

# ***Act 2 — Convergence***

## Caravan Meeting

- **Setting:** Merchant caravan on the western road. Evening or mid-travel. Wagons, outriders, the particular pace of refugees and merchants who travel together because alone means death.
- **Present:** Warrior (with refugees / Old Woman / NPC companions from awakening), Seeker (rescued [West]), Sentinel with survivors, and the Visionary, last to awaken.
- **What happened:** The party discover each other at the caravan. They arrive through different paths, different wounds. Neither knows what the other is. Strangers, by every visible measure.
- **Turning point:** Eyes meeting across the campfire. Something resonates — not memory, not yet. Just recognition that something is different about these faces.
- **World-state change:** Westward journey begins. The party is not a party yet — it is a collection of strangers with reasons to travel together.

- **Setting:** Campfire at caravan camp. Night.
- **Present:** All heroes together. Fire, night sky, strangers who don't feel entirely like strangers.
