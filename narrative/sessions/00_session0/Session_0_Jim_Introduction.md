# Session 0: Jim's Introduction — The Fork

---

## Setup Notes (GM Only)

**What you know that Jim doesn't:**
Jim's internal sense of direction has never failed him. It is not a tool he carries — it is how he is built, Rahban attunement in the body, as anatomical as balance. Today, for the first time, it is giving him two signals simultaneously. One is his own. The other has no mechanical explanation. It isn't broken. It's bilateral.

The Jade Illusionist is at the fork. They arrived before Jim did. They are waiting, though they would describe it as setting up.

The Illusionist does not know what Jim is. They have noticed an anomaly and are investigating in their fashion.

Whatever Jim chooses, he will reach the caravan. One road is faster.

**Tone:** Daylight. Quiet. Slightly surreal in a way Jim can almost explain. Not the weight of the other awakenings — no crisis, no confinement, no divine pressure. A young man standing in sunlight, confused about something that has never confused him before.

---

## The Fork

He is wrong.

Not lost — he doesn't have a word for lost that applies to himself, has never needed one. This is something else. This is two norths, both of them in his chest, both of them his, neither of them agreeing.

One pull is familiar. He knows the shape of it the way he knows the shape of his own hands: the Rahban attunement, the thing that makes *you're lost* a foreign construction, an error code that doesn't apply to him. It pulls left. Left toward the waystation. Left toward the route he'd explained to Grandmother Tev before he left, stop by stop, because Tev would worry otherwise and he loves her and also it was easier.

The other pull is right. And it doesn't feel like north. It feels like something that is not north, looking at the thing in his chest that has always known where north is, and saying: *yes, but —*

He has no instrument to check. He has never needed one. He can only stand at the fork and feel himself disagreeing with himself, which is a new experience, and not a comfortable one.

He has been here long enough that a bird has landed near his left foot, determined the foot is not food, and departed without comment.

He has been here long enough.

---

## The Merchant

He notices the merchant the way you notice someone who has already noticed you.

They're positioned at the edge of the fork — not blocking either road, not quite clear of either one. A small display of practical goods, a leather case open to show route markers and wayfinding tools in an unfamiliar design. The merchant is making notes in a small book. Or appears to be. Jim watches for a moment and registers, with the mild sensation of something being slightly off, that the pen isn't moving.

The merchant has an abacus at the belt. The beads are jade — not decoratively; worn smooth at their centers from thousands of passes, the kind of wear that takes decades. The jade catches the light at an angle that doesn't match the angle of the sun.

They look up.

The quality of attention is not what Jim expects from a merchant reading a potential customer. It goes through him differently. More careful. Like someone who has walked this road a hundred times and found, today, something they haven't classified before.

Jim approaches. Of course he does.

---

## The Exchange

**[GM: Let this breathe. Jim is a Bard and a Rahban; connection is his instinct. The merchant is warm, genuinely curious, attentive in ways that feel like flattery until you realize the attention predates any reason for it. Allow small commerce — if Jim examines the route markers, the merchant notes they're from roads "further east than most people travel." If Jim asks where the merchant is from: "Oh, I get around." Friendly. Technically accurate. Doesn't quite land. This isn't a clue yet. It's texture.]**

The route markers are from roads Jim has never traveled. The notation system is unfamiliar — older, he thinks, or from a different regional tradition — and he finds himself turning one over in his hands because the structure of it is almost recognizable, the way you recognize the shape of a joke in a language you don't speak.

"Unusual system," he says.

"It's consistent," the merchant says. "Which is rarer than people assume."

Jim puts the marker down.

---

## The Question

The merchant looks at him — still with that careful attention, the kind that goes through rather than at — and asks:

**"Which pull do you usually trust?"**

Not: *where are you trying to go?* Not: *is something the matter?* This.

Jim finds himself explaining. Both directions feel right to him, which is the problem, because both directions are him — that's how it works, that's how it has always worked. One he recognizes. One he can't account for. He's been standing here trying to determine which is the reliable one, except he has no way to check that doesn't involve the thing that's already disagreeing with itself.

The merchant listens the way very few people listen: completely.

"And the one you can't account for," they say. "What does it feel like?"

Jim considers this longer than the question seems to deserve.

"Like someone else's sentence finishing in my head."

The merchant writes something in the small book. This time, the pen moves.

"Have you considered," they say, still writing, "that both might be yours?"

Jim waits for the rest of the sentence.

There isn't one.

**[GM: The player may push back, may ask what that means, may be frustrated. The merchant is unfazed and gently interested in the frustration. They will not elaborate directly. If pressed: "I'm just noting it as a possibility. I could be wrong." They are not wrong. They are not going to explain why.]**

---

## The Truth

Jim asks — because asking is what he does, what he's for — what's down the road he can't account for.

The merchant considers whether to answer. This consideration is visible. Genuine.

"People," they say. "A group of them. They know exactly where they are." A small pause. "They don't know *who* they are."

Jim waits for clarification.

"That's all of it," the merchant says. "I've found that distinction tends to matter — to someone with your —" a gesture, vague, that seems to indicate all of Jim without specifying what about all of Jim is being referenced "— habits."

**[GM: "Someone with your habits" is deliberately unspecified. The merchant is noticing an anomaly and gesturing at it without naming it — because they don't have a name for it yet. Jim doesn't need to catch this. If the player asks what the merchant means: "You ask questions. That's the habit I meant." Leave it there.]**

---

## The Choice

**[GM: This is Jim's decision. Both roads are genuinely available. Do not steer it.]**

Jim looks left. Waystation. The route he explained to Grandmother Tev.

He looks right. People who know where they are but not who they are.

He picks up one of the route markers — the almost-familiar notation, the structure he can almost read — and sets it back down.

He makes his choice.

---

### Path A — The Unknown Road (right)

He sets off before he's entirely decided to, which is characteristic.

Behind him, not loudly, not quite directed at him — more a note made aloud:

**"The stories that sound familiar usually are."**

**[GM: Jim may keep walking. He probably will. But he may stop.]**

**If Jim keeps walking:** The words settle like road dust — present, then gone, then somehow still there. He doesn't look back. The disagreement in his chest resolves, not into one signal, but into something quieter: two pulls that have stopped arguing. He walks toward people who don't know who they are, because that is a more interesting destination than a waystation he already has mapped.

---

**If Jim stops and turns back:**

The merchant is still at their display, writing in the book. They look up with an expression of mild, genuine surprise.

> *"Hm? Oh — don't mind me. Old road habit. We talk to ourselves when traffic's light."*

**If Jim presses:**

> *"I was making a note. I misremember things if I don't write them down."*

**If Jim presses further:**

The merchant looks at him — the careful look, the one that goes through — and then:

> *"The weather. It's going to turn."*

**[It may or may not turn. Either way, the conversation is concluded. The merchant will not elaborate further. If Jim asks their name: they give one. It's a name. It doesn't catch on anything. Eventually Jim will go. The merchant will watch him go. Whichever road he takes.]**

---

### Path B — The Comfortable Road (left)

**[GM: This is a valid choice. Don't signal that it's wrong.]**

The merchant says, behind him, not quite to him:

**"The stories that sound familiar usually are."**

**[Use the redirect beats above if Jim turns back. If he keeps going:]**

He reaches the waystation by afternoon. It's exactly what a waystation is: functional, staffed by a family who recognizes his people's name, with the particular tidiness Rahban waystations have and which Jim has always found quietly reassuring.

He does his business. It's fine.

Sometime during the evening — the second cup of tea, or while he's copying a route note — he becomes aware that the pull in his chest is pointing somewhere that is not home, not here, not the waystation. It's pointing the way he didn't go.

It isn't urgent about it. It's patient. It has decided to wait.

He leaves that evening, or in the morning. The road he didn't take. The family doesn't find this strange — Rahban change their routes; that's the nature of routes. They pack him something for the road.

He finds the caravan later than he would have. He arrives into something already in motion: people who seem to know each other in a way that doesn't quite account for how long they've been traveling together.

He sits down at the fire because the fire is warm and he is Rahban and those are sufficient reasons.

**[The merchant's parting words — *the stories that sound familiar usually are* — will come back to him later, at a moment when they land differently. That moment is yours to place.]**

---

## What the Illusionist Takes With Them

They came to the fork because something interesting was there. They leave knowing: this one isn't in any account they've been keeping. Doesn't fit the heroes' frame. Doesn't fit any frame they have.

They are going to keep watching. Not to guide — to see what it does.

**[GM: The Jade Illusionist will not appear to Jim again until the campaign gives you a structurally perfect moment for a revelation. When they do appear, it will be in a different form. Jim will not recognize them. He will recognize, afterward, that he should have.]**
