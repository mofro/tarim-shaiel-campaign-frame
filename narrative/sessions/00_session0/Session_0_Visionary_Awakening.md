# Session 0: Visionary Archetype Awakening

## Overview

**Archetype:** Visionary
**Class:** Seraph (Splendor & Valor)
**Core Doubt:** "What does it cost to see clearly — and what does it cost not to?"
**Tool:** Obsidian disk (polished, palm-sized, carried without memory of acquiring it)
**Divine patron:** Sky-Father (watching, not directing)
**Setting:** A caravanserai on the western road, morning commerce underway
**Crisis:** An imperial payment dispute with a volatile edge — a crowd that is looking away, a confrontation that could resolve or rupture, and a Seraph standing in the middle of it trying to understand what they are
**Duration:** 30-45 minutes total play time (variable by path)
**Tone:** Disorientation through presence → recognition through reflection → crisis of action → identity through choice

---

## Campaign Context

This awakening serves the larger Session 0 architecture:
- **Individual crisis** specific to the Visionary archetype (sight that cannot be turned off, tested by a moment where acting on it has real cost)
- **Secret snippet moment** integrated into the crisis (the moment of commitment triggers mythic memory)
- **Convergence path** to caravan (the Seraph joins the westward road as a result of what they do or don't do)
- **Established wound** — the Visionary arrives carrying the question of whether clear sight obligates action, and whether they are capable of the answer it requires

The Visionary enters the convergence scene as *the one who stepped forward when everyone else looked away.* They arrive without a history anyone can see, having earned their place on the road through something that looked like presence and was actually more than that — though they don't have words for it yet.

---

## Setup Notes (For GM)

**What the GM knows that the player doesn't:**

- The Seraph did not arrive at the caravanserai by ordinary travel — they simply were there, already standing, when awareness returned
- The disk has been in their hand since before they were conscious of having a hand to hold it
- The lead rider's formation is operating under imperial authority that has not been formally revoked but no longer applies to this road under the Merchant Council spring agreements — both the rider and the merchant know this; the rider does not care
- The orc merchant's papers are legitimate and in order; the payment being demanded is a legal technicality being weaponized as leverage
- The young orc in the crowd is a Chain-Breakers Order sympathizer — not an active operative, but someone with family history in the liberation and very specific readiness for situations that look like this one
- External rescuers are not coming; this situation will resolve through the people present or not resolve cleanly

**Contested-site context:**
The caravanserai sits at a crossroads where imperial remnant authority overlaps with the new trade networks the orc merchant confederations have built since the liberation. The Wizard's agents have no specific presence here — but the structural tension being exploited is exactly the kind of fault line they seed and watch. If a divine figure visibly intervened in a volatile imperial-orc dispute in a public trade space, that is the kind of event that would be reported.

**Passive hook:** The lead rider paused when he surveyed the courtyard. He noted the Seraph. He will report what he saw to whoever he reports to. What reaches that report and what is made of it is a thread the GM holds for later.

**Dual-truth preservation:**
- Storyteller knows: The Seraph was present at this moment because something needed witnessing that the crowd was collectively declining to witness
- Player knows: They woke in a caravanserai with a disk in their hand, people giving them space, and a confrontation developing that no one else was going to do anything about

**R/H/K — The obsidian disk as Warren ambassador:**

The disk is not loyal to the Seraph. Its behavior reflects Warren interests. The GM reads the disk through this frame; the player only ever feels it.

- **Hunger:** The disk wants to illuminate. It is a showing-tool — its function is to make visible what is actually present rather than what is presented. Holding it while doing nothing is Hunger unfulfilled. Throughout the confrontation, the disk angles slightly toward the scene without being directed, catches light it wasn't pointed at, finds the reflection of things the Seraph hasn't consciously looked at yet. The Warren is calling for its function to be used. The Seraph's presence, when they step forward, is the Hunger being answered — they become the mechanism of illumination the disk was reaching toward.

- **Resist:** When the Seraph considers looking away — stepping back into the crowd's arranged avoidance, deciding this is not their problem — the disk doesn't cooperate. The grip develops a wrongness when pointed anywhere but the confrontation. Not heavier. Just: wrong direction. The Warren protecting its investment: this moment, this witness, this choice. The Resist signal does not command. It makes looking away uncomfortable in a way that has no explanation.

- **Know:** The snippet at the moment of commitment. The Warren delivers what it has been holding: *you have seen this before. You have been this before. The difference between then and now is not the sight — you have always had the sight. The difference is what you did with it.* This arrives regardless of which path the Seraph takes. It was waiting for the moment of commitment itself.

## Segment 1: The Waking (3-5 minutes)

### Opening Narration

**[No transition. Already here. Already standing.]**

Already standing. No memory of becoming so.

Blue sky above the caravanserai walls. Dust and cookfire smoke, the light early and gold. A full courtyard at morning — animals, cargo, handlers, the press and noise of commerce in three languages the mouth knows without the mind having caught up to knowing them.

Except here. Around this figure: a small clearing. Not conspicuous. People flowing past and around and away without looking — the way water moves around something fixed in a stream, not by decision but by what is simply there.

This has been true for some time. No one has looked directly.

Something is in the right hand: flat, round, the diameter of the palm. Obsidian, one face polished to a mirror finish. Cold where it has not been held; warm at the grip.

Looking into it: the courtyard, the well, the morning light. Their own face at the center of all of it.

Not the face they expected.

The disk does not explain. It shows. The crowd has already been responding to what it shows — the arranged space, the careful non-attention — and the person at the center of the reflection is the last to understand what has already begun.

### GM Notes

- The crowd is the environment. Don't let it become backdrop — it's what makes this opening distinct from every other awakening. The Seraph woke surrounded. Something was already happening.
- The arranged space is the first fact the GM delivers: not "you see a crowd" but "the crowd has given you space, and has been giving it for some time." The crowd didn't react to them arriving. They reacted to them being there.
- The disk's reflection: do not describe what the Seraph sees beyond *not the face they expected*. Let the player name it, or not. The disk is the first mirror; what it shows doesn't have a word yet.
- Prompt: *"What does your character do?"*

## Segment 2: The Courtyard (3-5 minutes)

### Understanding the Space

**[The setting resolves. The Seraph orients. And then the quality of the morning changes.]**

Before the disk can be lowered, the courtyard shifts.

Not dramatically. Commerce continues. The camels have not reconsidered the morning. But a change moves through the crowd the way a change in wind moves through standing grain: the transactions nearest the main gates slow, partners requiring sudden careful attention, the studied busyness of people who need to not be witnesses to whatever is about to enter.

Hooves. Multiple. The tempo of authority announcing itself before it arrives — not galloping, not leisurely. The pace that expects to be noted and expected.

The main gates admit six horsemen.

Old livery: faded red at the shoulders, a device on the chest that would have meant enforcement authority eleven years ago. Chainmail, functional rather than ceremonial. The horses are Fergana stock, well-bred, ridden by competent men who know they are competent and expect you to know it too.

The lead rider surveys the courtyard the way a ledger-keeper surveys a column of figures. Merchants. Cargo. Strangers. Anything worth noting.

His survey stops at the figure with the disk. Not long. But it stops.

Then continues, with the controlled neutrality of a man who has noted something and will return to it when he is ready.

The formation moves toward the far stalls where an orc merchant has been managing the offload of three mule-loads of eastern goods — silk in wax-sealed parcels, dye-blocks stamped with a Khotan mark, tools wrapped in oiled leather. The merchant is built like thirty years of cargo work, and he watches the approaching horsemen with the expression of someone calculating distances and finding the numbers unsatisfactory.

### GM Notes

- The lead rider's pause on the Seraph is real. Note it once, let it pass, don't explain. The player should feel seen without knowing why
- The merchant: not afraid. Calculating. These are different things and the difference matters for what comes next
- The crowd's collective quiet is immediate and experienced — everyone here knows what it costs to be noticed by men in old livery
- Player Agency: prompt *"What do you do?"*

## Segment 3: The Confrontation (5-8 minutes)

### The Dispute

**[The merchant, the horsemen, the crowd that has stopped watching. And something else at the edge of it.]**

The lead rider speaks in the imperial administrative tongue — technically a trade language; in his usage, a different instrument.

Transit tax. Corridor registration. New tariff schedule, eastern goods.

The merchant answers in the same language, accented with the confidence of someone who learned it as a working tool rather than a marker of allegiance. The papers are in order. He reaches for them.

The rider does not reach back.

*The corridor rate was renegotiated since those papers were issued. New assessment applies.*

*That rate doesn't apply here. The Merchant Council spring agreement—*

*Covers council members operating through registered waypoints.* The rider's voice holds the flatness of policy rather than argument. *Coin, now. Or the goods are held for formal assessment.*

The merchant's hands stop.

Both of them know the spring agreement covers this road. Both of them know the rider knows this too. The argument being made is not about tariff schedules.

The disk catches the morning light from where it is held. The confrontation reflects in the obsidian: horsemen, merchant, the mules that have stilled behind him as animals do when the air changes. The rider's hand, resting near his hip with practiced casualness.

And in the reflection — something is not what it claims to be. Not in the rider, not in the merchant. Something in the arrangement of the scene that sits differently in the obsidian than it sits in direct sight. The disk doesn't name it. It registers the way a temperature change registers: not identified, just felt. *Wrong, here, in some direction.*

Then, at the edge of the crowd:

Someone watching the confrontation with a quality of attention that is different from the crowd's studied avoidance. Young — teens or barely past. Orc, dressed as a traveler but not moving as travelers move. The particular stillness of someone who has already decided what they will do and is waiting to find out whether the conditions are being met.

The hand inside the coat.

The crowd around this person has given them the same unconscious space it gave the Seraph — but different. The space around the Seraph is uncertainty. The space around the young orc is the instinct of people who have learned to read the specific stillness of someone who has already chosen.

### GM Notes

- The payment dispute: the merchant's papers ARE in order; the new rate the rider cites IS technically in effect but doesn't apply to this road; both are true and both are being weaponized. The rider doesn't need a clean legal answer. The power of the interaction is that he doesn't need one.
- The disk's wrongness: felt, not analyzed. Do not name what it is responding to. It is the Warren showing the Seraph that something here is not what it presents itself as. The Seraph cannot yet identify this — only register it.
- The young orc: do not draw the player's attention directly. It should arrive as a detail in the scanning of the scene — noticed because the Seraph is the kind of person who notices what others are avoiding.
- The crowd's avoidance is the moral context: every person in that courtyard is making the same choice the Seraph will be asked to make. Most of them have already made it. The Seraph gets to see it in full before they choose.

## Segment 4: The Disk (3-4 minutes)

### The Quiet Before the Step

**[The Seraph, the disk, and the absence of the guidance that was supposed to come.]**

Three silences happening simultaneously:

The merchant has not answered. The rider has not moved. The space between the demand and the response that has not come is holding the shape of what happens next.

The young orc is still.

The Seraph, and the disk.

The disk has shifted in the hand without being directed — the angle has changed, catching the scene from a different geometry. The obsidian holds the morning, the confrontation, the crowd's practiced non-attention. Their own face at the center of it all.

Still more than expected. Still holding something the surrounding faces don't hold. The disk is not offering an explanation. It is showing the same face it showed in the first moment — the face the crowd has been responding to with arranged space and careful non-attention — and showing it with the patience of something that has been waiting a long time to show it to the person it belongs to.

From somewhere without location: a presence. Vast. The quality of attention that belongs to something that has been watching since before this caravanserai was built, from a distance that does not diminish the attention. Not direction. Not voice. Just: *watching. Noting. Present to what is about to happen.*

The prayer surfaces without decision — the reflex of a life organized around the assumption that asking would be answered:

*What do I do?*

The watching continues.

The watching is the only answer that comes.

### GM Notes

- This segment is quiet. Let it be quiet.
- The Sky-Father's presence is real and genuinely insufficient for what the Seraph needs right now. Let this register as frustrating — the absence of direction where direction was expected. The wound of this archetype begins with the recognition that divine connection will not resolve the choice.
- **The disk's Hunger:** It wants to be raised, to illuminate, to show — that is its function. The Seraph holding it at their side while doing nothing is Hunger unfulfilled. The disk has been angling toward the confrontation throughout, catching light it wasn't directed toward.
- **The disk's Resist:** When the Seraph's eyes move away from the confrontation, when the feet consider stepping back into the crowd's avoidance, the disk doesn't cooperate. The grip develops a wrongness when pointed anywhere but the scene. Not heavier. Just: wrong direction.
- Pause after narration. Ask: *"What does your character do?"*

## Segment 5: The Step (8-12 minutes, variable)

### The Standoff

**[The choice to step forward — or not. The space between the crowd and the confrontation. The moment that holds.]**

The rider's hand has moved to a writing tool. A ledger. The specific practiced motion of someone preparing to document a confiscation. His horse is still. The horse has done this before.

The young orc's hand is inside the coat.

The crowd has found pressing business elsewhere.

The disk in the hand. The face in the disk. The watching from without location.

The body moves.

Not decided — moved. The feet have already crossed some threshold before the mind has finished the sentence that would have preceded the decision. The open ground between the crowd and the confrontation is closing. The crowd is not in the way. It was never in the way. It has arranged itself, without acknowledging it, around the fact that this person was going to move.

One of the merchants near the well inhales.

The lead rider looks up from his ledger.

The young orc goes still in a different way — the stillness of someone who was waiting to see what would happen, and now something is happening, and it is not what they were waiting for.

---

When the Seraph enters the open space, something redistributes. Not magic. Not threat. Just: the weight of the moment finding a new center. The rider is looking at the Seraph the way he looked at them when he first entered the courtyard, but this time he does not look away. The merchant has gone very still.

Time extends.

Neither side moves.

The disk catches the morning light.

*[Tell the player: "The confrontation is holding. The parties are waiting. You are in the space between them, and something has paused. The moment won't hold forever — but it is holding right now. What do you do?"]*

---

### The Standoff Mechanic

*Place two dice on the table. Both showing 4. One for the horsemen. One for the young orc.*

**Counter starts at 4 (both dice).**

Each cycle: both sides speak or shift. The Seraph responds or acts.

**Hold — pure presence (no roll):** The Seraph does not engage beyond being there. The counter holds. The pause extends. Describe what each party does with a moment of being witnessed by something they don't have words for.

**Act — roll Difficulty 15:** Presence most likely. Player may argue Prayer Dice or domain ability (Splendor / Valor) for advantage before rolling.

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| Critical success | Counter −2 on target die |
| Success with Hope | Counter −1 on target die |
| Success with Fear | No change; GM gains 1 Fear |
| Failure with Fear | Counter +1 on target die |

**Counter hits 0:** That party stands down. Both at 0: the standoff resolves. The horsemen defer the payment issue — not satisfied, but the cost of pressing it has exceeded the value. The merchant exhales. The young orc's hand comes away from the coat.

**The snippet fires at the moment of the first step forward.** See Segment 6.

---

### Path A: Approaching the Young Orc with Authority (deviation)

If the Seraph moves toward the young orc with the posture of someone with power addressing someone with a problem — borrowed authority, even unintentional — the disk shifts in the grip.

Not heavier. Wrong.

The reflection shows the young orc's posture before the Seraph's mind has consciously read it: braced. The specific preparation for impact that belongs to someone who has learned authority as a word for *incoming.* The disk is showing them what they're reading as, from outside, before they can correct it.

The young orc's hand comes out of the coat. Not slowly.

The horsemen do not intervene. They watch with the particular attention of professionals assessing whether a situation has resolved itself usefully.

The scuffle is brief and not lethal — the young orc is not trying to kill anyone; they are trying to make the authority stop coming toward them. Resolve it appropriate to the situation and the Seraph's capabilities. When it ends, both parties are breathing harder than the violence warranted.

**What the disk says:** In the moment of impact, the snippet arrives fragmented — the full memory begins but does not complete. What surfaces is the voice of the comrade: *He's solid. I know him. Let it go.* And then the weight of afterward. Not the betrayal itself — just the quality of the space after, when the thing that was seen and not spoken had come to pass. The disk delivering its note: *you have done this before. You read the room. You chose the borrowed frame. You know how this ends.*

**Wound:** *I had presence. I spent it like authority. I saw his posture in the disk before I reached him. I didn't use what I saw.*

---

### Path B: Engaging the Horsemen (deviation)

If the Seraph challenges the horsemen directly — interposes with Valor's weight, demands they stand down, makes their authority the confrontation — the lead rider's assessment completes.

What happens next is professional and impersonal. These are men who know how to respond to challenges from individuals without recognized authority in public spaces. They have done it before. It is not a beating — it is a demonstration, a reminder, a teaching in the specific language of men who enforce things for a living. Non-lethal. Efficient. Not particularly interested in the Seraph as a person.

**The disk during the engagement:** Divided — like a lantern held at two angles at once. There is something right about standing against authority that has expired. Valor is not wrong here. But the disk's reflection at the moment of engagement shows the Seraph what they look like from outside: not the radiant figure the crowd has been giving space to, but someone who has made themselves a combatant. Someone who has given the horsemen exactly the kind of problem they are equipped to solve.

The scuffle is more significant than Path A and less clean. Resolve it appropriate to the Seraph's capabilities. When it ends, the horsemen have made their point and the Seraph is on the ground or steadied.

**What the disk says:** The snippet arrives in the aftermath — the Seraph recovering, the horsemen regrouping, the crowd very carefully not having seen anything. The same memory begins. What surfaces: the warmth of the comrade's fire. The ally's face across it. And then — what was visible in the disk that night, and what was not said about it. The disk asking rather than answering: *what were you, just now? What were you not?*

**Wound:** *I stood between them. As a combatant. I used what I am as a weapon in someone else's fight. The disk didn't tell me this was wrong. It asked me what I thought.*

---

### GM Notes (all paths)

- The Seraph's presence in the open space has already done something before any choice is made. The standoff exists *because* they stepped into it. This is true regardless of which path follows.
- During the standoff: as both dice descend, name what changes. The rider's hand moves away from the ledger. The merchant's posture shifts. Someone in the crowd turns back toward their transaction. The young orc's hand comes out of the coat slowly, and when they look at the Seraph, the look contains something unreadable — not gratitude, not acknowledgment, something prior to both of those.
- On Path B: the young orc slips out during the engagement with the horsemen. Quietly. This is its own detail — the GM holds it.
- On all paths: the rider pauses before leaving (or after subduing the Seraph) and looks at them one more time. He doesn't say anything. He looks like a man filing something away.

## Segment 6: The Snippet (2-3 minutes)

### The Memory That Surfaces

**[At the moment of the first step forward — wherever that step leads — the disk delivers what it has been holding.]**

*Not the courtyard. Not morning. Somewhere else: the specific disorientation of a memory that arrives with the weight of experience — not remembered, relived.*

---

A fire at a campaign's edge. Pine-resin and cook-smoke, the cold coming down from higher ground at night, the warmth of people who have survived a hard thing together and built something worth sitting beside.

Two figures across the fire. The comrade's face won't fully resolve — but the feeling does: the weight of trust built through things survived together. And beside them, the ally. New to the circle. Vouched for.

*He's solid. I know him. Let it go.*

The disk is in the hand, here too. And in its surface: something. The ally's reflection sits slightly wrong. Not monstrous. Not obviously dangerous. The specific wrongness of a thing that is not what it presents itself as — the same note the disk has always used for that signal.

The comrade's hand on the shoulder. *You worry too much. He's with us.*

Setting it down.

Not the disk — the knowledge. The seen thing. Choosing to fold the sight, to honor the comrade's judgment over what the obsidian showed, to be the kind of person who does not make accusations they cannot prove against someone a friend has vouched for.

The ally remained trusted. The comrade remained certain.

*Later:*

The weight of what the choosing-not-to-see had permitted. Not the event itself — the memory keeps that blurred, merciful — just the specific heaviness of having seen and having chosen the looking-away, and learning afterward what the looking-away had cost.

---

*The courtyard reconstitutes. The disk in the hand. The standoff, or the aftermath of whichever path was taken.*

*And with the courtyard: understanding, arriving without words.*

*Then, friends coming together. I read the room. I stepped back.*

*Now: potential enemies. I stepped forward.*

*The difference is not certainty. I was not more certain this morning than I was that night. The difference is the choice.*

---

### GM Notes

- Deliver the memory as read-aloud narration — the player receives it, they don't play through it
- The comrade's face should not resolve. The ally's name should not surface. These are fragments — the Warren delivering enough to illuminate, not enough to explain
- The disk's wrongness in the memory: it looked the same then as it looks now in this caravanserai. The same signal. The Seraph has always had this. They have not always acted on it.
- The final understanding: not stated as a lesson. Arrived at as weight. *The difference is not certainty. The difference is the choice.*
- After the snippet: pause. Do not ask a question immediately. Let the player sit. Then, quietly: *"Where is your character?"*

## Segment 7: The Aftermath (3-4 minutes)

### After the Standoff

**[Primary path — both counters at 0, the confrontation resolved. The morning after its crisis.]**

The lead rider closes his ledger.

No acknowledgment of what changed or why. No recognition of the Seraph, of the pause, of the specific quality of the morning that made pressing the matter cost more than it was worth. He turns his horse with the efficiency of a man who has completed his assessment and found the situation not worth continuing. The formation follows. The hoofbeats cross the courtyard and pass through the main gate and diminish on the eastern road.

The merchant does not move immediately. He watches the gate for the space of several breaths. Then he begins moving again — the same cargo, the same offload — but with the manner of someone who has remembered that he has a body and it is still here.

The young orc is gone. The hand that came away from the coat, the feet that turned toward the side gate — that was quiet. That was not the retreat of someone who has been stopped. It was someone who had seen something they didn't expect to see and gone away to think about it.

The courtyard restores itself. The weight-argument resumes. The camels have continued declining the morning. Somewhere, the smithy fire.

The merchant finishes securing the last mule-load. Then he looks at the Seraph — a direct look, the first direct look from anyone in the courtyard since the horsemen arrived.

He says something in the trade language, formal and without flourish:

*You stood there.*

Not a question.

*Can you use a blade?*

The disk in the hand. The reflection: the merchant's face, clear, looking at the Seraph with the expression of someone asking a practical question and meaning it practically. The disk says nothing. It shows the face. The face is offering something.

### GM Notes

- The merchant's offer: a caravan guard's weapon — a short blade, good road-steel, the kind a caravan outfit keeps for the people it hires. Possibly padded armor, possibly a traveling pack. Not divine equipment. Earned equipment. Offered by someone whose goods are still on the mules because of what happened this morning.
- The Seraph should receive this without knowing quite what to make of it. They stepped into the open space and held it. They didn't draw a weapon — they don't have one. They didn't claim authority. They just stood there. And now a stranger is offering them a blade.
- The young orc's departure: a thread. Unnamed. Do not follow it here.
- The lead rider's final look, before he left: the GM holds this. He noted them on the way in. He noted them on the way out. Whatever that becomes, it becomes later.

### Player Agency

The player can:
- Accept the merchant's offer (the blade, some gear, a word of thanks)
- Decline and move toward the caravan anyway
- Ask the merchant what he saw
- Look into the disk — which shows the courtyard and themselves, clear and unremarkable, whatever quality of "more" it held earlier quieter now

Prompt: *"The caravan is forming at the western gate. What does your character carry into the road?"*

## Segment 8: Convergence (2-3 minutes)

### The Road West

**[The caravan and what joins it.]**

The caravan forms with the particular organized chaos of its kind: draft animals being persuaded toward appropriate positions, cargo lashed and tested, the lead merchant's voice cutting through the courtyard noise with the authority of someone who has been giving this exact set of instructions for thirty years and has no patience for the animals that haven't learned them.

The Seraph is part of this now. Not because anyone announced it — because they are moving with it, and no one is suggesting otherwise, and the merchant with the eastern silks has already communicated something to the caravan lead, because the caravan lead gave the Seraph the look of a man who has been told something and has decided to accept the information.

*West.* The lead merchant to the Seraph, passing without stopping. *Three days to the waystation. You're welcome to the road.*

Not generous. Practical. People who have come through difficult mornings in caravanserais tend to need somewhere to go next.

The eastern gate closes behind the last animal. The road west opens.

The disk is in the hand. The blade — if accepted — is at the hip, unfamiliar in a way that will resolve with time and use. The morning has given the Seraph: a direction, a group of people moving through it, and the weight of a memory that arrived without asking and has not finished settling.

*The difference is not certainty. The difference is the choice.*

At the western gate: other travelers joining the caravan — people who were not there an hour ago, drawn to the same road by different circumstances. Some of them carry themselves in ways that are difficult to categorize: the distribution of their attention, the manner of people who have learned to notice exits and entrances from long practice.

Not acquaintances. But not strangers in the way most strangers are strangers.

The caravan moves.

### GM Notes

- The other travelers: the convergence arriving. Present as details in the motion of departure. Do not emphasize. Do not introduce.
- The disk in the caravan: quiet now, warm, patient. The Hunger signal will return. This is not its last morning.
- The wound the Seraph carries: they stepped forward without authority and without certainty. They held a space with nothing but their presence and the watching of a god who does not intervene directly. They received a weapon — if they took it — as thanks from a stranger. And they carry a memory that showed them the difference between the time they looked away and the time they didn't: not which one was right, but that the choice was theirs, and was always theirs, and the disk has only ever been showing them what they already had the capacity to see.

**The awakening ends here. The convergence begins.**

## GM Notes: Overall Structure

**Segment Breakdown:**
1. **The Waking (3-5 min):** Already standing, disk in hand, arranged space, first reflection
2. **The Courtyard (3-5 min):** Horsemen arrive, survey, pause on the Seraph, move toward the merchant
3. **The Confrontation (5-8 min):** The payment dispute, the crowd's avoidance, the young orc, the disk's wrongness
4. **The Disk (3-4 min):** The prayer without answer, the Sky-Father's watching, Hunger and Resist signals
5. **The Step (8-12 min, variable):** Standoff mechanic, Path A, Path B
6. **The Snippet (2-3 min):** The ally memory, the choosing-not-to-see, the difference
7. **The Aftermath (3-4 min):** The merchant's offer, the weapon, the road forming
8. **Convergence (2-3 min):** West, other travelers, the wound carried forward

**Total Estimated Time:** 30-45 minutes (variable by path and player engagement)

**Convergence Note:** The Seraph joins the caravan as a practical outcome of having been in this place and having done what they did. They are not invited because they are heroic — they are invited because the merchant vouched for them and the caravan lead accepted the voucher. The road is practical. The reason they are on it is not.
