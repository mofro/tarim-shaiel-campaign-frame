# Session 0: Sentinel Archetype Awakening

## Overview

**Archetype:** Sentinel
**Core Doubt:** "Is presence ever enough — or is the watch always incomplete?"
**Tool:** Cracked lantern (signal post mount; found, not bound)
**Setting:** Ancient fortress ruins on high ground above a branching gorge system
**Crisis:** Two groups below, two directions, one fractured signal — hold the post or move into the dark
**Duration:** 40-45 minutes total play time
**Tone:** Vast isolation → immediate impossible recognition → geometric crisis → identity through motion

---

## Campaign Context

This awakening serves the larger Session 0 architecture:
- **Individual crisis** specific to Sentinel archetype (presence as protection, tested by the limits of a fixed position)
- **Secret snippet moment** integrated into the crisis (the moment of commitment triggers mythic memory)
- **Convergence path** to caravan (survivors from the gorge join the westward road)
- **Established wound** — the Sentinel arrives carrying the question of whether their choice was right

The Sentinel enters the convergence scene as *the one who had to choose who to protect.* Unlike heroes who arrive through accident or capability, they arrive carrying the weight of a decision whose consequences they may not yet be able to fully count.

## Setup Notes (For GM)

**What the GM knows that the player doesn't:**

- The Sentinel did not arrive at the fortress ruins by ordinary travel — they simply woke there
- The signal post has been unlit for hours; the two groups below have been navigating in darkness
- Group A found the correct gorge approach and has been ascending — they are close, but the flood rising in the valley floor is pushing them faster than they can safely move
- Group B took the wrong tributary in the dark during the initial panic — they do not yet know the path ahead dead-ends; their flood risk is the same but their exit is not
- The cracked lantern has been at the signal post for years — it is not new; the crack is old damage, worn smooth at the edges
- The flood is glacial melt, not storm-driven; it will rise steadily, not catastrophically — time is real but not instant

**Contested-site context:**
The gorge system below was not random. The Wizard's agents seeded the panic that sent both groups into the dark — a coordinated action on the valley road below that scattered a larger caravan party. The flood is natural and coincidental; the agents did not cause it, but they read the terrain and chose their moment. The Sentinel woke at the signal post because the immune system's counter-force placed them there: not where the fight was, but where the gap would be. The agents may have expected the Sentinel to be drawn down into the valley toward the visible conflict, leaving the gorge approaches unlit.

**Passive hook:** The scattered caravan below — the one the agents disrupted — contains someone who saw what actually happened. A merchant, a guard, a traveling scholar. If the party ever returns to this valley, that witness is findable. What the agents did to scatter the caravan was distinctive enough to be recognized by anyone who understands what to look for.

**Dual-truth preservation:**
- Storyteller knows: The Sentinel was placed precisely here, at exactly this gap, because something needed watching that couldn't watch itself
- Player knows: They woke at a signal post with a cracked lantern and voices coming up from the dark in two directions

**R/H/K — The lantern as Warren ambassador:**

The cracked lantern is not loyal to the Sentinel. Its behavior reflects Warren interests — the interests of whatever Warren faction placed the Sentinel at this post. The scene's physics already encode the arc; the GM reads the lantern through this frame, not the player.

- **Resist:** The forward beam — steady, reliable, aimed at what the signal post was designed to serve. The Warren protecting its investment: this position, this watchpoint, this specific gap. When the Sentinel holds the lantern forward, something settles in the grip. Not comfort — *correctness*. This is what the lantern is for. This is what it wants to be: true. A single strong beam, a clear signal, a position held. When the Sentinel considers moving, the primary beam doesn't waver. That steadiness is not encouragement; it is the Warren's preference made visible. *Stay where you were placed. Be the watchtower flame.*

- **Hunger:** The crack's sideways scatter. Light going in directions the lantern was never designed to reach — already angling toward Group B before the Sentinel has decided anything. A Warren calling for extended coverage, more ground illuminated, the watch expanded past its designed scope. The scatter is the Warren's appetite arriving ahead of the Sentinel's intention. **What the Sentinel feels when the crack becomes the focus:** the grip doesn't change, but something does. The lantern held forward has a quality of wholeness to it. The lantern angled for the scatter is divided — light going two ways at once, neither beam what it would be alone. The lantern does not resist this. It simply shines wherever it's pointed. But it offers no confirmation that the crack will reach them. It offers no opinion at all. The Sentinel cannot read certainty from the light. That absence is the Hunger signal in full: *I will go there. I cannot tell you if it will be enough.*

- **Know:** The snippet at the moment of commitment. The Warren delivers what it has been holding: *fixed positions have always had edges. The watch-fire did its job. Something still happened.* This arrives regardless of which path the Sentinel takes — the revelation is not conditional on the outcome. It was waiting for the moment of commitment itself.

**The three paths read through R/H/K:**
- **Hold:** The Sentinel answers Resist — stays in the Warren's preferred position. The forward beam is what it was built to be. But the Hunger signal (the scatter) was already reaching toward what the post couldn't cover.
- **Move:** The Sentinel follows Hunger — carries the lantern to where the scatter was already pointing. Whether the Warren intended this outcome or merely called for it is worth leaving unresolved.
- **Split:** The Sentinel tries to answer both signals at once. The Know moment arrives: the Warren's patience with this question runs to longer than the Sentinel has been alive to ask it.

## The Vigil Clock

### What the player sees

Place two dice on the table — one for each group below, both showing 4. These numbers represent how much time each group has before the flood decides for them. Your actions push those numbers up. The scene pushes them down. Where the dice sit when it ends is what happened.

### How the dice move

**Group A's die** holds steady while the signal is maintained. If the Sentinel leaves the post or the lantern moves, it ticks down 1 per narrative beat.

**Group B's die** ticks down 1 per narrative beat regardless. It will not hold on its own — they cannot see the signal and have no way to self-correct.

> *Narrative beat:* each time the GM marks a scene transition or judges that meaningful time has passed — roughly every two minutes of real time, or when the fiction demands it.

### Action rolls — Difficulty 14

A roll is called when the Sentinel takes a physical or navigational action that could meaningfully succeed or fail. Holding the lantern steady is not a roll. These are:

| Action | Attribute |
|---|---|
| Moving with the lantern down the cliff path | Agility |
| Angling the crack to reach the tributary mouth | Finesse |
| Calling guidance over flood noise and panicking voices | Presence |
| Reading the gorge acoustics before committing to a move | Instinct |
| Wedging the lantern at the post to free both hands | Instinct |
| Steadying a Group A climber over the final section one-handed | Strength |
| Keeping the flame alive while moving fast through wind | Finesse or Instinct |

Before rolling, the player may argue that Pari-kin or a relevant archetype ability applies. If the argument holds, grant advantage on the roll.

### Roll results

| Result | Die effect |
|---|---|
| Critical success | Target group's die +2 |
| Success with Hope | Target group's die +1 |
| Success with Fear | Target group's die +1; other group's die −1 |
| Failure with Hope | No change |
| Failure with Fear | Target group's die −1 |

*Success with Fear* represents time well spent — but the cost lands on the group not being helped. The flood doesn't pause while you're focused elsewhere.

### Path behavior (GM only)

- **Hold:** Group A's die holds steady automatically (no ticks) while the signal is maintained. Group B's die ticks every beat. The Sentinel may roll to guide Group B through the crack from a fixed position — but the geometry is against it. Add +2 to difficulty, or call at disadvantage.
- **Move:** Group A's die begins ticking the moment the Sentinel leaves the post. Group B gets direct rolls at standard difficulty. The Sentinel is racing to push Group B's die high enough before Group A's drops too far.
- **Split:** Both dice follow their natural tick rate. The Sentinel rolls for one group per action, alternating. No passive hold for either group.

### Resolution

When the scene ends, read each die:

| Value | What happened to that group |
|---|---|
| 5–6 | Safe — arrived with time to spare |
| 3–4 | Safe — arrived shaken and close, but clear |
| 2 | Recoverable — one more action or desperate push gets them out |
| 1 | In danger — they may survive, but not unscathed |
| 0 | Did not make it |

Both dice at 3 or above = the Sentinel held the line. One die at 0 = the Sentinel made a choice, whether they meant to or not. The wound they carry into the convergence scene comes from this math, not from a GM judgment call.

## Segment 1: The Dark Waking (3-5 minutes)

### Opening Narration

**[Space before person — the Sentinel wakes to height and openness, the situation already in progress around them. No rush. The voices are the interruption, not the opening.]**

![[Light to Guide - Sentinel.mp3]]

The height registers, before awareness does, as a yawning absence: nothing pressing close, just an openness that must extend all the way to a horizon that black night has made theoretical.

Something vast is below. Your body knows this as surely as the stone under your cheek is cold and hard. And just as solidly.

Where  ...  is this.

The question surfaces without urgency. The darkness doesn't answer.

You get to your feet.

Wind moves — not a gust, but a current that has been crossing this ground longer than you've been conscious of it. It carries cold from higher up, and beneath that, the smell of old stone: dry, crusted, undisturbed. And underneath, from further below, something almost loam — earth with moisture in it somewhere deeper down.

Your eyes adjust slowly. Stars overhead, numerous in the way altitude permits, closer than they should be. You can sense nothing near in almost every direction save one.

Your hands move in exploration across fitted stone: worn at the crown by age, exposure, and by contact. Perhaps by the accumulated weight of other hands through other nights. Below it, on the interior face, a courtyard faintly resolves in meager starlight — walls at various heights, chest-high and knee-high and gone altogether in places.

This place feels like a deliberate choice.

The wind shifts. From below: the sound of water, somewhere.

The signal post is at the parapet's highest corner, visible when you turn. Your hands find the bracket first — iron-ringed, built to hold a serious fire, deep enough to throw light across the whole valley below. Cold inside. Ash, compacted and old. Nothing here to light.

Below it, sheltered at the post's base: a lantern. Iron body, glass intact on three sides. A small pouch of flint and tinder wedged beside it — not forgotten, placed. Someone who maintained this post understood: the bracket-fire needs preparation and fuel; the lantern needs only will.

The spark catches on the third strike. The wick takes the flame.

The light comes out divided. A crack runs from base to crown — old damage, the edges worn smooth from years of being exactly what they are — and the flame breathes through it in two directions at once: forward, out over the valley, and sideways at an angle no whole lantern would permit.

The wind shifts again. Below, the water... advances.

And then — from below, in two directions at once —

Voices.

### GM Notes

- The Sentinel wakes to *space*, not crisis — let the orientation breathe before anything intrudes
- "You get to your feet" before the wind section: they're standing in the dark, feeling the openness, before the situation makes itself known
- The water sound arrives before the voices — plant it plainly; "advances" is enough
- The post has two light sources: the iron bracket (signal fire, cold and depleted) and the lantern (fueled, flint and tinder placed beside it). The Sentinel never has to choose — the bracket is empty, the lantern is ready. This is practical, not significant. The significance is in the crack, not the choosing.
- The crack is revealed by the flame — it doesn't exist until the light does. Old damage, matter-of-fact. Don't editorialize.
- Voices as interruption: everything before them is orientation. The player should feel the scene was already settled when the voices arrived.

### Player Agency

The player is now awake and aware of:
- Their position: high ground, ancient fortress ruins, stone parapet
- The tool: cracked lantern, lit at the signal post by the Sentinel's own hands — the crack revealed only when the flame caught
- The situation: voices from below, two directions, not yet understood
- The darkness: the gorges are not yet readable

They can act. Prompt them: *"What do you do?"*

## Segment 2: The High Ground (5-8 minutes)

### What the Ruins Show

**[The lantern's light — even fractured — begins to reveal the waystation. Context arrives through observation, not explanation.]**

The fortress reveals itself in pieces, the way ruins do: suggesting what was rather than stating it.

Stone walls chest-high in places, knee-high in others, collapsed altogether where the weight of centuries decided to redistribute itself. A courtyard of sorts, open to the sky, the signal post at its highest corner — positioned to be visible from the valley below on clear days, firelit from the surrounding terrain on dark ones. Whoever built this chose the site with care: the rock face drops away sharply on three sides, and the fourth, the approach, is a narrow path cut into the cliff that a single person could hold against many.

Old char on the stones around the bracket, laid down over seasons and decades — this post has been lit many times, by many hands, whenever the gorge below needed a lamp at its crown. The bracket sits cold and depleted now. The lantern was the ready thing.

The lantern in hand throws its divided light: forward, over the parapet, into the dark where the valley should be — and sideways, backward, across the stones of the courtyard. The crack doesn't weaken the forward beam so much as *add* to it. More directions than intended. Less precision. More reach.

The path down is visible in the forward light: a stone-cut descent, switchbacking against the cliff face, leading to where the gorge system opens below. From the parapet edge, the terrain resolves in fragments. Two gorges. The near approach on the left — the correct path, the one that climbs toward this position in a series of navigable switchbacks. The far approach on the right — a tributary, narrower, its walls closer together, going in a direction that looks parallel to the main gorge but isn't. It bends. It narrows. It does not arrive here.

The voices are clearer now.

Left: multiple people, the sound of feet on stone, a voice giving direction, the particular cadence of movement that knows what it's doing but is going faster than comfort allows. Water sounds beneath them — not present, but close. Rising.

Right: fewer voices, slower rhythm, a silence between exchanges that might be distance or might be something else. Going deeper into the tributary rather than back.

### GM Notes

- The ruins establish the Sentinel's *context*: this post has been held before. They woke into a role, not just a location
- The two gorges should feel like a navigational problem made visible — the player should be able to see why Group B is in trouble even before contact
- The water sound under Group A is the flood signal: plant it, don't explain it
- The crack's sideways scatter is the first hint of its value in motion — the light is going directions the Sentinel didn't direct it
- Keep Group B's situation implied, not stated: *going deeper* is all that's needed here

### Player Agency

The player now understands:
- The signal post and its purpose — this is a waystation beacon
- Two groups below, two separate gorges
- Group A: correct path, ascending, something behind them
- Group B: wrong gorge, going deeper
- The geometry is legible from above in a way it isn't from below

Prompt them: *"What do you do?"*

## Segment 3: Two Directions (3-4 minutes)

### First Contact

**[The Sentinel calls down, or is called to. The situation declares itself fully.]**

The voice from the left gorge reaches first, because it is closer and because it is looking for something to reach:

*"The light — is someone there? Can you hear—"*

A pause in which the careful distance between hope and certainty is maintained, the way people who have been disappointed calibrate their optimism.

*"We need the path. The water is — the lower crossing is gone. We have children."*

Not panic. Controlled urgency. The voice of someone who has been making decisions in the dark for long enough to know that panic is a luxury they can't afford.

From the right, carried at greater distance through the narrower walls: a different voice, pitched differently — not calling up, not yet certain there is an up to call to. Conferring with someone close by.

*"...doesn't open. I told you, there's no—"*

*"Then we go back."*

*"We can't go back. The water—"*

Silence, then, that has the texture of people looking at each other in the dark and arriving at the same conclusion simultaneously.

Then, louder, directed upward:

*"Is anyone there? We've taken the wrong — we need direction."*

### GM Notes

- The two voices should feel distinct: Group A is disciplined, experienced, close to safe; Group B is realizing their situation for the first time
- "We have children" is the human-stakes anchor for Group A — not manipulation, just fact
- Group B's exchange is overheard, not directed at first: they don't yet know the Sentinel is there
- The flood under Group A is real and present — "the lower crossing is gone" establishes it without requiring explanation
- Group B's flood is implied: "we can't go back, the water" — the same flood, the same timing, the wrong gorge

### Player Agency

The player can:
- Call down to one or both groups
- Assess which group is closer to safety, which is further from it
- Begin to understand the physical distances and timing involved
- Ask questions — the voices will answer

The situation is now fully stated. Everything after this is the player deciding what to do about it.

## Segment 4: The Reach (5-8 minutes)

### The Geometry Becomes Concrete

**[The Sentinel tests the lantern, tests the geometry, and arrives at the shape of the impossible.]**

The parapet edge shows what the darkness was hiding: the left gorge approach is forty, perhaps fifty meters below the signal post at its nearest point — a distance a clear signal reaches easily. The right gorge is further and angled away, its walls curving inward as they deepen, turning the tributary into something that funnels sound without directing light.

Testing it: hold the lantern over the parapet toward the right gorge. The forward beam reaches the gorge mouth but bends wrong against the near wall — the angle of the cliff face catches the light and scatters it before it can penetrate the tributary's depth. Group B cannot see the signal from where they are. The gorge walls are doing what gorge walls do.

The crack helps, partially. The sideways scatter catches the near cliff face and throws an indirect glow into the tributary's upper section — enough to suggest direction to someone who is looking, not enough to guide someone who doesn't know where to look.

Group A can see the lantern clearly. They are already adjusting their line toward it.

Group B needs someone to come to them.

The flood sound under Group A is growing. Not rapidly — this is not a wall of water, it is a rise, patient and geological in its indifference, the accumulated melt of somewhere higher channeled into the places where people thought it was safe to walk. Safe until it wasn't.

Group B has less time than Group A, or the same time through a worse path. The tributary will fill from below just as the main gorge is filling. The difference is: Group A has a way out, and they can see the light that marks it. Group B has a way out too — backward, through water, into the correct gorge — but they cannot find it without guidance they cannot receive from a fixed position.

### GM Notes

- Let the player physically work through the geometry: encourage them to think about angles, distances, what the lantern can and cannot reach
- The crack's partial reach into the tributary is real but insufficient — it hints at the behavioral save without delivering it from a fixed position
- The flood's pace: steady, not sudden. The urgency is real but not instant. The Sentinel has time to make a considered choice, not a panicked one
- Group A is self-sufficient with the fixed signal — they just need it to stay on
- Group B requires the Sentinel to move — nothing else reaches them reliably
- **The lantern's felt voice at this moment:** When the Sentinel holds the lantern forward over the parapet toward Group A, something in the grip is settled — the lantern doing what it was made to do, the light going where it is supposed to go. When they angle it toward the crack's scatter, testing whether it reaches the tributary, that quality changes. Not resistance. The lantern shines wherever it's pointed. But the beam is divided, the forward beam diminished, and the scatter offers no confirmation — it reaches or it doesn't, and the light cannot tell them which. If a player asks "does the lantern feel different when I angle it?", the answer is: *yes. Not heavier. Just less certain. Like it knows it wasn't made for this, and is doing it anyway.*

### Player Agency

The player now understands:
- Group A: guided by the fixed signal, ascending correctly, flood behind them
- Group B: cannot see the fixed signal, flood behind them, path dead-ending ahead
- The lantern from the post cannot do both jobs
- Moving the lantern helps Group B but risks Group A losing their guide

Prompt them: *"What do you do?"*

## Segment 5: The Fracture (3-5 minutes)

### Time Enters

**[The variables stop being abstract. The flood makes itself heard. Group B's situation worsens.]**

Group A's voice again, closer now — they have been ascending while the geometry was being worked out:

*"We can see you. We're coming. Don't move the light."*

From the right gorge, Group B's voice: something has changed. The calculating tone has been replaced by something more compressed.

*"It's rising faster. We need—"*

A child's voice, briefly, from the same direction. Not words. Just sound.

Then the adult again, controlled again, but the control is costing more than it was:

*"If someone can hear us. We're in the tributary. The east fork. We came in from the valley road and we turned — we didn't know it dead-ended. If there is a way out, we can't see it. The water is at our knees."*

Water at their knees.

Group A is perhaps ten minutes from the top.

Group B, without guidance, does not have ten minutes of good options.

The lantern's crack throws its sideways light against the near wall. In motion — descending the cliff path, moving toward the tributary mouth — that scatter would change. The angle would change. The light that currently misses Group B's gorge by thirty degrees of cliff face might, from a lower position, from a moving position, find a different angle. Might reach.

Might.

### GM Notes

- The child's voice is not manipulation — it is information. There are children in Group B too. Don't underscore it; let it land and move on
- "Water at their knees" is the time anchor — Group B is in the flood now
- The crack's potential in motion: let the player discover this, don't state it. They may ask "what if I move?" — let them reason it through
- Group A's "don't move the light" is the competing demand, stated plainly, without drama. They need what they need

### Player Agency

The player now faces the full choice:
- Hold: Group A makes it. Group B's fate is uncertain.
- Move: Group B has a chance. Group A loses their anchor.
- Move with the cracked lantern held at the right angle: both groups might track it. Risky.

This is the last moment before commitment. Prompt them: *"What do you do?"*

## Segment 6: The Signal (5-7 minutes)

### The Commitment

**[The player chooses. The consequences begin immediately. This segment plays out the choice's direct result — not its full aftermath, but the first returns.]**

### If the player holds the post:

Group A arrives. Eight people — the voice, two others, four children, one elderly man who is carried the last section of the climb. They emerge from the switchback path and onto the fortress stones with the particular silence of people who have been keeping themselves quiet for too long and don't know yet that they can stop.

The voice — a woman, it turns out, scarred at the jaw, the kind of scar that happens in the particular circumstances that teach people to give directions in the dark — looks at the Sentinel for a long moment.

*"You held it,"* she says. Not gratitude. Inventory.

From the right gorge, below, a sound that is not voices.

### If the player moves with the lantern:

The cliff path is cut into the rock at steep angles, the kind of descent that requires hands as well as feet in sections. The lantern goes in the hand that can be held out — away from the body, at the angle that sends the crack's scatter toward the tributary mouth rather than back against the cliff.

From above, Group A will see the light moving. They will have to decide what to do about that on their own.

The tributary mouth is a slot between two close walls. The lantern's fractured light enters the slot in the way that a whole beam wouldn't — the scatter finds the inner walls and bounces, irregular and incomplete but present. Enough. Group B is visible: six people, knee-to-thigh deep in water that has the grey-brown color of what it has collected on its way here, faces turned toward the unexpected light.

The way out is behind them. The Sentinel is the guide.

### If the player tries to serve both:

The lantern is held at the parapet at the angle that sends the crack's scatter toward the tributary — the lower angle, as though the light is trying to fall rather than project. Group A can still see the primary beam. Group B might see the scatter.

Might.

Group A's voice, ascending: *"Don't — what are you doing? Keep it still."*

Group B's voice, from the tributary: a pause, then — *"There. Something. East — is that — go toward it."*

The angle is wrong for certainty. It is right for possibility.

### GM Notes — All Paths

- **Hold the post:** Group A arrives complete. From the right gorge, the sound of water where voices were. The Sentinel does not know what happened to Group B. Neither does the player. This is the wound.
- **Move with lantern:** Group B is saved. Group A: most arrive — they were close, the path was clear, the woman's discipline carried them. One of the children, the youngest, was carried by the old man. He made it. They all made it, this time. The Sentinel was not there to see it. This is also the wound.
- **Split the difference:** Both groups mostly make it. Someone from Group B turns an ankle on the scramble out, slow, requires help. One of Group A's party — not a child — loses the light briefly, takes a wrong step, is bruised but functional. Nobody dies on either path. But both groups arrived with someone who almost didn't. The Sentinel was everywhere and completely present nowhere. This is also a wound.

The choice has no right answer. Plant each wound clearly and move on.

## Segment 7: The Secret Snippet (2-3 minutes)

### The Trigger

**[The moment of commitment — whichever choice was made — is when the memory surfaces. The weight of having chosen fires something older.]**

### Setup

The snippet arrives at the moment of commitment: when the Sentinel's hands are steady on the lantern and the choice has been made and the consequences are beginning. Not before. Not after. At the precise moment of *this is what I am doing.*

**[Deliver the snippet quietly — written and passed, or read aloud very low. The player reads it while the scene holds: the lantern in hand, the voices below, the choice already in motion.]**

---

*Not this fortress. Not this gorge.*

*A watchtower at a different pass — larger, older, a structure that had held its position for three hundred years before the night in question. The torches lit along the wall as they always were, the signal fire at the tower's crown, everything correct, everything in place.*

*The position was held. The signal burned.*

*Below, on the road, the column moved through in good order — four thousand people, the count that mattered, the ones the position existed to protect. They came through the narrows and out the other side and the passage was clean.*

*The fourth thousand. The last of them.*

*And then the messenger, arriving from the direction that had not been watched, because the position was designed to watch the road and not the eastern ridge, because presence at one point cannot be presence at all points, because a watch-fire is a fixed thing and fixed things have edges.*

*What happened on the eastern ridge that night?*

*The count that mattered was four thousand. The count that came through was four thousand. Both true.*

*And the watch-fire was still burning when morning came, exactly as it should have been, having done exactly what it was built to do.*

* The lantern still in hand. And yet... did the signal stand correct?*

*The question that has no answer forming in the bones like cold: what is protection, if it only covers what it was aimed at?*

---

### GM Notes

- Deliver this at the exact moment of commitment — not earlier
- The snippet does not tell the player their choice was wrong. It tells them this question is *old*
- The count came through. The watch-fire did its job. Something still happened.
- Do not explain the eastern ridge. The player will fill that silence with whatever fits their character
- After the snippet: pause. Then simply continue. *"What does your character do with that?"*

## Segment 8: The Road (3-5 minutes)

### What Remains

**[Resolution. The survivors gathered. The caravan path. The wound carried forward.]**

Dawn comes into the gorge from the east, the way it always does in narrow places: later than the open sky above, as though the canyon has to be convinced.

The people who made it are on the high stones of the fortress courtyard. Some sitting. Some still standing, because sitting down means admitting that the thing that was pursuing them is no longer pursuing them, and not everyone is ready to admit that yet.

The flood has not retreated — it will not retreat for hours, perhaps longer. The valley road below is impassable. The only way forward is the one the fortress sits above: a trail along the ridge, connecting eventually to the road west. The Silk Road, or what it has become in this era of its long life.

A caravan is on that road.

The woman with the scarred jaw, if she is there, says it practically: *"West is the only direction. There's a merchant train — we saw their dust yesterday before everything—"*

She stops. Considers.

*"They'll take survivors. They always do. It's good business."*

The cracked lantern is in hand, unlit now in the morning. The crack runs its full length, visible and old, the edges worn smooth. It has been broken for a long time. It was broken before tonight. It will be broken tomorrow.

It worked anyway.

The road west forms from the people who made it through. The Sentinel moves with them, or leads them, or follows behind them — depending on what the choice was, depending on what the wounds are.

The question that has no answer yet: *what is protection, if it only covers what it was aimed at?*

That question travels west.

### GM Notes

- The wound does not resolve here. Do not offer resolution
- The woman with the scarred jaw, if present, is a practical ally — not warm, not effusive, but real. She names the caravan, she names the direction, she does not ask questions she doesn't need answered
- The cracked lantern in morning light: visible now, unromantic, just a cracked tool that worked. Let it be seen plainly
- The phrase "it worked anyway" is not a comfort. It is a fact. Let it be both
- End on motion, not resolution — they are walking west, carrying the question

### Player Agency

The player can:
- Speak with the survivors
- Look back at what the gorge now holds
- Simply walk
