# Ancestry Secret Names — GM Reference

*Two ancestries in Tarim-Shaiel carry sacred self-names unknown to the wider world.
These are not passwords or plot devices — they are expressions of identity that predate
the current age. Players may eventually hear them, but only if they earn that intimacy.*

---

## YULDUZIR — The Elven Self-Name

**What the world calls them:** *Serenvar* ("those who have stillness")
**What they call themselves:** *Yulduzir* ("star-people")

### Linguistic root
Turkic *yulduz* (star) + ancient Iranian nominal ending *-ir*. The *-ir* form is
older than the Persian register most of Tarim-Shaiel uses — it sounds like something
that was named before the current world's lingua franca existed. Because it was.

### What it means to Elves
*Yulduzir* encodes their cosmological origin story: they do not believe they are
*of* the world in the way other peoples are. Their celestial trance (what others
call sleep) is understood internally as a return — brief, nightly — to the place
they came from. Their mystic transformations are read as the star-nature expressing
itself through the body. An Elf who lives long enough and deeply enough eventually
*looks* like what they believe they are.

The name is not secret because Elves are ashamed of it. It is private because they
do not share it with people who would not understand it — which is most people.

### Reveal conditions
An Elf NPC speaks *Yulduzir* to a player character when:
- The player has demonstrated genuine curiosity about Elven cosmology (not
  transactional curiosity — actual interest)
- The player has earned personal trust with a specific Elven NPC over time
- The moment feels like an invitation rather than an information dump

When it lands, it should feel like being let in on something ancient. The player
has been calling them *Serenvar* for sessions. Hearing *Yulduzir* from an Elf's
own mouth — unprompted, quietly — is a different thing entirely.

## TENGIRCHI — The Orcish Self-Name

**What the world calls them:** *Tulpar* (reclaimed Imperial label — "the winged
horse-people," originally used as a slur)
**What they call themselves:** *Tengirchi* ("people of the sky-father")

### Linguistic root
Turkic *Tengri* (the sky, heaven, the supreme divine force of the steppe cosmology)
+ *-chi* (one who belongs to / practitioner of / people of). *Tengri* is not a
named god in the Abrahamic sense — it is the sky itself as living divine force,
vast and impersonal and encompassing.

### What it means to Orcs
*Tengirchi* predates the Empire. It survived 80 years of magical enslavement not
because it was written anywhere — it wasn't — but because it was passed
mouth-to-ear, spoken quietly between people who were not supposed to have an inner
life the Empire didn't control. Keeping the name alive was a form of resistance
that cost nothing visible and meant everything.

Most Tulpar alive today know *Tengirchi*. It is spoken within clans, at threshold
moments — births, deaths, the sealing of significant oaths. It does not appear in
trade agreements. It does not appear in the diplomatic academies. Outsiders who
have heard it are rare, and the ones who repeated it carelessly did not find the
Tulpar forgiving about it.

### Reveal conditions
A Tulpar NPC speaks *Tengirchi* to a player character when:
- The player has stood with Tulpar in a moment that mattered — not performed
  allyship, but actual shared stakes
- The NPC has reason to believe the player will carry the word with appropriate
  weight
- The context is intimate: a fire, a death, a threshold crossed together

This is not a word given to someone who asked nicely. It is a word given to someone
who has demonstrated they understand what it costs to keep something alive.

## The Sky Resonance — A GM Thread

Both secret names reach toward the sky:
- *Yulduzir* — star-people (celestial origin, nightly return)
- *Tengirchi* — sky-father's people (Tengri as living divine cosmos)

The Serenvar and the Tulpar have almost never spoken directly about this. Their
histories did not bring them into close contact — Elves withdrawn, Orcs in chaos
then trade-building — and neither people volunteers their inner cosmology to
outsiders.

If a player character ever finds themselves in a room where a Tulpar elder and an
Elven elder are both present, and both have spoken their sacred names to that
player, and the player puts it together and asks — *that* is a scene worth
preparing for. Neither elder would have planned for it. The silence that follows
the question would be its own answer.

What they share is not the same thing. *Tengri* is vast and impersonal; the Elven
stars are specific, orienting, a place of return. But the direction is the same.
That they have never compared notes on this is one of the quieter losses in
Tarim-Shaiel's history.

*This thread has no required resolution. It exists to reward players who pay
attention.*
