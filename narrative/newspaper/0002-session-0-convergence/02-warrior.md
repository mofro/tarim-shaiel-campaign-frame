The Warrior woke in a refugee camp without knowing how she'd gotten there — a ravine in the eastern drought lands, thirty souls sheltering under cloth stretched over frames never meant to hold this long. Fires burned low. A place built for hiding, not living.

The camp's leader rose with a spear in hand the moment the Warrior moved. Didn't point it. Didn't need to. What followed was a conversation instead of a fight: the refugees had fled something in the east that moved in a direction no one expected and that no one could name with confidence.

When the Warrior put her blade away it wasn't trust. It was a recognition that standing in a stranger's camp armed while not leaving was its own kind of statement.

A child watched from behind the camp leader's legs. Eventually: *Are you like the ones who burned the village?* An old woman appeared with soup without being asked. The handoff — bowl to hands — was the whole transaction. She walked on without pressing.

Before dawn a scout arrived: imperial patrol, eight soldiers, moving through within the hour. The camp folded with practiced efficiency. The Warrior took the perimeter.

The soldiers came close enough. The blade called. She didn't answer.

The boots passed. The voices faded. They never knew she was there.

By morning the camp leader looked at the Warrior differently. *You can come. If you want. You can fight, can't you? I mean — really fight.*

She went with them. Still confused. Still going west.
