The Sentinel woke in a ruined fortress above a flooding gorge, a cracked lantern burning beside them, lit by hands they didn't remember using. From the parapet, the valley below had become a sound problem: two groups of voices, two gorges, water rising in both.

Left: several voices moving correctly — upward, toward the fortress, someone giving direction with the calm of a person used to deciding things in the dark. Scared, but moving.

Right: fewer voices, quieter, moving the wrong way. Deeper into the gorge instead of up. Already past the point where the lantern's light could reach them.

It didn't take long to decide.

The descent started on foot. Partway down the switchbacks, the shape changed — smaller, lower, four legs instead of two, built for exactly this kind of terrain in the dark. A fox, covering ground the climb wouldn't have allowed.

The group rounded a bend and found a stranger standing where the trail leveled. The woman leading them — scarred at the jaw, Zere, she gave her name partway up the climb — stopped the group before anyone spoke.

*This way's faster. There's a ledge past the switchback. I'll show you.*

She looked for a beat too long to be simple trust. Then nodded once.

They came up onto the fortress stones before the flood reached the lower crossings. From the right gorge, later: water where voices had been.

By dawn the survivors were on the ridge trail, the caravan dust still visible to the west. *They'll take survivors,* Zere said. *Good business for them.*

The lantern hung at the belt, unlit. Something else had done the work.
