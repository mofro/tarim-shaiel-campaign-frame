The Visionary woke already standing in a caravanserai courtyard, a disk of polished obsidian in her right hand, the morning's commerce flowing around her the way water moves around something fixed in a stream. She didn't know how long she'd been there. The crowd did — they'd been giving her space since before she noticed.

Six horsemen arrived through the main gate. Old imperial livery, enforcement authority that would have meant something years ago. The lead rider's survey stopped on a Tulpar merchant managing the offload of eastern goods.

Transit tax, the rider said. New tariff schedule. The merchant's papers don't cover the new rate.

The merchant's papers covered it. Both men knew it. The argument wasn't about tariffs.

The disk shifted in her hand without being told to, showing her something in the shape of the scene that plain sight didn't. At the crowd's edge: someone watching differently than everyone else, a hand inside a coat, the specific stillness of a person who had already decided something serious.

She moved — not decided so much as already underway, the open ground closing before the thought that should have preceded it had finished forming.

Standing there was already something. Then the disk caught the sun — deliberate — and threw it into the rider's eyes. Wings, then. The full measure of what the crowd had been giving space to all morning, finally shown plainly.

The rider's face did something complicated. He closed his ledger. Turned his horse. The formation followed him out without explanation.

Volkath watched the gate for several breaths. Then looked over.

*You stood there. Thank you.* He gave his name like an introduction, not a fact. Said she was more than welcome on his caravan. Said it directly.
