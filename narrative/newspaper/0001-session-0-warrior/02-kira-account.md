The woman who leads this camp — a soldier before she was a refugee — will say only that the town is gone. She gestured vaguely east when pressed for direction; a smear of smoke still hangs on the horizon, perhaps a day's walk. She has not gone back to look.

What drove them out, she cannot describe with confidence. A battle, she says. Something that came from a direction no one expected, moved in a way no one had words for. The retreat was practiced, almost professional. These people had run before.

A caravan heading west was confirmed within the hour. The camp is folding. They move at dawn.
