Word came from the perimeter: an imperial patrol, eight strong, moving through within the hour. The camp moved. Practiced. No panic. Tents folding, fires dying, possessions gathered, children pulled close. The choreography of people who had done this before.

The soldier looked at the Warrior directly. "We can't fight them. Eight soldiers. We're thirty — most children, most old. Fight, and everyone dies. You can watch the perimeter. Can't you?"

The Warrior assented. Went to the edge. Waited.

Eight men. Loose formation. No consistent direction to their scanning. They didn't move like soldiers who knew what they were after. They moved like men who were running from something. Whatever had driven these people into the ravine had perhaps been chasing them too.

The blade waited. The Warrior held. When the soldiers drew close enough to discover the camp — something flashed. And the Warrior stood, muscles coiled, power held like water behind a dam. And she was the dam. She chose to hold.

The boots passed. The voices faded. They never knew she was there.

Dawn came. The soldier approached, and something in how she looked at the Warrior had shifted overnight. Not fear anymore. Appraisal.

"You can come. If you want." A pause. "You can fight, can't you? I mean... really fight."

The Warrior went with them. Didn't say much. Just stood when the camp moved, and moved when they did. Still confused. Still going west.
