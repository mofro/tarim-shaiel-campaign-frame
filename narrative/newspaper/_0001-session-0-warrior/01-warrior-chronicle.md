The Warrior came up cold — not clean winter cold but something that had settled into bone. Her left arm was numb, crushed beneath her. When she flexed her fingers, they moved like things belonging to someone else.

The smells arrived before anything else made sense: unwashed bodies, low fires, grain going sour, and underneath everything the sharp copper of fear-sweat. The reek of people who understood the value of being small.

A blade came up with her arm, trailing muck. Crude iron — no shine, no pride. Just function hammered into shape. Thirty souls sheltered in a shallow ravine nearby, cloth stretched over frames never meant to hold this long. Fires burned low. A place built for hiding, not living.

A child saw her wake and bolted — not toward safety, just away, bare feet finding purchase in the muck. A woman with a soldier's bearing rose in one motion, hand finding her spear. She didn't point it. She didn't need to.

"Don't move," the soldier said, twenty feet out and already in a crouch. "I'm going to ask you something. Think before you answer. What do you remember?"

The Warrior answered honestly: not a lot. She asked who they were. The woman said: refugees. Their town had been overrun by something no one could make sense of — a battle no one understood, that everyone simply fled. She'd marshaled the retreat with everyone she could save.

The conversation settled into something tense but not unfriendly. At some point — without announcement — the blade went back. Not surrender. Not a gesture of trust. Just a recognition that standing in a stranger's camp with a weapon drawn while not leaving was its own kind of statement.

The camp adjusted. Not full acceptance. Adjusted distance.
