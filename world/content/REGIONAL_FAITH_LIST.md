# Regional Faith List

*Draft for GM review (#384). Nothing here is locked. Shares are rough seeds for the settlement passes (child C), not census figures. Deity material is drawn from `world/mythology/LOWER_PANTHEON.md` and `world/mythology/PANTHEON_SUPPLEMENT_NOTES.md`; where the Supplement revises the Lower Pantheon, the Supplement wins. Faith labels are analog names from `MYTHOLOGICAL_MAPPING_FRAMEWORK.md`; in-world names are not set.*

## Intent

A settlement's faith should tell the GM three things: what its shrines look like, what an ordinary person says and does about luck, death, oaths and water, and which neighbours it quietly distrusts. This list replaces the "good / neutral / evil gods" buckets in the Fantasy Town Generator output. Faiths here are **lists with effects**, not alignments. No faith is the villain, and no deity is placed above another in a way a player could use to rank them.

## Ground rules

- **The same-force principle holds.** Faiths that name different deities may be addressing the same force (`LOWER_PANTHEON.md`, design principle). The list records the mask worn, not a claim about what is behind it.
- **Heroes are not religious figures.** No faith here worships, remembers or awaits the heroes, and no entry maps a hero onto a deity. (User decision, #384, 2026-11-06.)
- **Decision 4 stays deferred.** This list does not say what any faith believes about the Wizard, the Held Breath, the Warrens, or who the Court answers to. Where the source files carry cosmological GM notes, they stay in the source files.
- **Retired and renamed names.** The Chance-Weaver no longer exists as a separate deity: Khvarenah (Persian face) and Caishen (Eastern face) are masks of the Jade Illusionist. The Remembrancer-Who-Forgets is **the Last Voice** (Soŋ Ses). Death is plural: the **Ölüm Sürüsü** on the steppe, the **Wūyā Tǐng** (Crow Court) in the east, the Shadowing in the Persian register, and the Yamadutas in Indian tradition.
- **Sacred self-names are not used.** The ancestries' secret names stay in `narrative/gm_secrets/ANCESTRY_SECRET_NAMES.md`.

## How to read an entry

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Share** | Rough share of a typical settlement's people who hold this as their main faith. Per region, shares plus *Lapsed / other* come to about 100. |
| **Pull** | Who it draws: ancestries, trades, conditions. Raise the share where the pull applies. |
| **Practice** | What a player sees: shrine form, ritual, everyday speech. |
| **Service** | What a settlement gains from having it: assessors, scribes, healers, guides. |
| **Friction** | What it quietly distrusts, and why. Friction is a hook, not a feud. |
| **Fantasy-only** | Marked where the faith has no real-world analog. |

## The faiths

### F1. The Road-Keeper (Rashnu-e-Rah, Yol-Tanri, Tù-Dì Gōng, Kshetrapala)
*Fantasy-only; ambient. Almost everyone acknowledges it; few hold it as a main faith.*
- **Pull:** caravaners, guides, innkeepers, anyone about to travel.
- **Practice:** stone boxes at crossroads, a word spoken aloud to the road before riding, rice at a trail marker. Persian face keeps scales and accounts; steppe face has no shrine.
- **Service:** road-ledger priests who know who travelled safely and who did not.
- **Friction:** none. It is the shared floor under every other faith.

### F2. The Lamp (Sufi-inflected devotion)
- **Pull:** Persian-register merchants, scholars, Tulpar who keep the syncretic layer.
- **Practice:** lodge and courtyard rather than temple; recitation and shared meals; wandering shrines on the steppe edge.
- **Service:** hospitality, arbitration between traders, lettered clerks.
- **Friction:** fire-keepers (competing for the same merchants), strict temple clergy.

### F3. The Fire (Zoroastrian-analog; Atash-keepers)
- **Pull:** Kuhban (fire mysticism), old Persian and Sogdian families, river towns.
- **Practice:** a kept flame, water reverence (Anahita), purity rules about bodies and rivers. Fouling a river is a religious offence, not only a civic one.
- **Service:** priests who read purity; engineers who treat canals as devotion.
- **Friction:** the Lamp, over lodges that cook on temple-grade fire; any settlement that dumps waste upstream.

### F4. The Open Sky (Tengri; shamanic)
- **Pull:** Tulpar, Kalan, steppe clans, caravan guards who ride out.
- **Practice:** open-air sky altars, ancestor barrows, no images (Tengri cannot be depicted). Shamans read the death-company by name: Kızıl Kanat, Kara Yel, Tatlı Uyku, Susuz, Gece Gölgesi.
- **Service:** shamans who read weather, road memory and the kind of death approaching.
- **Friction:** walled temples, image-makers, anyone who claims to speak for the sky.

### F5. The Caves (Mahayana-analog Buddhism)
- **Pull:** Eastern Gateway and Tarim settlements, pilgrims, copyists, the dying and bereaved.
- **Practice:** cave temples, library caves, flying-figure murals, pilgrimage. Guardian deities (the Four Heavenly Kings) stand at gates.
- **Service:** scribes, translators, medicine, travellers' lodging at monasteries.
- **Friction:** the Fire (relics and bodies), civic ritual where the temple is made to serve the court.

### F6. The Folk Way (Chinese folk religion and Daoist-analog)
- **Pull:** Eastern Terminus townsfolk, soldiers (Guanyu), merchants (Caishen), petty officials (Crow Court bureaucracy).
- **Practice:** street shrines, paper offerings, household altars, oaths sworn in Guanyu's name. A single crow on a roof is an appointment made; a flock is still deliberating.
- **Service:** oath-witnesses, diviners, fortune-sellers, household door-warding.
- **Friction:** foreign faiths that refuse the offering custom; clergy who take payment for oaths.

### F7. The Civic Rite (Confucian-analog court ethic)
*An overlay, not a competing faith. Counts toward share only in Eastern Terminus.*
- **Pull:** officials, clerks, examinees, garrison commanders.
- **Practice:** ancestral halls, ritual order, bureaucratic heaven.
- **Service:** records, examinations, formal mediation.
- **Friction:** anything that offers a loyalty above the court.

### F8. The Merchant Saints (Tarim syncretic)
- **Pull:** oasis traders of every ancestry; practical-magic hedge-priests.
- **Practice:** saint-tombs along the route, offerings to the founder-merchant of a family or a well, a charm for each cargo. Mixes Buddhist, Lamp and Fire forms freely.
- **Service:** blessed contracts, caravan insurance, well-keepers.
- **Friction:** orthodoxies of every kind, which each call it hedging.

### F9. The Ledger (Chitragupta; Indian-diaspora)
- **Pull:** Indian-sphere trade communities, clerks, anyone with a charge against them.
- **Practice:** small shrines with a ledger-figure, petitions to verify the record. The audit is binding on the petitioner too: asking him to confirm your innocence opens everything he has written about you.
- **Service:** audits of deeds, accounts, claims.
- **Friction:** Folk Way oath-witnesses, who guard the same ground.

### F10. The Mountain Bride and the Wind (Anahit; Vata / Vayu)
- **Pull:** pass villages, herders, Simiah (the Wind-Lord's children).
- **Practice:** shrines on the approach side of the pass so the sleeper is not woken; wind read as message. A favourable gust at the right moment is meant, not lucky.
- **Service:** weather-readers, pass guides, apotropaic shrine-keepers.
- **Friction:** loud or visibly powerful strangers.

### F11. The Forge-Record (Žarokaz; Kuhban)
*Fantasy-only; ancestry-bound.*
- **Pull:** Kuhban first, craft guilds and banking houses that want a certified lineage.
- **Practice:** a journeyman's first independent work is held in firelight and read by a priest. "The forge has seen it." Three generations of fire is the highest compliment.
- **Service:** master assessors for craft, genealogy and weapon histories. Expensive; a status marker.
- **Friction:** lucky-find wealth and unearned inheritance.

### F12. The Still Mirror (Shizuka no Kagami; Serenvar)
*Fantasy-only; ancestry-bound.*
- **Pull:** Serenvar, scholars past a certain age.
- **Practice:** mirror-pools, the Kagami no Mae rite (speaking all one knows into a reflective surface as acknowledgment, not transmission). The deity reflects without mourning.
- **Service:** quiet; the pools are visited, not staffed.
- **Friction:** the Last Voice, which grieves where the Mirror does not.

### F13. The Last Voice (Soŋ Ses)
*Fantasy-only; a practice more than a creed.*
- **Pull:** Tulpar remembrancers, oral-tradition keepers of any ancestry, survivors of broken lineages.
- **Practice:** an annual naming rite (full name, family, homeland, said aloud); invoked as a warning, never a protector. "Feed the Last Voice by continuing."
- **Service:** keepers of names and recitations.
- **Friction:** anyone who edits a record, or a tradition, to a purpose.

### F14. The Hearth Layer (Door-Warden, Fever-Breaker, Silk-Spider, Battle-Voice)
*Fantasy-only; household and trade powers. Everyone has some of it; it rarely leads.*
- **Pull:** households, healers, contract-makers, soldiers at the threshold of a fight.
- **Practice:** threshold charms, fever-night vigils, knotted-thread contract rites, a breath before a cry.
- **Service:** midwives, hedge-healers, contract notaries.
- **Friction:** none.

### F15. The Road Without Altars (diaspora merchant folklore)
- **Pull:** Jivar, Rahban, and travelling families with no sacred geography of their own.
- **Practice:** hearth-stories, luck rites borrowed locally, an offering at whichever altar is nearest.
- **Service:** portable shrine-craft, trade omens.
- **Friction:** none; others may read this as irreverence.

## Quick draw guide (for child C settlement passes)

1. Read the settlement's `parent_region`; take the region's table. A sub-region uses its parent's table plus its stated addition.
2. Roll the faith by share. A settlement of 800 people or fewer has at most three faiths with a public shrine.
3. Apply the ancestry pulls: Kuhban toward F11 and F3, Serenvar toward F12, Tulpar toward F4 and F13, Simiah toward F10, Jivar and Rahban toward F15.
4. Name the pre-eminent deity once as the settlement's shrine; others appear as side shrines.
5. Add the Road-Keeper and one Hearth Layer shrine regardless of roll.
6. Pick one friction from the winning faith and let it surface in the settlement's local trouble.

## Open points for the GM

These are the calls this draft leaves untouched. Each is described with the case for it and what it costs to change.

- **Real-religion labels versus in-world names.** The analog labels follow the mapping framework. Naming them in-world (for example, a Lamp order with its own title) would make the table feel more like the world and less like our notes; it costs a naming pass and a decision about how close to keep to the real names.
- **Share precision.** The shares are seeds so the settlement passes have something to draw from. If you want a region to feel more one-faith or more plural, raising or lowering a line is a one-line edit.
- **F7 Civic Rite as a faith.** Treated here as an overlay, since it does not compete for adherents the way the others do. The alternative is to drop it from the share table and name it only in Eastern Terminus settlement notes.
