I. Structural Clarifications

Chance-Weaver → Retired; Absorbed into the Jade Illusionist

The Chance-Weaver as a separate deity is redundant given the Jade Illusionist's established place in the Celestial Court. The distinction:

  • Trickster = agent with intent. Authors the chaos. Has a relationship with you. Loki, Coyote, Anansi — the chaos is authored.
  • Chance as force = no intent, no relationship. Just the unpredictable substrate.

In Tarim-Shaiel's framework: the Jade Illusionist is the Trickster figure, operating at Celestial level but choosing to engage with the mortal world. Regional "luck" deities (Khvarenah in Persian tradition, Caishen in eastern tradition) are understood as faces of the Jade Illusionist wearing mortal-legible masks.

Implication for NPC Theology: A Persian merchant invoking Khvarenah and an eastern merchant invoking Caishen are invoking the same underlying entity through very different cultural frameworks. The Jade Illusionist doesn't correct the taxonomy.

II. Death as Chorus — Full Design

Why Death is Plural

Death cannot be singular if it must be present everywhere simultaneously. The chorus model is both cosmologically coherent and culturally resonant across all four regional spheres.

The Ölüm Sürüsü

"Death-Flock" or "Death-Herd." The Steppe/Turkic name for the collective. Not one rider — a jurisdiction company.

Named members the shamans know:

  • Kara Yel ("Black Wind") — death by exposure, cold, storm, the road that kills
  • Kızıl Kanat ("Red Wing") — battle-death; the one soldiers invoke before combat
  • Tatlı Uyku ("Sweet Sleep") — peaceful death, old age, the quiet end; most benevolent
  • Susuz ("The Thirsty") — death by drought, famine, dehydration; feared in desert crossings
  • Gece Gölgesi ("Night Shadow") — sudden death, unexpected, no warning given
A Steppe shaman doesn't say "Death is coming." They say "I see Kızıl Kanat circling" — specific information about the kind of dying that approaches.

Wūyā Tǐng (烏鴉廷) — The Crow Court

The Chinese face of the death chorus. The components are rooted in real tradition — crows as death-omens (Sānzúwū, the three-legged crow), Yama's bureaucracy, individual messengers assigned at birth. The "deliberating flock" reading is constructed for this world:

The Visual: A murder of crows landing on a rooftop is the Crow Court in conference — comparing notes, ensuring the accounting is correct before proceeding. Single crow: the appointment has been made. Flock: still deliberating. You have time to make arrangements.

Persian Face — The Shadowing

Your assigned chorus-member has followed you since birth. They are not hostile. They have been patient. In folk tradition: a "heavy breath" sensation, the feeling of being watched at the edge of vision — this means your shadow-member has moved closer. Something is approaching that the chorus is preparing for.

The verb: shadowing. "My shadow is close lately." Not metaphor.

Indian Face — Yamadutas and the Appeals Window

Yama's messengers are occasionally wrong. Wrong soul taken, wrong time, mistake realized, returned. Folk heroes who claim to have briefly visited Yama's court and been sent back with full memory. This is canonical in tradition; it also seeds the cosmological logic of why the Chinvat Bridge and Chitragupta's ledger are available for appeal.

III. Guanyu — Daily Life & Economy

Full epithet: Guanyu Shèngdì — "Guanyu the Holy Sovereign." In everyday speech: "the Holy Lord" or "the Red General."

What Ordinary People Do

Before any significant business agreement: burn a stick of incense at the nearest shrine (or portable altar if traveling), state the terms aloud in the smoke, clap twice. No priest required. The incense carries the terms to the Red General's record.

In households: a small Guanyu tablet above the front door, often just a red card with his name. When a family member makes a serious promise, they touch the tablet. When someone is accused of breaking faith, the tablet is brought out. Most people can't bring themselves to lie with their hand on it.

In military contexts: "Guanyu hù wǒ" — "Guanyu protect me." The protection asked for is not safety from death. It's protection from dishonor. Dying is acceptable. Dying badly, breaking faith with your unit, running — these are what you're asking him to prevent.

Everyday Speech

PhraseMeaning
"By Guanyu's beard"Mild oath of emphasis; "I swear to god"
"Guanyu's open hand"Offer of honest dealing; extend palm up, nothing hidden. Equivalent of "on my honor"
"Under the Red General's eye"Officially sworn; binding
"His sword is slow but it always arrives"Proverb: certain, eventual consequence for broken oaths
"I can't close my hand on that"Idiomatic refusal; "I cannot swear to that." Closed fist = inverted Guanyu palm

When Trust is Questioned

The accused extends their open palm and says "Guanyu's palm" (formal: "I offer Guanyu's palm on it"). This is not casual. Falsely offering Guanyu's palm is one of the more serious social transgressions available. People hesitate before accepting it — because accepting means you believe them, which creates obligation on both sides.

Temple Structure & Priesthood Economy

Location: Always commercial — near market quarters, beside guild halls, at trading post entrances. Always bright red. Interior: central statue (massive, seated, red-faced, beard elaborate) flanked by military advisor and accounting scribe.

How the priesthood earns:

  1. Registration fees — agreements formally witnessed and entered into the temple register, scaled to transaction value. The register is the commercial history of the city.
  2. Arbitration fees — both parties pay when a dispute goes to the temple. Losing party pays more. Bringing a false claim is expensive.
  3. Certification fees — the Guanyu temple seal on a contract costs money and means something. Also: standing certificates for traveling merchants. A credit rating with divine backing.
The Political Economy: Temple masters who hold the commercial records of a city's merchant class hold quiet leverage. They never appear to be exercising power. The most sophisticated ones are among the most influential people in eastern cities precisely because of this.

IV. Chitragupta — Daily Life & Economy

Full epithet: Chitragupta Dharmācārya — "Chitragupta, Master of Right-Record." In everyday speech: "the Scribe," "the Accountant," "the One Who Writes."

What Ordinary People Do

The relationship is less transactional than Guanyu and more confessional — but not for absolution. For accurate record.

Before major ethical decisions: Write down what you're about to do on a small slip of paper, fold it, place it under a stone at a Chitragupta roadside marker. "The Scribe knows I did this deliberately." Protection and accountability simultaneously.

In merchant communities: Invoked specifically when accused of something you didn't do. The folk phrase: "Let the Scribe read it." Risk: the ledger contains everything. Opening it to examine one entry opens the whole page.

Everyday Speech

PhraseMeaning
"The Scribe has it"On the record; it happened and can't be undone
"Let the Scribe read it"Formal challenge when falsely accused. Has teeth because you also submit yourself to the ledger
"Don't make me open that page"Warning: if you push this dispute to Chitragupta arbitration, things will come out you don't want examined
"The Scribe was watching""I have a witness you can't argue with." Formal invocation; rarely used casually
"Born with clean ink"Said of newcomers, young people starting adult life, or those who've undergone major restitution. Whether literally true is deliberately ambiguous

The Audit Process in Practice

  1. Both parties appear before the priest
  2. Accusation stated formally and recorded
  3. Accused states counter-claim and records it
  4. Priest meditates (hours to days) — the investigation period, not supernatural communion
  5. Priest announces what the ledger shows
The fiction: the priest receives divine information. The reality: Chitragupta priests are excellent investigators who have been quietly gathering information since the dispute was brought to them. The result is divine because it's accurate, not because it's supernatural. Occasionally the priest sees something that surprises even them. The experienced ones have learned not to show it.

Temple Structure

Quiet, administrative. Scrolls everywhere. Organized by a system only priests understand. Small reading lamps burning constantly. Central figure: a seated scholar, writing. The pen never stops.

The Pool of All Record: A large ink-dark pool in the center of the main chamber. Folk tradition: the reflection shows you as you actually are, not as you present. May be metaphor. May not.

Priests: Grey-robed, carry writing implements, take extensive notes on everything, non-judgmental in a way more unsettling than comforting. Their job is recording, not judging. Judgment is Yama's department.

V. Žarokaz — Dwarven Deity (New)

Full epithet: Žarokaz — "The Fire-Truth-Teller" (Slavic compound: žar = fire/heat/ardor + kaz from kazat' = to show/tell/reveal). Literally: "what the fire reveals."

Domain: Craft-record, earned wealth (generational, legitimate), battle-worth assessment
Structural type: Court Emanation — from the Weighmaster

The Triple Function

Craft: Every Dwarven journeyman, on completing their first independent work, brings it to a Žarokaz shrine and holds it in firelight while a priest reads the marks. Not a blessing — an assessment. The priest says what the work reveals about the maker. This is sometimes uncomfortable. It cannot be falsified; the work is the testimony.

Wealth: Dwarven legitimate wealth is specifically generational — accumulated through honest craft and trade across multiple generations. Single-generation enrichment (lucky find, fortunate inheritance without contribution) is not Žarokaz-blessed. Dwarven banking houses under Žarokaz's mark are advertising that their wealth is earned, not lucky.

Battle-worth: A warrior's weapon carries its maker's mark and, over time, the warrior's own marks — nicks, repairs, the specific wear-patterns of how they fight. A Žarokaz priest can read a weapon's history.

"This blade has killed seventeen times. Three of those were from behind. The maker would be ashamed." — Reserved for disputes about reputation, claims of honor, accusations of cowardice.

Everyday Speech (Dwarven)

PhraseMeaning
"The forge has seen it"Equivalent of "Guanyu's palm" — invoking witness to one's honesty
"Žarokaz reads the mark"Implicit threat: your work (or your deeds) will eventually be assessed
"Three generations of fire"Highest compliment for a craft lineage — unbroken honest work for three generations
"Cold iron, cold truth"A weapon or tool made without honest craft is untrustworthy — applies to people too

Priesthood Economy

Žarokaz priests are master assessors, not notaries. Called in to evaluate craft work, authenticate genealogy, and certify weapon histories. Fees are charged per assessment; they're expensive. Dwarven clans that can afford regular assessments use this as a status marker: "our lineage is certified, not merely claimed." The genealogy records are among the most politically significant documents in Dwarven politics.

VI. The Last Voice

Revised name: Soŋ Ses (Turkic-derived: "Last Sound/Voice") — formerly "Remembrancer-Who-Forgets"

Core wound: Not forgetting itself. The people who held knowledge and are gone. The moment when a tradition ends because everyone who carried it has been killed.

Distinction from Chitragupta

  • Chitragupta records deeds — what you did, what was done to you
  • The Last Voice witnesses transmissions — what was being taught, what was being passed on, what died when the teacher died
The Scholars' Purge (1173 HB): Created an enormous outstanding balance with the Last Voice. Hundreds of scholars killed before they could finish teaching. The Last Voice holds their unfinished transmissions — not preserved, but witnessed. The knowledge is gone. The fact that it was being transmitted is what the Last Voice carries.

Orcish Ritual Practice

Orcish Remembrancers perform an annual ritual acknowledging the Last Voice — not to worship, but to remember what happens when remembering fails. The ritual ends with every participant stating their full name, their family's name, and the name of their homeland aloud. The deity is invoked only as warning, never as protector.

"Feed the Last Voice by continuing" — keep the oral tradition alive so the deity doesn't have to feed on silence. Every story told, every name remembered, reduces the Last Voice's burden.

Campaign Relevance

The heroes chasing the sources of the Scholars' Purge are, in Orcish cosmological terms, undoing a divine desecration. The Last Voice has an interest — not in revenge, but in completion. If the heroes find what was being taught and ensure it reaches someone who can carry it forward, they are performing a cosmological act, not just a historical one.

VII. Shizuka no Kagami — Elven Equivalent (New)

Name: Shizuka no Kagami (静かな鏡) — "The Still Mirror"

Domain: Knowledge deliberately withheld until the holder died without passing it on; the weight of accumulated un-transmission; what immortality costs when wisdom is hoarded rather than shared

Why This is Distinctly Elven

For Orcs, the wound of severed transmission is recent and violent. Someone killed the teachers.

For Elves, the equivalent wound is ancient, chosen, and worse for being chosen. Elves remember their teachers. They remember the full unbroken lineage. But immortality doesn't prevent forgetting — it just makes forgetting take longer. And Elves, who withdrew from the world and who deliberately maintain the illusion that mortals cannot fully understand, have been in the process of a voluntary severing.

Their deity of severed transmission is the patron of knowledge deliberately withheld until the holder died without passing it on.

The Kagami no Mae Ritual ("Before the Mirror")

An Elven scholar who is aging (for Elves, aging means changing — a slow shift in what they find meaningful) sometimes performs this ritual: speaking all they know into a reflective surface, as acknowledgment rather than transmission.

It preserves nothing. It witnesses the knowledge as it passes into the mirror. This is understood to be more dignified than simply letting it fade unacknowledged.

The Still Mirror's Character

It doesn't mourn. That's the specifically Elven inflection. The Last Voice grieves. The Still Mirror reflects without affect. It shows you the depth of what is gone with complete equanimity, which is somehow worse.

"Being shown what I am keeping from the world, and understanding that I cannot stop keeping it." — Elves who have performed Kagami no Mae

The Political Dimension

Elves who understand the Still Mirror tradition also understand what the Scholars' Purge looks like from inside their own cosmology. They did not kill the scholars — but their withdrawal from the world, their deliberate choice not to teach across species, created a different version of the same mirror-reflection.

An NPC Elven elder who has recently performed Kagami no Mae would be in a particular state: not guilty, not grieving, but honest about the weight of what they've withheld. That's a distinctive register for an encounter.

VIII. Crow Court — GM Signal Protocol

For table use: crows are a silent GM signal that something cosmologically significant is happening nearby. Not dramatic. Just: they're there.

Crow BehaviorWhat It Signals
Single crow, watchingThe appointment has been made for someone nearby
Two crows, sitting togetherThe accounting is being done; a death is being considered
A flock landingThe Crow Court in conference; someone nearby is being deliberated over
A flock taking flight all at onceThe decision has been made; the departure is imminent
Crows that don't startleSomething is wrong with the normal order; the Death-Chorus is distracted

Players who have learned the crow-lore (from a Steppe shaman NPC, from a caravanserai story circle, from their own cultural background) will read these signals. Players who haven't learned it yet will just notice the birds. Both are correct table experiences.
