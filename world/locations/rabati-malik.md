# Rabati Malik

*Known for: the last honest tariff on the road, or the first excuse for a bad one — depends who's collecting.*

Real-world basis: Rabat-i Malik, an 11th-century fortified caravanserai on the Samarkand–Bukhara road (Navoi Region, Uzbekistan), built by a Karakhanid khan — an 18m monumental entrance portal, with the domed Sardoba cistern nearby. Already referenced in `world/content/geography_routes_caravanserai.md`.

---

## Overview

The portal is the first thing anyone sees and the last thing anyone stops noticing — eighteen meters of carved stone announcing an authority that hasn't governed anything in a long time, and everyone passing under it knows it. Behind it, the domed cistern keeps the caravanserai alive through seasons the wells outside can't be trusted in. Morning here means commerce in three languages at once — animals, cargo, and handlers filling the courtyard from the moment the gates open. It also means the road's older authority still rides through on occasion: imperial livery, faded but not retired, collecting tariffs the Merchant Council's spring agreements were supposed to have settled. The two claims to authority rarely resolve cleanly.

## Character & Culture

Population is transient by design — a few hundred at rest, several times that during a caravan season, almost none of them permanent. Tulpar merchants and guards make up the largest working presence, alongside Rahban waystation staff and a rotating mix of whoever the road has delivered that week. Everyone here has learned when to look busy: a courtyard that's visibly working is a courtyard nobody official wants to slow down and inspect too closely. Merchants keep two sets of books for exactly this reason — one true, one for whoever's asking.

## Notable Features

- **The portal** — eighteen meters of carved stone, the caravanserai's monumental face and the only part of it built to impress rather than function.
- **The Sardoba cistern** — domed, cool, the reason this stop exists where it does rather than somewhere more convenient.
- **The market rows** — the courtyard's working center, loud, dense, three languages overlapping before anyone's had breakfast.
- **The streets behind the market** — narrower, residential, the market's noise doesn't carry back here the same way. Rooftops overlook the lanes; it's easy to lose someone around a corner, easier to overlook them from above without meaning to.

## What Players Know

- The tariff schedule changes more than the Merchant Council admits to.
- Something happened here involving four strangers passing through a season back — the guards who were on duty that day don't talk about it.
