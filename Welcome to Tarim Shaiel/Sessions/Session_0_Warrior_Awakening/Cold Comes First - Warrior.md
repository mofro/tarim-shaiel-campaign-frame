### Opening Narration

\[\[Cold Comes First - Warrior MUSIC.mp3\]\]

Cold comes first—not the clean bite of winter air, but something more claustrophobic and cloying. Something that's settled into your bones like sediment, patient and aching. Your left arm is numb where it's been crushed beneath you dully aching from the weight, and when you try to flex fingers, they move like foreign things, more like distant relatives or belonging to a stranger. How long have you been lying like this?

The smells come next, deep and unbidden but a necessary evil of your haggard breathing. They are layered like a burial: unwashed bodies pressed too close. Smoke—cooking fires, low and abashed, nothing eager about them. Lower still, grain gone sour, rotting from the inside out. And underneath _everything_, sharp and copper-bright: fear-sweat. That particular reek of people who understand, deeply, the necessity of caution, and the peerless value of being small. What it means to be hunted.

The wind, because of course, there is a breeze you realize, carries with it sounds in fragments. You can feel it as much as hear it, touching your skin and tickling your ears. A child crying somewhere distant—not the thin wail of hunger, but something quieter. Self-aware enough at whatever young age to somehow know it shouldn't cry too loud. Fire crackling, but subdued, speaking in whispers. Voices urgent and careful. Footsteps on packed earth, deliberately slow. The shuffle of people who have learned to move as though apology itself has weight.

You open your eyes.

Darkness greets you—not absence of light but a thickness to the air itself, like looking through cloth. Shapes emerge slowly: angles that might be shelters, humps that could be sleeping bodies or discarded bundles. And beneath you, pressing against your cheek, the mud.

This isn't the honest mud of a riverbank that dries clean in sun. This is earth that's given up. Degraded past soil into something closer to rot—compacted by years of feet that had nowhere else to stand, stained by blood washed thin until it forgot it was ever red, host to whatever got dropped and never retrieved _because retrieval meant admitting you'd lost something worth keeping_. Your left arm sinks into it past the wrist, skin bonded to cold wetness like an unwelcome embrace from a cold lover, a pact you don't remember making. Something solid rests against the pulse point.

You move to lift your arm, half in recoil and half for balance and the movement costs you. As the muscles pull and the slow sucking sound of muck challenging your release from its intimate embrace you feel the solid thing's weight shift and for a moment—just a moment—you feel it resist. A shy partner, unconvinced of the wooing. But move it does.

Iron.

A blade emerges with your arm, trailing streamers of muck. Simple and dark—the kind of crude iron that was never meant to catch light and throw it back. No shine of care, no gleam of pride. Just function hammered into shape by someone who knew what they were doing but didn't waste time making it pretty. The grip is bone wrapped in leather gone the color of old bruises, the kind that yellow at the edges when they're trying to heal. A leather thong cinches it to your wrist, tight enough to announce itself with every pulse but not quite tight enough to numb. The blade itself carries stains that might be rust. Or might be something that _was_ rust before it decided to become a question.

You look at it—this... this tool. This rudimentary expression of violence and mis-repair, that you apparently brought to whatever this place is—and the first thought that surfaces isn't relief. Not recognition either, though something about the weight feels _known_ in a way that bypasses memory entirely. Just a certainty that arrives simple and terrible and absolute:

*   \-This is my chain--
