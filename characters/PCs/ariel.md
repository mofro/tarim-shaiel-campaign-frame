## Who They Are

Ariel acts on conviction, not authority — she moves toward what she has decided is true before others have finished naming the problem. She sees what needs to happen and trusts that reading more than she trusts the formal structures around her. This is not arrogance, exactly; it is the quality of someone for whom the gap between "perceiving clearly" and "acting on what you perceive" has always been shorter than it is for most people. The cost of that clarity is that she cannot look away from what she sees, even when looking away would be easier.

> **Ancestry note:** Ariel is Vaghri — naturally feline humanoid. Her flight is a Seraph class ability, not a transformation. Do not describe her changing form.

## Divine Tool

**Obsidian disk**

A disk of polished obsidian, palm-sized, that shows reflections it has no business showing — faces Ariel does not recognize, places she has not been. It grows cold in the presence of deception and warm in the presence of what it registers as convergence: moments where the shape of things is clarifying toward a point. It does not so much help Ariel see clearly as remind her that what she sees is not all there is. She has learned not to stare at it too long in one sitting.

## GM Notes

Ariel's arc runs through the Visionary's core tension: was she really chosen, or just convenient? The obsidian disk is the surrendered layer made manifest — it shows what she has no right to look away from, including what she might prefer not to see.

The disk's response to the Wizard's presence (reserve for Act II) is a planted signal: what the disk registers as "convergence" may not be what Ariel would call convergence if she understood it fully.

History with Captain Kaan is a live thread: he has no reason to trust her, but also no reason to think she'd bother lying to him. This ambivalence is a resource for Thread A in Session 2.

Surrendered layer — *Perceptual*: she cannot look away; the cost is that some of what she sees will be things she was not meant to see yet.
