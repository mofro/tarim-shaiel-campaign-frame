## Who They Are

Sahir reads rooms and people the way some read text — quickly, attentively, and with an instinct for what each party needs to feel heard. He withholds his own position until it serves the negotiation, which means people often leave conversations with Sahir feeling understood without being quite sure what he himself believes. This is not manipulation, exactly; it is the posture of someone who learned long ago that having a side too early closes doors that might otherwise open. The two-pulls at the fork near Waycross are the first time his instrument has disagreed with itself, and he has not found the right frame for it yet.

> **Ancestry note:** Rahban do not get lost. Sahir's experience of two simultaneous valid readings at the fork is unprecedented — not disorientation in the ordinary sense, but an instrument receiving two contradictory correct answers at once.

## Divine Tool

**Copper and wood compass**

A navigation instrument, palms-length, with a tapered wooden body and a copper cap at one end. The needle does not always point north. In places of genuine convergence — where roads, histories, or people meet with real significance — it spins twice and settles on something that is not north but is, apparently, correct. Near Waycross's fork it gave two readings simultaneously: one strong pull in each direction, held simultaneously, the needle trembling between them. The first time in Rahban recorded history that a navigation instrument has done this. Sahir keeps it in his inventory. He hasn't told anyone what it did.

## GM Notes

Sahir's arc runs through the Bridge's core tension: the peace he negotiated may have prioritized stability over justice, and the instrument that never lied to him is now telling him two truths at once.

The compass is the surrendered layer made manifest — it always found the correct path, which means Sahir never had to choose. Now it's showing him two simultaneously valid destinations. The choice between them *is* the arc.

The two-pulls at the fork is Act I's planted seed for Sahir specifically. The fork's significance should deepen as the campaign progresses — one of those two pulls goes somewhere the compass has been before.

Surrendered layer — *Relational*: he gave up the right to have a side; the compass gave him certainty instead; now neither is holding.
