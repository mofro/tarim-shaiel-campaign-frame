## Who They Are

Veyra reads situations through threat and response — not because she is paranoid, but because she has spent long enough in dangerous places that it is simply how she organizes the world. She is dependable in the way that load-bearing walls are dependable: the people around her lean on her presence without thinking about it. When the problem is not a sword, she goes quiet and waits for someone else to name it. The waiting is not passivity — it is the patience of someone who knows their particular gift is not always the answer, and is still working out what to do with that.

## Divine Tool

**Iron blade**

A single-edged blade, unremarkable in length, with iron that has never rusted and an edge that does not chip. It is worn comfortable, the grip's wrapping replaced twice but the spine unchanged. The blade remembers strikes it has no business remembering — it grows warm against pressure before Veyra has consciously registered the threat. It has refused the killing blow on at least one occasion Veyra can name, stiffening at the wrist at the wrong moment, and she has not fully made her peace with it. She does not know if it was right.

## GM Notes

Veyra's arc centers on the gap between *protecting* and *controlling*: she protects because she can, and has not yet fully examined whether that's the same thing as protecting because it's wanted. The blade's refusals are a slow signal — it knows something about the cost of force-as-the-clean-answer that she doesn't yet.

Her walking east is an open seed. If Lisa brings her back into the party, she will have had time alone with whatever the east holds. If Lisa keeps the distance, that becomes its own thread.

Surrendered layer — *Tactical*: the clean answer is always force; complexity enters only when force has already failed. Watch for moments where Veyra reaches for the sword and the blade does not cooperate.
